Where to Stay in San Blas: Island Cabins, Hotels or a Sailboat

Where to stay in San Blas, Panama: island cabins, eco lodges and sailboats compared, with prices and which islands to pick.

There are no conventional hotels in the San Blas Islands, Panama. The Guna, who govern the archipelago, allow no big hotels or chains, so you sleep in a Guna-run island cabin, a small Guna-owned lodge, or on a crewed sailboat. As of November 2026, a cabin package with three meals and the 4x4 from Panama City starts at about $140 to $175 per person per night.[1]

If you search for hotels in the San Blas Islands, Panama, booking sites show plenty of "hotels". Most turn out to be thatched cabins on islands you can walk around in ten minutes. That is the whole product, and it is deliberate. Guna Yala is an autonomous Indigenous territory, and the Guna decide who builds, who runs the boats and who sleeps where.

This page compares the three real ways to spend the night: a cabin on a Guna island, a small eco lodge, or a bunk on a crewed sailboat. It covers what each is like, which islands to pick and what a stay costs once fees and transfers are added. For the bigger picture of culture, islands and what to do, start with the complete guide to the San Blas Islands (Guna Yala).

The Options

Hotels in the San Blas Islands, Panama: What Exists and How It Compares

San Blas has no hotels in the sense of a building with a lobby, air conditioning and a pool. Panama's official tourism site lists what you can choose from. At the basic end there are hammocks under palm trees, campgrounds and small cabins without electricity or running water. A step up are slightly more comfortable over-water cabins, such as those on Isla Diablo. Beyond that there are a few hotels and sailboats you sleep on.[2]

All of it is Guna-owned. The comarca's tourism regulation reserves its tourism business register for Guna investors.[3] The same rules require cabins and small resorts to follow a Guna architectural model using local materials, which is why "eco lodge" here means wood, cane and thatch rather than a marketing label.[4] The Panama Tourism Authority's 2025 annual report counts more than 40 registered accommodations in Guna Yala.[5]

Island Cabins

Island Cabins: What a Night on a Guna Island Is Like

An island cabin is a thatched hut on the sand or on stilts, with a bed, a mosquito net and very little else. One operator describes its beach cabins as typically 10 by 12 feet, with sand floors, basic beds with mosquito nets and solar lighting. Power for charging devices runs only during certain hours.[7] Another describes a cabin night this way: no air conditioning, a shared bathroom often on land, sometimes a single generator-powered light for a few hours, and a fixed menu, usually fresh fish with rice.[8]

Nights are warm. Average nighttime temperatures are around 75°F (24°C), so the lack of air conditioning matters less than people fear.[7]

Bathroom
Usually shared, in a separate block outside the cabins. Water often comes from a tank rather than plumbing, so showers are short and cold.
Electricity
Solar or generator, a few hours in the evening. Bring a power bank and a headlamp.
Meals
Three a day are included in packages: fish or chicken, rice, patacones. On a long stay the menu repeats.
Drinking water
Usually not included. Buy bottles in Panama City or at the supermarket stop on the drive.
Phone signal
Unreliable. Plan to be offline.
Check-out
Early on some islands. Misdub, for example, asks guests to leave the cabin at 9 a.m. for cleaning.

Shower water, drinking water and a fan should never be assumed. One operator tells guests to ask about all three before booking.[9] On the six-night itinerary of another operator, drinking water is sold by the bottle or brought from a supermarket.[10]

Which San Blas Island to Stay on

The island you pick sets the mood more than the cabin does. Sea San Blas's comparison, checked in September 2026, lists the cabin types for its six overnight islands. Franklin and Pelícano have shared group cabins for 6 to 8 people and private cabins for 2 to 4. Aroma has group cabins. Aguja has private rooms for 1 to 2 and private cabins for 2 to 4.[9] Pelícano's own site prices its cabins at $145 to $175 per person per night and lists Aroma from $140.[11]

Most of these islands are small enough to walk around in minutes. Seeing anything else means paying for a lancha (the local motorboat) excursion, unless your package includes one.[8]

Over-water Cabins

Over-water cabins are the comfort upgrade. Sea San Blas sells private over-water cabins on Yanis for 2 to 3 guests and on Misdub for 2 to 4.[9] Pelícano's premium over-water cabin costs $175 per person per night and comes with its own bathroom.[1] On Naranjo Chico (Narasgandup), an over-water cabin with a private bathroom is listed at $105 per person for the first night and $88 for each extra night. That rate includes meals, the boat and the port tax, but not the 4x4 from the city.[12]

Good to know · BATHROOMS

"Private cabin" usually means a private sleeping space, not an en-suite bathroom. Sea San Blas states that none of its six island stays has an en-suite, even in private cabins. If a toilet of your own matters, ask for it by name before you pay.

Families and Solo Travelers

Rules differ by island. Franklin is adults only. Pelícano charges a child rate for ages 5 to 10 in private cabins. On Misdub, children count toward the cabin's capacity.[9] Children under 10 are exempt from the comarca entry fee.[13] Solo travelers on multi-night packages are often placed in a shared cabin, or camping with equipment provided, where couples get a private one.[10]

Eco Lodges

Eco Lodges and Fly-In Lodges: The Closest San Blas Gets to a Resort

There are no San Blas resorts in the usual sense. A handful of Guna-owned lodges farther along the coast do offer private bathrooms, longer hours of electricity and small-plane access, and these are the nearest thing to an eco resort in Guna Yala. They cost more and take more planning. In return they are far quieter than the islands near Cartí that day-trippers reach.

Sapibenega Kuna Lodge

Sapibenega has the most hotel-like setup in San Blas. Guests get 24-hour electricity, private bathrooms with hot showers, composting toilets, and over-water and beach cabanas with queen and double beds. The flight from Panama City to Playón Chico takes about 40 minutes, followed by an 8-minute canoe ride to the lodge.[14]

Kuna Niskua and Dolphin Lodge

Kuna Niskua is owned by a Guna family. It runs on solar power, its rooms have private bathrooms and its rates include local tours, but it is not on a beach. Dolphin Lodge is an all-inclusive lodge reached by flying into Mamitupo and taking a boat. Its standard rooms have electricity most of the day, while the larger suites have power around the clock and private bathrooms.[14]

Yandup and Dad Ibe

Yandup Island Lodge near Playón Chico is reached only by private flight, priced at $468 to $875 per person round trip.[15] Older listings describe eight Guna-style cabins, most of them over the water with private bathrooms, electricity from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. and no hot water. Dad Ibe Lodge, near the Achutupo airstrip, is listed as three cabins over the sea with private bathrooms and hammocks, with meals and the airstrip transfer included. Its published rates are old, so ask for current ones.

Premium Stays in the Dutch Cays

At the top of the range, Anaimar sits on Banedub in the Cayos Holandeses (Dutch Cays), an outer group of uninhabited cays that day tours do not reach. It sells a one-night package at $650 per person with a two-person minimum, and extra nights at $260 per person with three meals and one drink per meal. The entry fee is not included.[16]

Coral Lodge also appears on lists of San Blas lodges. It is described as an all-inclusive eco resort on a white-sand beach, with over-water villas with Jacuzzis, meals, soft drinks, kayaks and snorkel gear in the price.[14] It is the one place on those lists that calls itself a resort, so confirm its exact location and transfer route before you count it as a stay on the islands.

Not San Blas

Laguna Azul Eco Lodge: In Bocas del Toro, Not San Blas

Laguna Azul Eco Lodge is not in San Blas. It sits at Punta Laurel on Isla Popa in Bocas del Toro, the archipelago at the opposite, western end of Panama's Caribbean coast, within the area of Bastimentos National Marine Park.[17] Bocas and San Blas are separate trips. If both are on your list, plan them as separate legs of the itinerary.

The lodge is a small over-the-water property. One listing describes six rooms, a full-service restaurant and bar, and a published rate of $500 a night for a double. That rate is all-inclusive, with transfers and one daily tour, but the listing is undated.[18] Another source says eight rooms, with all meals cooked by a professional chef, and gives the opening year as 2011.[19] A booking listing shows rooms from 500 balboas (PAB), which equals 500 dollars.[20]

Star ratings in listings run from three to four, and one booking site describes breakfast as paid rather than included. Check the meal plan before you book. To get there, fly Air Panama from Panama City to Isla Colón, then take a 35-minute private boat to Isla Popa.[19]

If an over-water eco lodge is what drew you to the search, the Bocas version has more comfort than anything in Guna Yala. See our Bocas del Toro travel guide for the archipelago and our comparison of Panama's eco and jungle lodges for similar places.

Sailboats

Sleeping on a Sailboat in San Blas: Prices and What You Get

A crewed sailboat costs several times more than a cabin. In return you wake up anchored off a different island each day, with a bathroom on board on most boats and meals cooked by the crew. It is also the only practical way to spend several nights in the outer cays. One charter company says stays of 4 to 7 nights suit trips through the Dutch Cays and the Coco Bandero islands.[21]

What Boats Cost per Night

Sailing Life Experience publishes daily rates for two people, with a 2-night minimum, as of November 2026.[22]

  • Small sailboats up to 40 feet with an outdoor shower start at $550 a day.
  • Sailboats of 40 to 50 feet with a private bathroom start at $580.
  • Catamarans of 40 to 57 feet with private cabins and bathrooms start at $900.
  • Catamarans with air conditioning start at $1,350.

The same company adds 20% at Carnival, Easter, Christmas and New Year, and does not include the Guna Yala entry fee in its price.[22]

Other operators publish similar rates. BlueMarys, a 58-foot Beneteau, charges $600 to $690 a night in high season for two guests sharing the boat with others. Booking the whole boat costs $1,300 a night for two and $1,900 for four to six.[23] Nomad Sailors lists $750 to $1,600 a night for two depending on the boat. On a full boat the price drops to $260 to $275 per person per night, and several of its boats add 20% on public holidays.[8]

What Is Included on Board

Crewed charters bundle the captain, cook and food, which is what makes them comparable to an all-inclusive cabin package. BlueMarys serves four meals a day, with soft drinks, beer, wine and water within reasonable limits. It also provides a dinghy, snorkel gear, a paddleboard, wifi and a private bathroom for each cabin.[23] Another agency promises at least the three main meals and soft drinks, plus crew, chef and water toys.[24]

Air conditioning is the exception, not the rule, so ask if you sleep badly in heat. Ask too whether the price covers fuel, drinks beyond meals and the transfer to the boat. For full charter pricing by day, weekend and week, see our guide to San Blas catamaran and yacht charters.

If you are traveling on to Colombia, the multi-day passages that sleep their way through San Blas are a different product, with different boats and safety questions. Our guide to sailing from Panama to Colombia through San Blas covers them.

Prices

A night in San Blas costs from under $100 per person on a simple island lodge to $650 per person at the top end. A crewed boat costs more per person unless it is full. Nightly rates are not like hotel rates, because most include meals and the boat, and many include the 4x4 from Panama City.

Before You Go

Booking, Entry Fees and Getting to Your Island

You can book a cabin directly with an island operator or through a tour company in Panama City. Either way, plan the transfer, the cash and the passport before you go. Pelícano sells its cabins directly, with the 4x4 and boat included.[1] Naranjo Chico sells lodging and the boat, and offers the 4x4 as an add-on.[12] Some boat seats from Cartí are pre-sold to tour operators, so a cabin booked on a hotel site without transport can leave you stranded at the dock.

For the full transport breakdown, including flights and sailboat transfers, see our guide to getting from Panama City to San Blas. Before paying a Panama City agency, you can check the Panama Tourism Authority's official list of authorized tour operators on atp.gob.pa.[25]

The Usual Road-and-Boat Arrival

The standard route runs by 4x4 to the port of Cartí, then by lancha to your island. One operator's timetable has hotel pickup at 5:00 to 5:45 a.m., arrival at Cartí around 8:30 a.m. and the first island by about 9:30.[26] Only 4x4 vehicles are allowed past the Guna checkpoint.[3]

1

Hotel pickup in Panama City 5:00-5:45 a.m.

Shared 4x4s usually carry about six passengers. Pack light and keep your passport in your hand luggage.

2

Supermarket stop 15-20 min

Buy drinking water, snacks and anything else you want for the island. Nothing on the islands takes cards.

3

Guna checkpoint on the mountain road

Show your original passport and pay the entry fee in cash. The last stretch of road is steep and winding, so take motion-sickness pills before it starts if you need them.

4

Cartí pier and lancha 25 min to 2 h

Pay the port fee if it is charged separately, then board the boat. Put bags in a dry bag, because the ride can be wet.

5

Check in on the island by about 9:30 a.m.

You get the cabin, a briefing on meal times and the hours when the electricity is on.

Shared round-trip 4x4 transfers cost $50 to $70 per person, and the lancha $20 to $30 more.[26] Boat times depend on the island and range from about 30 minutes to 2 hours.[27] Lanchas do not cross open water after 4:30 p.m., so a late start can cost you a night.[26] Return boats usually leave the islands in the early morning or around 3 p.m.[8]

Flights leave from Albrook (PAC) and take under an hour. They can be canceled in bad weather, and a private charter to El Porvenir starts at about $200 per trip.[28] Flights are the normal access to the lodges at Playón Chico, Achutupo and Mamitupo. Fly-in visitors pay a comarca fee at the airstrip, quoted at $10 to $20 depending on the strip.[15]

Entry Fees, Passport and Cash

Operators quote the Guna Yala entry fee slightly differently. Most give $22 for foreign visitors and $7 for Panamanians and residents, with the $2 Cartí port fee included in those totals. Pelícano's pages give $20 plus a $3 dock fee, or $23 in total.[29][30] The fee is collected by the Congreso General Guna, the territory's governing body, and children under 10 are exempt.[13] Package prices do not include it, so carry it in cash.[1] The Congreso's tourism office says it is reviewing its tourism rules, so confirm the figure shortly before you travel.[31]

Good to know · PASSPORT

Foreigners need the original passport with the Panamanian entry stamp at the checkpoint. Photocopies and phone photos are refused, and residents need their residence ID as well.

Bring enough US dollars for the whole stay. The islands have no ATMs and no card payments.[32] The Cartí port closes at 5 p.m.[33]

Rules on the Islands

The comarca's tourism rules restrict photographing and filming in Guna communities and ban harpoon fishing by visitors.[34] Ask before taking pictures of people or homes, and buy molas (the embroidered textile panels Guna women make) directly from the women who sell them. Before paying a deposit, read the cancellation terms closely. One operator's published refund tiers contradict each other.[10]

When to Go

From December to April, nights on the islands are cooler and breezier. From May to November, evenings are warmer and more humid, with a better chance of seeing bioluminescence in the water.[7] Another operator names April to June as the best time.[14] For how the seasons play out on each coast, see our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Panama.

Good to know · PACKING

Pack a headlamp, a power bank, earplugs, reef-safe sunscreen, quick-dry clothes and a dry bag. A sleeping-bag liner helps in basic cabins.

Questions

Before you go

Are there hotels on the San Blas Islands?

Not in the usual sense. The Guna, who govern Guna Yala, allow no big hotels or chains. Lodging is Guna-run island cabins, a few small eco lodges and crewed sailboats. Booking sites call some cabins hotels, but expect thatch, sand floors and shared bathrooms.

Are there any resorts or luxury hotels in San Blas?

There are no resorts with pools or air conditioning on the islands. The most comfortable stays are fly-in lodges such as Sapibenega, which has private bathrooms, hot water and 24-hour electricity, and premium cabins in the Dutch Cays at about $650 per person for one night. For resort comfort, a crewed catamaran with air conditioning comes closest.

Which San Blas island is the best to stay on?

It depends on the crowd you want. Pelícano is calm and takes children. Aguja is quiet, with private rooms a solo traveler can book. Franklin is social and adults only. Aroma and Chichime are the cheapest and most backpacker-oriented. Compare them in our San Blas Islands guide.

How much does it cost to stay overnight in San Blas?

Island cabin packages run about $140 to $175 per person per night as of November 2026, including three meals and the 4x4 and boat from Panama City. Simpler lodges start under $100 per person but charge extra for the 4x4. Add the cash entry fee of about $22 per foreigner, plus drinking water and drinks.

Is Laguna Azul Eco Lodge in San Blas?

No, it is on Isla Popa in Bocas del Toro, at the other end of Panama's Caribbean coast. It is a small over-water lodge reached by flying to Isla Colón and taking a 35-minute boat ride. One published rate is $500 a night for a double, all-inclusive, but check the current meal plan, because listings disagree.

How long does it take to get from Panama City to San Blas?

About four hours door to island by 4x4 and boat. Pickups are around 5 a.m., the drive to Cartí takes about 2.5 to 3 hours and the boat 25 minutes to 2 hours. Small planes from Albrook take under an hour but can be canceled in bad weather.

Do San Blas cabins have electricity and private bathrooms?

Electricity is limited and bathrooms are usually shared. Most cabins have solar or generator power for a few hours in the evening, cold tank showers and a bathroom block outside. Some over-water cabins and the fly-in lodges have private bathrooms, so ask for one by name.

Can I book a San Blas cabin without a tour?

Yes, some island operators sell cabins directly, often with the 4x4 and boat included. If you book only the room through a hotel site, arrange the 4x4 and lancha yourself, because boat seats from Cartí are often pre-sold to tour operators.

Do I need cash in San Blas?

Yes, bring all the US dollars you will need. There are no ATMs on the islands and cards are not accepted. The entry fee, port fee, drinks and tips are all paid in cash. You also need your original passport, not a copy.
