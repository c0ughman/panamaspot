San Blas Islands (Comarca Guna Yala), Panama: the complete guide

The complete San Blas Islands guide: how to get there, day trips vs overnight stays, sailing, prices for 2026 and Guna Yala rules.

The San Blas Islands are the island chain of the Comarca Guna Yala, a self-governed Indigenous territory on Panama's Caribbean coast where the Guna people set the rules, run every cabin and collect the entry fee. Most visitors take a 2.5 to 3 hour 4×4 ride from Panama City to the port of Cartí and then a short boat ride. A day tour costs about $114 to $148 and two nights start around $275, plus a $22 cash entry fee for foreigners that some tours include and others charge on top.[1]

San Blas is the colonial-era name that maps and tour operators still use. The official name is Comarca Guna Yala, the "land of the Guna." The difference matters more than it sounds. This is not a resort coast but a territory where outsiders cannot own land, scuba tanks and drones are banned, and your passport is checked before you reach the port.

Expect basic comforts: shared bathrooms, limited electricity, patchy signal and cash for everything. In return you get clear water, small sand islands with a few palms and cabins, and Guna communities that run tourism on their own terms.

The comarca

What the Comarca Guna Yala is, and why San Blas works differently

Guna Yala is an autonomous Indigenous comarca, not a national park or a resort zone. Panama created the comarca on 19 September 1938, and the Congreso General Guna governs it.[2] That status grew out of the Guna Revolution of 25 February 1925.[3] Guna fighters attacked the Panamanian forces stationed in the islands during Carnival. US warships stopped the reprisal, and a treaty followed that let the Guna live under their own laws.[4]

The government recognized the spelling Guna Yala, rather than Kuna Yala, in October 2011, because the Guna sound is not a "K".[5] You will still see San Blas, Kuna Yala and the old Archipiélago de las Mulatas on maps and booking sites.[6]

The 2023 census counted 32,016 people living in the comarca, down from 33,109 in 2010.[7] That is the comarca's own population. The same census counts 112,319 Guna nationwide, many of whom live outside Guna Yala.[8]

The Ministry of Health puts the territory at 2,307.6 km², with 365 islands, four corregimientos and 120 populated places.[9] Other government documents give 2,358.2 km² and 3,206 km², so the area figure depends on which document you read.[9][2] Operator sites count anywhere from 350 to 400 islands. Most references agree on about 49 inhabited ones.[10] San Blas Adventures instead counts 49 villages, 42 of them on islands and seven on the mainland.[4]

Who governs Guna Yala

Three Saila Dummagan (great sailas) lead the Congreso General Guna. Each community has its own saila, who is both political and spiritual leader, and decisions are taken in the Onmaked Nega, the congress house.[4] Representatives of all the islands meet in two general assemblies each year.[3] On paper, the comarca is a single district with four corregimientos: Narganá, Ailigandí, Tubuala and Puerto Obaldía.[11]

The capital is Gaigirgordub, which was called El Porvenir until 1 July 2016.[12] It has an airstrip, government offices, the Museo de la Nación Guna and an artisans' cooperative.[13] Older guides and many tour pages still say El Porvenir.

Why outsiders cannot own land or islands

Guna law prohibits outsiders from owning property in the comarca.[3] The land is held collectively. A company, foundation or private agreement does not turn comarca land into property that can be freely sold.[14] In 2018 the Congreso cited Decreto 33 of 6 March 2015, which recognizes collective and exclusive Guna rights over about 120,000 hectares of mainland and nearly 365 islands.[15] This is why every cabin, boat and guide you use is Guna-run.

Guna Yala map: how the comarca is laid out

The comarca has the Caribbean to the north, Colombia to the east, Darién and Panamá provinces to the south and Colón to the west.[5] The archipelago runs about 226 km along the coast, ending just short of the Colombian border.[16]

Almost every road visitor goes to the western end, the Gardi sector. It runs from Gaigirgordub to Río Sidra (Urgandi) and has 28 communities, with Cartí Sugdub as its political center.[2] Cayos Holandeses lies farther out, about two extra hours by boat from Cartí.[17] Farther east, Ailigandí has a museum about the 1925 revolt, and Playón Chico is linked to the mainland by a bridge.[16]

Fees and rules

Guna Yala entry fee and visitor rules in 2026

Foreign visitors pay a $22 cash entry fee and Panamanian residents pay $7. The Congreso General Guna collects it at the checkpoint at km 19 of the El Llano–Cartí road.[1] One operator lists $23 and $8, so carry a little extra in small bills.[18] The fee is charged per person on the day you travel in, and one operator says children under 10 are exempt.[19] The money goes to the Guna authorities, not to the Panamanian government or your tour company.[19]

Whether the fee is in your tour price depends on the operator. AMPA Tours includes it in its $148 day tour but adds it to its multi-day trips. Sanblastours charges it separately on every format.[20] To get the resident rate, show a passport or residency card.[19] Everyone else needs an original passport. Sea San Blas does not accept photocopies at the entry check.[21]

Some inhabited islands also charge their own small visitation fee, and many Guna ask about $1 to be photographed.[16] If you are on a tour, your guide usually tells you when these come up.

Good to know · CASH

There are no ATMs on the islands and change is scarce. Bring the entry fee in exact bills plus a stack of $1 and $5 notes for island fees, coconuts, drinks and crafts.

The rules visitors must follow

The Congreso's Reglamento de Turismo requires Guna tourism operators to brief visitors on the code of conduct.[22] The list of prohibited activities in circulation is signed by the Congreso's tourism office and dated 2018.[23] It includes:

  • No drones, jet skis or kites.
  • No oxygen tanks for diving, which in practice means snorkeling only.
  • No walking through community streets in swimwear, and no nudity on beaches.
  • No spearfishing.
  • No dogs on the islands.
  • No photos or filming without permission at the congress house or at ritual and religious ceremonies.
  • No charter tourism, meaning outside boats operating without Guna authorization.

Breaking these rules can mean a fine and confiscation of your equipment.[23] Scuba diving is off the table everywhere in the comarca, and windsurfing is also prohibited.[16][24] If diving is the point of your trip, our guide to diving in Panama compares Coiba, Portobelo and Bocas del Toro. Do not take coconuts either. They belong to someone, and the Guna sell them for $1 or $2 each.[25]

Trip types

Which San Blas trip is right for you: day trip, one night, two or three nights, or a boat

Two nights on an island suits most people. It is the shortest stay that leaves one complete day out there, with no 5 a.m. road trip at either end. The other formats make sense in specific situations:

  • One free day only: a day tour, accepting a 13 to 14 hour day.
  • Short on time but want a sunset: one night.
  • First proper visit: two or three nights in a beach cabin.
  • Want to see many islands and sleep on the water: a shared sailboat.
  • Family, group or privacy: a private catamaran or yacht.
  • Traveling on to Colombia: a multi-day island-hopping crossing.

Day trip from Panama City

A day trip works but is a long day: listings run 13 to 14 hours door to door.[26] Pickup is usually between 5:00 and 5:30 a.m. You are on the islands from about 9:30 a.m. to mid-afternoon and back in the city around 6:30 to 7:30 p.m.[24][21] Day groups visit the same few islands at the same hours, and the islands go quiet once they leave.

One night

Sanblastours' 2-day, 1-night trip costs $190 per person. That includes door-to-door 4×4, boat transfers, a beachfront cabin, three meals and a Guna guide.[20] On day two the boat returns to Cartí at 2:00 p.m. and you are dropped off in Panama City around 6:00 p.m.[20] One night does not mean two full beach days. It means an afternoon, an evening and a morning.[21]

Two or three nights in an island cabin

Three-day, two-night trips start at $275 per person with both Sanblastours and San Blas Frontera.[20][27] San Blas Frontera's version spends the two nights on two different islands. It includes Panama City transfers, two breakfasts, three lunches and two dinners.[27] On Pelícano, Sea San Blas includes one excursion for one night, up to two for two nights and up to three for three nights or more.[21]

Want to move around? Panama San Blas Tours lists a 4-day trip that sleeps on a different island each night, from $499.[28] Beyond four nights, there is little to do but swim, read and repeat. That suits some people well and bores others.

Shared sailboat

Shared sailing trips start at about $200 per person per night, with a two-night minimum.[29] Most sailboat stays last four to seven days, and only a limited number of accredited boats have Guna permission to take tourists.[24] Sailing is the easiest way to see remote cays like Cayos Holandeses without long lancha rides.

Private catamaran or yacht charter

A private boat costs more than a shared cabin, but you set the route and the pace. For a family or a group that fills the boat, the price per person can come out close to a shared trip. Our guide to San Blas catamaran and yacht charters breaks down what a day, a weekend and a week cost.

Crossing to or from Colombia

San Blas Adventures sells a 5-day, 4-night speedboat island-hopping trip between Panama and Colombia for $595. It involves about two hours on the boat each day.[30] The route avoids the open-sea crossing, which the operator says otherwise takes 30 to 50 hours.[30] Sailboat crossings from Cartagena usually take three to five days and stop in San Blas.[31] Our guide to sailing from Panama to Colombia through San Blas compares the boats and how to vet a captain.

What a tour price includes, and what it leaves out

AMPA's $148 day tour covers the 4×4, the boat to two or three islands, lunch, snorkel stops, return transport, the $22 tax and one non-alcoholic drink.[29] Sea San Blas's Pelícano day trip includes shared 4×4 and boat transport, lunch and one mealtime drink. Breakfast at the morning stop is optional and paid separately, and the border tax is extra.[21] A Marriott-listed day tour run by Tao Travel 365 includes island taxes, snorkel gear and one drink.[32]

Prices well under $100, such as a "boat route from $79" listing, are partial prices.[33] Add the 4×4, the entry fee and lunch before you compare. Then budget pocket cash for photo fees of about $1, coconuts at $1 to $2, and molas from about $15 for larger pieces.[16][25]

Are the San Blas Islands expensive?

By Panama standards, yes. One 2026 estimate puts a budget traveler's daily spend in Panama at about $37.[34] A $297 two-night trip costs about eight days of that budget. The price reflects the 4×4 and boat logistics and the fact that every meal is cooked on an island with no shops. It also usually covers transport, all meals and excursions.

Getting there

How to get to the San Blas Islands from Panama City

For most travelers there is one practical route: a 4×4 from Panama City to the port of Cartí, about 2.5 to 3 hours, then a motorboat to your island.[35]

The only road links Panama City with the western end of the comarca around Cartí. It is passable only by 4×4 or small truck, there is no public transport, and Uber and taxis are not allowed in.[24] Departures leave before 5:00 a.m. for the El Llano–Cartí road, with an ID check at the checkpoint before the port.[17] Estimates for the drive range from 2.5 hours up to 4 hours, depending on the operator and the road that day.[24][16] AMPA sells a shared 4×4 round trip on its own for $140.[29]

From Cartí, the boat to the nearer islands takes about 25 to 30 minutes, and up to an hour for some.[32][36] Cayos Holandeses is about two hours more.[17] For every route compared side by side, including driving your own 4×4, see our guide to getting from Panama City to San Blas by 4×4, boat or flight.

Can you fly to San Blas?

Only in a limited way. Flights to the western airstrips, including Cartí and Río Sidra, stopped after the new road opened and Aeroperlas collapsed.[16] Aeroperlas ceased operations in 2012.[37] Most airstrips in the comarca can now be reached only by charter, and Blue Skies points to Corazón de Jesús as the best-served option for flying in.[24][38]

Check that any air-taxi operator is certified. Panama's civil aviation authority has warned that uncertified non-regular flights from Albrook face fines of $1,000 to $50,000.[39]

Good to know · BOAT RIDES

Lanchas are open boats, and passengers often arrive soaked on windy days. Put your phone and passport in a dry bag. If you get seasick, sit in the middle of the boat and skip a heavy breakfast.

The islands

San Blas islands worth knowing: where tours go and where to stay

Nearly all road visitors stay in the western Gardi sector, within about half an hour of Cartí. The usual bases are small cabin islands such as Pelícano, Perro Chico, Chichime, Aguja, Yanis and Aroma.[31][38] Cayos Holandeses is the remote option, with clearer water and fewer people.

  • Aguja: close to Cartí port and an easy first stop, including on day trips.

  • Cayos Limones: another group of cays known for clear water.

Most of these descriptions come from operator island lists.[38][16][31] The Franklin and Wailidup stays are AMPA's.[29]

Inhabited islands and Cartí Sugdub

The community islands are where the Guna live. They are crowded, practical places, not beach stops. Cartí Sugdub (also written Carti Sugtupu or Gardi Sugdub) is one of the most visited and one of the most densely packed.[6] Panama's climate plan says rising seas could permanently flood it and several neighboring islands. It describes the community's move to the mainland, about 15 minutes away by boat, as the region's first climate displacement.[40] By December 2025, many families had moved to the new mainland settlement of Isber Yala in 2024, while others were still waiting.[41]

Visit community islands with a guide, keep a shirt on, and ask before every photo. Rules on photography are stricter here than on the cabin islands.

Camping in San Blas

Some islands rent tent space or hammocks as well as cabins, and Chichime is the best known for it.[42][38] Camping is still arranged through the Guna family that runs the island, and the island usually provides your meals.

Accommodation

Can you stay on the San Blas Islands? Cabins, hammocks and boats

Yes. You can sleep in a Guna-run beach cabin, a hammock, a tent or aboard a sailboat, but not in a resort or a chain hotel.[42] Stays are all-inclusive by necessity, because the only restaurants are the ones run by the places you sleep.[16] Most accommodation has no air conditioning, TV, internet or hot water, and some has no electricity.[16]

Bathrooms are usually shared, even in private cabins.[21] Most places do not provide towels, soap or shampoo, and electricity is limited, so bring a flashlight or power bank.[24] Have your stay arranged before you leave Panama City. Islands are hard to contact, and some operators say you cannot visit without a booked stay.[24]

Weather

San Blas weather: rain, wind and sea by season

San Blas is warm all year, about 27 to 29°C by day and 23°C at night, and rain is possible in any month.[24] Unlike Panama's Pacific side, the Caribbean coast has no truly dry season. Rain eases somewhat in February and March.[43]

Dry season vs rainy season

Operators usually call December to April the best time, with more sun and calmer water.[31] Others cut that window to December to March.[24] Panama's weather institute notes that northern frontal systems bring rain to the Caribbean side between December and February.[21] In January and February, short spells of strong wind and rain, lasting up to three days, can hit places near the Caribbean coast.[44]

That is why one operator recommends April to November instead and calls the rest of the year windy, with rocky boat rides.[45] In practice, December to March tends to bring more sun and more wind. The wetter months bring more showers and often calmer water. Seasonal forecasts can override the averages: the Caribbean, including Guna Yala, was forecast up to 20% wetter than normal for December 2025 and January 2026.[43]

Sea conditions, visibility and hurricanes

On clear days, underwater visibility can reach 30 meters.[16] The islands lie outside the hurricane belt, so a direct hit is very unlikely.[24] Distant Atlantic storms can still stir up the sea.

For a recent snapshot, the forecast for 14 October 2026 gave Caribbean highs of 29 to 31°C, east-northeast winds of 5 to 15 km/h, and isolated afternoon showers over the Guna Yala mountains.[46] For how San Blas fits with the rest of a Panama trip, our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Panama covers both coasts.

Good to know · WIND

For boat crossings, wind matters more than rain. Check a marine wind forecast such as Windguru for the Cartí or El Porvenir area a day or two ahead. Leave a spare day before any flight home, because operators can delay crossings in rough seas.

Culture

Guna culture: molas, dress, food and how to be a good guest

The Guna are a matrilineal people, meaning family lineage and inheritance pass through the mother.[47] Traditionally newlyweds live with the bride's family.[25] Dulegaya, a Chibchan language spoken by 50,000 to 70,000 people, is the language of daily life. Spanish is used in schools and written documents.[4]

A hand-sewn, layered textile panel; the word means "clothing" or "blouse" in Dulegaya.

Molas and traditional dress

Molas are made by reverse appliqué: layers of colored cloth are cut and stitched to reveal the colors below. The oldest known examples are thought to be 150 to 170 years old.[5] Designs were long geometric. In the last 50 years, makers have added flowers, animals, birds and sea scenes.[5]

Guna women wear molas as the blouse panels of their traditional dress, and the panels sold to visitors come from the same work. Larger pieces sell from about $15.[25] Buy directly from the maker when you can, so the money reaches her.[14]

Guna food

Traditional food is simple. Tule masi, a plantain and fish soup, is the common dish, and fish is often smoked to keep.[4] Cabin islands usually serve fish, and lobster or red snapper from the grill when the catch allows.[42] Coconuts were the main currency until the late 1990s, and a good year still brings in more than 30 million of them.[3]

Etiquette on the islands

  • Photos: always ask first. Many Guna ask about $1, and buying a craft usually earns the photo for free.[16]
  • Dress: Guna men do not go shirtless and women cover cleavage, stomach and most of the legs. Match that in the villages.[3]
  • Gifts: school supplies for children are welcome; sweets are not.[25]
  • Trash: there is no working waste system, so take your rubbish back to Panama City.[3]
Practicalities

Money, phone signal, health and what to pack

San Blas runs on cash, and you should bring all of it from Panama City. There are no ATMs on the islands, and cards are rarely accepted.[31][38] Carry $1 and $5 bills, because change is scarce.[24]

Mobile signal is unreliable across the area.[42] A prepaid SIM or eSIM from Tigo or Más Móvil gives you the best chance of some data.[31] Do not plan to work from the islands.

Medical care in the comarca is basic. The main clinic for the western islands is on Río Sidra, and anything beyond simple care means the long trip back toward the city. Bring any medicine you need, seasickness tablets and a small first-aid kit. For crime and road safety across the country, our honest guide to whether Panama is safe for tourists covers the city and the highways.

What to pack

  • Original passport, not a copy.
  • Entry-fee cash plus small bills for extras.
  • A dry bag for the boat.
  • Towel, soap and shampoo.
  • Flashlight or power bank.
  • Snorkel mask, if you prefer your own.
  • Seasickness tablets and sun protection.
  • A bag to carry your trash out.
  • One small bag only: tours often ask you to leave big luggage in the city.
Booking

How to book a San Blas trip without the common mistakes

Most travelers book a package from a Panama City operator that bundles the 4×4, the boat, the cabin and meals. Booking directly with an island is cheaper, but islands are hard to reach and most hosts speak only Spanish.[24] Booking platforms such as Airbnb add a significant premium.[24] For day, overnight and private trips compared operator by operator, see our guide to San Blas tours from Panama City.

1

Decide on nights first

The number of nights sets everything else. Two nights gives one complete day on the islands; one night gives an afternoon and a morning.

2

Choose the sleeping set-up

Pick a private cabin, a group cabin, a hammock or a boat cabin. Ask about the bathroom, which is usually shared.

3

Check inclusions line by line

Confirm meals, number of excursions, the 4×4 both ways, the boat, the entry fee and any cash charges on the day.

4

Fix pickup and return

Agree the pickup point and drop-off time. Leave slack before flights in case weather delays the boat back.

5

Pay through official channels

Read the cancellation terms, keep the confirmation, and pack your passport and cash.

Those steps follow the booking sequence Sea San Blas lays out.[21] Legitimate Guna tourism businesses register with the comarca's tourism registry, and the rules require them to brief you on the code of conduct.[22]

Be wary of foreign-run boats offering San Blas trips. The Congreso has rejected foreign sailboats operating without permission, and only a limited number of accredited sailboats may take tourists.[15][24] The same goes for uncertified air taxis out of Albrook. If anyone offers to sell or lease you an island or a plot, stop before paying and find out which land regime and authority actually apply.[14]

Questions

Before you go

How far are the San Blas Islands from Panama City?

About 2.5 to 3 hours by 4×4 to the port of Cartí, then 25 minutes to an hour by boat to most islands. Some operators quote up to 4 hours on the road. Remote cays such as Cayos Holandeses add about two more hours by boat.

Can you drive from Panama City to San Blas?

Only in a 4×4 or a small truck. The single road to Cartí is too rough for ordinary cars, there is no public transport, and Uber and taxis are not allowed into the comarca. Most visitors use a shared 4×4 booked with their tour. Every option is compared in our guide to getting from Panama City to San Blas.

Can you do a day trip to San Blas from Panama City?

Yes, for about $114 to $148 per person as of October 2026. Expect a 5:00 a.m. pickup, about five hours on the islands and a return around 6:30 to 7:30 p.m. It is a 13 to 14 hour day, so most visitors who can spare the time stay at least one night.

How much is the Guna Yala entry fee?

$22 for foreigners and $7 for Panamanian residents, paid in cash at the checkpoint on the El Llano–Cartí road. One operator lists $23 and $8, so carry a little extra. Some tours include it, but many charge it separately.

Are the San Blas Islands worth visiting?

Yes, if you are happy with basic comforts. You get clear water, tiny sand islands and Guna-run hospitality, but also shared bathrooms, limited electricity and almost no signal. Two nights is the stay most visitors find worth the long road in.

Are the San Blas Islands expensive?

They cost more than most of Panama, but trips are mostly all-inclusive. A typical two-night trip comes to about $297 with the entry fee, covering transport, meals and excursions. Budget extra cash for island fees, coconuts, drinks and molas.

Can you stay overnight on the San Blas Islands?

Yes, in Guna-run beach cabins, hammocks, tents or aboard a sailboat. There are no resorts or chain hotels, and bathrooms are usually shared. Our guide to where to stay in San Blas compares the options by island and price.

When is the best time to visit San Blas?

December to April is the usual pick for sunshine, with February and March the least rainy. The Caribbean side gets rain all year, and December to February can bring strong northerly wind and choppy crossings. Temperatures stay around 27 to 29°C year-round.

Can you scuba dive in Guna Yala?

No. Scuba tanks are banned in the comarca, along with drones, jet skis, kites and spearfishing. Snorkeling is allowed and is the main activity, with the best reefs around Cayos Holandeses.

What is the capital of Guna Yala?

Gaigirgordub, which was called El Porvenir until July 2016. It has an airstrip, government offices, the Museo de la Nación Guna and an artisans' cooperative. The comarca itself is governed by the Congreso General Guna.
