Chorro Las Mozas: El Valle's Swimming Falls

Chorro Las Mozas in El Valle de Antón: how to reach the swimming falls, trail time from town, pools to swim in, and what to bring before you go.

Chorro Las Mozas is a set of three small waterfalls and river pools on the Río Antón, about 2 km north of central El Valle de Antón. It is a 5-minute taxi ride or a roughly half-hour walk from town, followed by 15 to 30 minutes on a riverside path, and the posted entry is $2 for adults as of 2026. It is worth going for a cool swim in shallow pools, but the path is narrow and slippery, and you should stay out of the water whenever the river runs brown after rain.[1]

Chorro Las Mozas is where El Valle goes to get into the water rather than just look at it. Chorro El Macho, on the other side of town, is the tall fall people photograph. Las Mozas is a local swimming spot: a community-run balneario with a car park, a river path and a string of pools between the three falls.

Plan on one to two hours, or longer if you continue upriver to the bigger natural pool. If you are still choosing between falls, our guide to all the El Valle de Antón waterfalls covers every one in the crater.

Overview

What Chorro Las Mozas is and why people go

Chorro Las Mozas is a series of waterfalls on the Río Antón, on the road toward Cerro La Cruz. Each fall is an easy walk from the last, and you can swim in the pools they form. Many visitors stop at the first or second fall instead of walking the whole stretch.[2] The falls are modest, around 10 to 12 meters high.[3] The site is run by a community cooperative.[4]

The draw is the swim, not the height. It works as a balneario, with bathrooms and free covered cabins near the parking area where families settle in for the afternoon. Reviews describe it as a short walk and a refreshing swim. Some people swim right by the car park and others walk farther upriver. It holds a 4.5 rating on Google from 578 reviews.[5]

Is it worth the walk?

Yes, if you want to swim in a river on a warm morning and you are steady on wet rock. No, if you are after one dramatic waterfall, if you have toddlers who would want to go beyond the first pool, or if heavy rain has fallen in the hills that day. Travelers who mainly want a big fall for photos usually do better at Chorro El Macho, the valley's tallest waterfall.

The legend of the three sisters

The name means "the falls of the maidens", and the best-documented version of the legend goes like this. Tobalo, an indigenous warrior, had three daughters who all secretly loved Caobo, a young man famous for the balsería game. Caobo loved someone else. On a full-moon night the sisters met at a large stone by the river and wept until their tears became the three falls.[1] In some tellings the sisters then entered the water and still live there. On San Juan night they offer gold, and anyone who reaches for it is pulled into a whirlpool.

Another version circulates in which each fall is a sister who was scorned by her parents.[6] Local accounts that trace the story to oral tradition treat the unrequited-love version as the original.

Getting there

How to get to Chorro Las Mozas from town: route, trailhead and walking time

Chorro Las Mozas is about 2 km north of El Valle's center, in the opposite direction from Chorro El Macho.[3] You can walk, cycle, drive or take a taxi to the entrance on the road. From there a riverside path leads to the falls. A fifth option is to finish the long India Dormida ridge hike at the falls.

By taxi, on foot or by bike

A taxi from the town square to Chorro Las Mozas takes about 5 minutes and usually costs $2 to $3.[7] Taxis in the valley have no app. You flag them on the street or have your hotel call one, and trips within the valley generally run $2 to $5.[8] Agree on the fare before you get in, and arrange a pickup time if you don't want to walk back.

Walking the 2 km takes roughly half an hour along the road. It is pleasant early in the day and less so in the midday heat or in rain. A bike makes the trip quick, and it pairs well with other stops in the north of the crater. Bring a lock, because you will leave the bike at the parking area while you walk the river path.

By car

If you drive, you stop at a booth on the road before the parking area and pay without getting out of the car. Parking costs $3 per vehicle on top of the per-person entry.[1]

The path follows the river from the balneario area. It begins as concrete with a railing. Signs put the first fall at 5 minutes, but most people take closer to 15.

3

The path narrows into a ledge carved into the canyon wall, and spray can wet it.[1] Some stretches are only wide enough for one person at a time.

4

Optional: the large natural pool about 30 min more

The main walk along the Río Antón takes about half an hour. You can continue roughly another half hour to a larger natural pool.[9]

Arriving on the India Dormida circuit

Fit hikers can make Las Mozas the end of a full day. The India Dormida route runs about 6 km and takes 4 to 6 hours. It is rated moderately difficult and passes Piedra Pintada, the summit and three waterfalls before ending at Chorro Las Mozas. The trail fee is listed at $2 for locals and $5 for foreigners.[4] From the ridge you reach Cerro La Cruz and come out on the road between El Valle, Antón and Penonomé. You then walk past the cemetery to Cerro Violín, where a trail drops down to the falls beside the Río Antón.[4]

The common starting point, Piedra Pintada, is a 10 to 15 minute walk from the town center.[10] Our India Dormida hike guide covers the ridge itself, and our comparison of the best hikes in El Valle shows how it stacks up against Cerro Gaital and the waterfall walks.

Do you need a guide?

Not for the falls themselves. The river path from the parking area is short and hard to lose. On the India Dormida circuit a guide is optional. Certified guides are available at the trail entrance, or you can follow the trail posts on your own.[4] There is no published guide fee for this route, so agree on a price at the trailhead. In the rainy season, from May through November, vegetation closes in and the route is harder to follow, and going with someone who knows it makes sense.[7]

Fees

Chorro Las Mozas entry fee, parking and opening hours

The most recently posted entry fee is $2 for adults, $1 for children and $0.75 for retirees with a Panamanian ID (cédula). It is paid at the booth on the road, and parking is $3 per vehicle.[1] Older listings, including our English waterfall guide, give $1 for adults, $0.50 for children and $0.75 for seniors, so expect the lower figure to be out of date.[7]

Opening hours are not reliably posted. Treat Las Mozas as a daytime site, arrive in the morning and expect to be out by mid-afternoon. In the rainy season it can close early if the river rises to a dangerous level.[1] Listings describe it as a family balneario with rules against alcohol and loud music, so leave the speaker and the cooler of beer at the hotel.

Good to know · CASH

Fees are small, but everything is paid in cash at the booth. Carry singles and coins, because breaking a $20 at a rural entrance booth is not guaranteed.

Swimming

Las Mozas waterfall swimming: where to swim and how deep the pools are

The pools at Las Mozas are mostly shallow river pools, ranging from wading depth to around waist height. There are no measured or posted depths anywhere on the site. Some people swim in the river right by the parking area, and others walk farther up for quieter pools.[5]

The third fall is where most serious swimmers end up. It is also where you see people jumping from a raised ledge into the pool below. Depth there changes with the river level and nobody checks it for you. If you are tempted, wade in first and confirm the landing spot is deep and free of rocks, every time. In the driest months the pools can be too shallow for comfortable swimming at all.[7]

The larger natural pool, about half an hour beyond the main falls area, is the place for a proper swim. Fewer people make the extra walk.[9] There is no lifeguard service anywhere along the river, so treat this as unsupervised wild swimming. Never swim alone, and keep children within arm's reach.

Which pool suits whom

  • Families with small children: the river near the parking area and the first fall. It is shallow and close to the bathrooms and cabins.
  • Confident swimmers: the third fall and the larger natural pool, in clear water and dry weather.
  • Anyone, after rain: no pool at all if the water is brown or murky. See the next section.
Safety

Safety on the trail and at the water

Las Mozas is easy to reach, but it is the riskiest waterfall in the valley. The rocks are slippery and there are no guardrails beyond the first stretch of path.[7] The two hazards are the ledge path to the upper falls and a river that can rise fast.

The path

After the railing ends, the path becomes narrow and uneven and runs as a ledge cut into the canyon wall, where spray from the river keeps the rock wet.[1] Walk it in shoes with real grip and keep your hands free. Pass slower walkers only where the path is wide. Reaching the second fall is the most exposed part. Local accounts advise that children should not go past the first fall.

Flash rises on the Río Antón

The Río Antón can rise within minutes after heavy rain in the upper crater, even when it is not raining in El Valle itself. If the water looks brown or turbid when you arrive, don't swim. Staff usually close access to the third fall in those conditions.[1] This is not a theoretical risk. In October 2024 a river surge flooded around 30 homes in the Caña Blanca area of El Valle de Antón.[11] SINAPROC, Panama's civil protection agency, set up an early-warning camera system on the Antón basin after repeated flooding.[12] Its standing advice in the rainy season is not to cross swollen rivers or streams.[13]

Good to know · WATER COLOR

Clear or greenish water is normal. Brown, murky water or a sudden rise in noise and current means rain upstream. Get out and move to higher ground on the path, even if the sky above you is blue.

Simple rules that cover most risks

  • Go in the morning, before afternoon storms build over the hills.
  • Check the water before you get in, and check it again every so often.
  • Don't jump until you have checked the landing depth yourself.
  • Don't walk the ledge section in flip-flops or carrying a bag in each hand.
  • On the India Dormida circuit, the hazards also include strong wind on the ridge in the dry season, lightning on exposed sections and drops without railings.[4]
When to go

Best time of day and season to visit Chorro Las Mozas

The best time is a weekday morning in the early dry season. There is still water in the river by then, the rain has eased, and you have the pools before the local crowd arrives. Afternoons in the rainy season bring frequent showers, and the site can close early if the river rises.[1]

By season

  • May to November (rainy season): the falls carry the most water, but the path is wetter, vegetation closes in and the trail is harder to follow beyond the main falls.[7] For a visit in July or August, check conditions with your accommodation or the El Valle municipality that morning.[1] November, the end of the rains, can still bring heavy afternoon storms.
  • December to early January: usually the best balance. The rain has eased and the pools are still full.
  • January to March: January and February are the driest months, with low water. By February and March the pools may be too shallow for a comfortable swim.[7]

By day of the week

Las Mozas is a popular local meeting place, so weekends and Panamanian holidays bring families, picnics and full cabins. Arriving early on a weekday is the simplest way to get the pools largely to yourself.

Packing

What to bring to Chorro Las Mozas and what to leave behind

Bring shoes that grip on wet rock, cash, a swimsuit and a way to keep your phone dry. Leave behind anything you would hate to drop on the ledge path. The list is short because the walk is short, but footwear decides how far you get.

  • Footwear: trail shoes, sport sandals with a heel strap or water shoes. Flip-flops make the ledge section hard and unsafe.
  • Cash: small bills and coins for entry, parking and the bathrooms.
  • Swim gear: swimsuit, quick-dry towel and a change of clothes. You can change near the cabins.
  • Dry bag or zip-lock: for your phone and cash on the spray-soaked path.
  • Water and a snack: food options are limited at the falls, so eat in town before or after.
  • Rain layer: from May to November, assume an afternoon shower.

Leave behind speakers, alcohol and glass bottles, given the site rules. Don't bring a heavy daypack or anything valuable you would have to leave unattended while you swim.

Compared

How Chorro Las Mozas compares with the other falls around El Valle

Las Mozas is the pick for swimming. Chorro El Macho is the pick for a single impressive fall, and the India Dormida falls suit hikers who want waterfalls as part of a longer walk. El Macho lies in the opposite direction from Las Mozas.[3] A taxi from Las Mozas on to El Macho usually costs $3 to $4, so seeing both in one morning is easy.[7]

Fitting Las Mozas into your day

A morning swim at Las Mozas, a taxi to El Macho, then lunch in town is a common half-day. It leaves the afternoon for the Sunday market, the hot springs or a rest. Questions

Before you go

Is Chorro Las Mozas worth visiting?

Yes, if you want a river swim rather than a big waterfall. The three falls are modest, around 10 to 12 meters, but the pools are the best swimming in El Valle. Skip it after heavy rain or if you want one dramatic fall to photograph, which is what Chorro El Macho offers.

How do I get to Chorro Las Mozas from El Valle de Antón?

Take a taxi for about $2 to $3, or walk roughly half an hour. The entrance is about 2 km north of the town center, in the opposite direction from Chorro El Macho. You pay at a booth on the road, then follow a riverside path from the parking area to the falls.

How long is the walk to Chorro Las Mozas?

About 15 minutes to the first fall and around half an hour to the main falls area, measured from the parking area. A larger natural pool lies roughly another 30 minutes upriver. Walking from town to the entrance adds about half an hour each way.

How much does Chorro Las Mozas cost?

$2 for adults, $1 for children and $0.75 for retirees with a cédula, as of 2026. Parking is $3 per vehicle. Older listings show $1 for adults, so bring a little extra cash in small bills.

Is it safe to swim at Chorro Las Mozas?

It is reasonably safe in clear water and dry weather, but there are no lifeguards. The rocks are slippery and the river can rise within minutes after rain upstream, even if it isn't raining in town. If the water is brown or murky, don't swim.

How deep are the pools at Las Mozas waterfall?

Most pools run from wading depth to about waist height, and nothing is measured or posted. The pool at the third fall, where people jump from a ledge, is deeper but varies with the river level. In February and March the pools can be too shallow to swim comfortably.

Can you jump from the falls at Chorro Las Mozas?

People do jump from a raised ledge at the third fall, but it is unsupervised and at your own risk. Depth changes with the season and after rain. Always wade in and check the landing spot for depth and rocks before you jump.

Do I need a guide for Chorro Las Mozas?

No, not for the falls themselves. The river path from the parking area is short and easy to follow. If you arrive on the 4 to 6 hour India Dormida circuit, a certified guide is optional and can be hired at the trail entrance. In the rainy season, going with someone who knows the route helps.

When is the best time to visit Chorro Las Mozas?

A weekday morning between December and early January usually gives the best mix of full pools and less rain. In the rainy season (May to November), go early to beat afternoon storms. Weekends bring local crowds.

What is the difference between Chorro Las Mozas and Chorro El Macho?

Las Mozas is for swimming and El Macho is for the view. Las Mozas has three small falls with river pools along a path. Chorro El Macho is the valley's tall headline fall, with a paid trail and a separate pool fee. A taxi between them costs about $3 to $4, so many visitors see both in one morning.
