Bocas del Toro Real Estate: Island Homes and Land for Sale

Buying property in Bocas del Toro as a foreigner: which islands, titled vs ROP land, real prices, island logistics and how to buy safely.

Bocas del Toro real estate runs from small houses on Isla Colón listed around $150,000 to oceanfront estates and private islands well above $1 million, but the price is the easy part. The real risk is the title. Much of the island land is sold as rights of possession (ROP) rather than registered title, so the safest buys are titled properties, mostly on Isla Colón, checked by your own lawyer before any money moves. Banks rarely lend on ROP land, it does not count for residency by investment, and it has to go through a separate legal process to become title.

The province is nine main islands plus a large piece of mainland in Panama's northwest corner, on the Costa Rican border.[1] Most foreign buyers looking at Bocas del Toro real estate want the archipelago, not the mainland.[2] Asking prices in late 2026 run from about $59,000 for a new two-bedroom house in mainland Almirante to $22.5 million for private islands and estates.[3]

This guide covers what that range buys, which islands have titled land, how ROP works and how it gets titled, what a purchase costs on top of the price, and what daily life looks like when water comes from the sky and the commute is a boat.

Overview

Buying Bocas del Toro Real Estate: The Honest Picture

Bocas is a small, cash-driven, title-sensitive market where the paperwork decides whether you own an asset or an argument. Buyers from the US, Canada, Europe and Latin America have been coming for about two decades.[4] Over that time the area has also built up a history of property scams, usually land sold without a clear chain of possession or with boundaries nobody surveyed.

Three facts shape almost every deal. First, a lot of island land is ROP rather than titled.[5] Second, buyers usually pay cash, because Panamanian bank financing is mostly unavailable in Bocas.[6] Third, outside Bocas Town and a few developments, a home means solar panels, rain tanks and a boat.

The national rules are friendly to foreigners. Foreign buyers generally have the same ownership rights as Panamanian citizens on titled property.[4] Our guide to buying property in Panama as a foreigner explains the countrywide process. This page covers what is different on the islands.

Who Bocas Suits, and Who Should Look Elsewhere

Bocas works for buyers who can pay cash, are comfortable with boats and rain, and want a Caribbean home or a small rental business more than a fast resale. It is a poor fit in four cases:

  • You need a mortgage. Banks generally will not lend against ROP land, and even titled Bocas property is usually bought for cash.[3][6]
  • You want residency through the purchase. ROP land does not qualify for residency by investment, so your choice narrows to titled property at the right price.
  • You need regular specialist care. Isla Colón has a public hospital and private clinics, with more in Changuinola, but specialists mean a trip to David or Panama City.[3]
  • You dislike rain. Annual rainfall in the province reaches 6,000 mm.[7]

Living costs are not low either. There are no large supermarkets, only smaller shops, and food costs more than elsewhere in Panama because most goods arrive by boat.[1] For a countrywide comparison of budgets and towns, see the guide to living in Panama as an expat.

Good to know · LISTING COUNTS

Portals disagree wildly on how much is for sale. One shows 1,807 "houses" in the province and another 40. Duplicates, mainland lots and stale listings inflate the big numbers, so a local agent's current inventory is a better read of the market.

Where to Buy

Isla Colón vs Carenero vs Bastimentos vs the Mainland: Where to Buy

Isla Colón is the safest first purchase for most foreigners. It has the airport, roads, grid power in parts and the deepest stock of titled lots. The other islands trade convenience and paperwork certainty for quiet, water views and lower land prices per square meter.

Isla Colón: Services, Roads and Most of the Titled Land

Bocas Town is the commercial center, with the airport, the grocery stores and the services everyone else on the archipelago boats in for.[4] Big Creek, just outside town, has road access and larger plots.[4] Paunch, near the surf break, is where new houses with pools are listed at $445,000 to $609,000 and apartments at $179,500 to $414,000.[3]

Titled lots on the island's west coast are advertised at 5 to 12 minutes' drive from town, and lots on the road to Drago at 20 to 25 minutes.[8] Some of these advertise electricity at the road or a few hundred meters away, which is the detail that decides your build budget.

Isla Carenero: Town Across the Water

Carenero sits a short water-taxi ride from Bocas Town and draws surfers. Listings include a titled parcel "with concession options" and beachfront lots near the north-side surf break.[9][6] Inventory is thin, so prices swing with whatever is on the market that month.

Isla Bastimentos: Old Bank and Red Frog

Bastimentos mixes Old Bank village, the Red Frog Beach development and long stretches of national park. Red Frog listings run from $225,000 for ocean-view lots to $750,000, with two-bedroom condos at $399,000 and marina slips at $79,500.[10] A house in Old Bank is listed at $325,000.[11] Marine-park land cannot be privately titled.[3] Developments here usually come with community fees, so ask for the current amount and what it covers before you make an offer.

Solarte and the Outer Islands

Solarte is a few minutes by boat from Bocas Town and known for jungle homes, waterfront houses and private compounds.[4] Many homes out here are sold as ROP: a Discovery Bay house on Solarte and a bayfront home with a shared pool are both listed that way.[8] Private islands and estates are listed from about $1.3 million to $22.5 million.[3]

Whole islands come with an extra rule. Island land can be sold for tourism only within an approved tourism development zone, and no more than 50% of an island may go to foreigners or a majority-foreign company.[12]

The Mainland: Cheap, Large, and Partly Off-Limits

Almirante has the lowest entry prices in the province, with new two-bedroom houses from about $59,000 and 27 hectares of acreage listed at $500,000.[3] At the other end, a four-bedroom home at Santa María Golf in Almirante is listed at $2,056,768.[11]

The mainland has two catches. Parts of it fall inside the 10 km border zone where foreigners cannot own land.[5] Changuinola and Almirante also sit in flood-prone ground, and both were put on yellow alert for flooding in July 2026.[13]

If you want to live on one island before committing, our guide on which Bocas del Toro island to stay on compares Isla Colón, Carenero and Bastimentos by area and budget.

Land Title

Titled vs ROP Land in Bocas del Toro

Titled land is registered property you own outright. ROP is a recognized right to occupy national land, with no registry record behind it. The difference decides whether you can borrow, get residency, insure the title and resell cleanly.

Titled (titulada)
Registered at the Public Registry with a finca number, a registered survey and a searchable history. Eligible for bank mortgages and title insurance.[3]
Rights of possession (ROP, derechos posesorios)
No registry record, boundaries that can be informal, and purchases that are almost always cash.[3] You buy the seller's possession, not a title.
Overwater concession
Structures over the sea are allowed through a state concession, not ownership. The water and seabed are never part of the title.[3]
Public domain
Beach strips, mangroves, national and marine parks, comarcas and other public land cannot be privately titled at all.[3]
Good to know · LISTING LABELS

"Titled" in a listing is a sales claim, not proof. Ask for the finca number and have your own lawyer pull the record at the Public Registry before you sign a promise to purchase.

Turning ROP into Title: Law 80 of 2009

ROP on coastal and island land can become title through ANATI, the national land authority, under Law 80 of 2009. The law requires peaceful, uninterrupted possession as owner for more than five years.[14] ANATI's own procedure sets the same threshold.[15] You may read elsewhere that seven years are needed; the statute and ANATI both say more than five.

5+ yearspeaceful possession needed to title island or coastal land
10 mpublic beach strip on the Caribbean, never titled
50,000 m²largest parcel eligible for a free Law 80 title

The five years do not have to be yours. A buyer can step into the prior possessor's time, so a seller's documented years count toward your claim.[16] That makes the paper trail of past possession as valuable as the land. ANATI accepts proof such as certifications from the corregimiento or the mayor's office, at least three sworn statements from neighbors, photographs, appraisals and utility contracts.[15]

Parcels of up to 50,000 m² can receive a free title, with no administrative charges and free registration.[14] Possession can rest on tourism, residential, commercial or agricultural use, among others.[17] Lawyer and surveyor fees still apply, though. One firm quotes $3,500 to $12,000 for the full process and 12 to 36 months when nobody files an objection.[18] Not every ROP parcel can be titled: if it sits in a park, a mangrove or a beach strip, no amount of possession changes that.

Beachfront: The 10-meter Strip and What Can Never Be Titled

On the Caribbean coast, the first 10 meters inland from the high-tide line are public beach.[16] Beyond that strip, land out to 190 meters further inland is the coastal zone that can be titled under Law 80.[16] Riverbanks, lakes, ports, estuaries, Indigenous territories and ecological reserves are excluded.[16]

Titles granted under Law 80 must keep public access to the beach open. Blocking it is fined from B/.1,000 to B/.5,000, or B/.5,000 to B/.20,000 for repeat offenses.[16] Our guide to beachfront and land in Panama covers these rules countrywide.

Building Over the Water

Overwater houses are what many buyers picture, and they are the most regulated thing you can build. Owners who have been through it describe a sequence: you hold the land behind the water, apply for a concession usually granted for 20 years and renewable, and get further permits including an environmental impact study. Applications can be rejected, especially over coral. Check the concession's term, holder and renewal date on any overwater home before you buy it.

The Border Zone

Foreign individuals cannot own land within 10 km of the national border.[3] The ban also covers companies with foreign capital, so a Panamanian corporation does not get around it.[5] The islands are outside the zone, but parts of the mainland province are inside it.[5] For any mainland parcel, your lawyer should confirm its distance to the Costa Rican border.

Why Title Matters for Residency

The Qualified Investor visa needs $300,000 in a new, first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale property, and the property must be titled.[3] The value that counts is the lower of the price paid and the market value, minus any mortgage. The funds must come from abroad, and you must keep the property for five years. Government fees are $5,000 to the National Treasury plus $5,000 to the National Immigration Service for the main applicant. ROP land never qualifies.

The Friendly Nations visa accepts a titled property of $200,000 or more if your country is on the list.[3] The list covers 51 countries; Colombia, Venezuela, Italy and India are not on it. The Pensionado visa rests on a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month, or $750 with a $100,000 property, so ask your lawyer whether a specific property qualifies before you rely on the lower figure. The guide to Panama residency visas compares all three, and retiring in Panama covers the Pensionado route in depth.

Houses, Villas and Condos

On Isla Colón, a one-bedroom house in Paunch is listed at $179,000, a three-bedroom at Sunset Point at $699,000 and the five-bedroom Pool House at Paunch at $1,300,000.[19] A luxury estate in Big Creek is listed at $975,000.[20] Developer projects add jungle homes in Burn Brae Plantation at $339,000 to $599,999.[1] Bocas Homes describes Burn Brae as villas built on titled lots.[21]

On Bastimentos, Red Frog has jungle rental lodges at $344,000 and a five-bedroom ocean-view house at $650,000.[10] Old Bank has a three-bedroom house at $325,000, and Dolphin Bay a three-bedroom at $450,000.[11] Bocas del Toro condos for sale are mostly in these developments: two-bedroom Red Frog condos at $399,000 and Paunch apartments from $179,500.[10][3]

Hotels, Overwater Buildings and Income Property

A large share of what is for sale is business property. One agency's inventory is 32% land, 27% business and 24% homes.[6] Examples include the titled El Pelícano Hotel at $490,000 and an overwater hotel and rental building in town at $695,000.[20] There is also an overwater resort in Big Bight at $625,000, an eco-resort on Isla Pastores at $475,000 and a titled downtown commercial building with a dock at $475,000.[10]

Claims such as "high occupancy" or "strong rental history" come from the seller. Ask for booking records and bank deposits, and check whether the overwater part has a current concession.

Good to know · RENTAL INCOME

Panama taxes only Panamanian-source income, and rent from a property in Panama counts as Panamanian-source. Budget for tax on any rental income, wherever you live.

Costs

Closing Costs, Taxes and Mortgages for a Bocas Purchase

On a titled resale, the buyer's main closing cost is the lawyer, at about 1% to 1.5% of the price, plus small notary and registry fees.[3] The bigger extra on the islands is often making the house livable off the grid.

Transfer Tax and the 2026 Exemption

The 2% property transfer tax (ITBI) is the seller's tax, calculated on the higher of the price and the cadastral value. Any contract clause making the buyer pay or reimburse it is void.[22] Since 1 September 2026, Law 546 of 2026 exempts the first B/.120,000 of the taxable base on the first sale of a new home, if the sale is formalized within two years of the occupancy permit.[22] Above that, reduced rates apply up to B/.200,000, and the general rate applies beyond.[23][24] This mostly affects developers selling new builds, but it can show up in their pricing.

Annual Property Tax and Costs When You Sell

Titled property pays annual property tax under Law 66 of 2017. The brackets run from 0% to 1%, with a separate regime for a primary residence. Paying the full year before 30 April with no arrears earns a 10% discount.

When you sell, capital gains tax is 10% of the gain, with an advance of 3% of the price paid before the sale is registered. Factor that into any plan to buy, build and flip.

Can You Get a Mortgage in Bocas?

In practice, plan on cash. Bank financing is mostly unavailable for Bocas purchases, though some private sellers finance short term.[6] Several island listings advertise owner financing.[8]

Panamanian banks do lend to foreigners on titled property in general. Foreign buyers usually need at least 30% down, and terms rarely exceed 15 years.[25] Down payments of 30% to 50% and rates of roughly 4% to 7% are the figures commonly quoted for foreign buyers in 2026.[26][27] Expect to provide 12 to 24 months of bank statements, two bank reference letters, two years of tax returns and an appraisal by a bank-approved valuer.[25] Developer financing may be offered on pre-construction condos.[1]

Logistics

Water, Power, Boats: Island Logistics

Off Isla Colón, plan on generating your own power, catching your own water and owning a boat. Many outer-island homes run entirely on solar and rainwater. On-grid living is possible in parts of Isla Colón, Isla Carenero, Old Bank and the Red Frog area. Elsewhere, assume you are on your own.

Flights from Albrook take about an hour.[3] Check current schedules, which change by season. Copa also flies from Tocumen to David, the nearest airport on the Pacific side.[28]

By road, the trip to Almirante takes 8 to 9 hours, then about 30 minutes by water taxi.[3] Shared water taxis from Almirante leave about every 30 minutes from 6:00 to 18:00 and take 25 to 35 minutes.[29] Fares are $5 or $6 per person depending on the operator, paid in cash.[29][30] The full route options are in our guide on how to get to Bocas del Toro.

For building materials and vehicles, a car ferry runs daily except Monday, leaving Almirante at 7:00 and Isla Colón at 15:00.[31] Ferry Bocas lists one-way rates of $40 for a car with up to five passengers and $80 to $380 for trucks and trailers by size.[32]

Between islands, water taxis are everywhere, but most island residents end up wanting their own boat.[33] Fares climb the farther you are from Bocas Town, which adds up if you commute daily for groceries.

Good to know · LAST BOAT

No water taxis cross from Almirante at night. Arrive by about 5:30 pm or plan to sleep on the mainland.

Electricity

Grid power reaches parts of Isla Colón, Carenero, Old Bank and Red Frog, and almost nothing beyond. Under Law 6 of 1997, the distributor must connect users within 100 meters of a line in its area without charging a contribution. Farther away, connection is still mandatory but may need extra investment.[34] That is why listings stress "electricity at the road" or "200 meters away".[8]

Panama's tourism master plan lists a power line project for Isla Colón and Isla Bastimentos, with no date attached.[35] Do not buy on the assumption that it will arrive. For off-grid homes, owners report solar systems costing upwards of $10,000.

Water and Septic

Rainwater is the default supply on most islands, and with rainfall reaching 6,000 mm a year, quantity is rarely the problem.[7] Storage, filtration and pumps are what cost money. Owners put a combined rain catchment system, pump, water heater and septic at $5,000 or more. Some Isla Colón developments have piped water: one west-coast gated community lists an aqueduct for its canal-front lots.[8]

Flooding, Storms and Sea-Level Rise

Panama lies south of the main hurricane belt, so hurricanes are not the main threat.[3] Rain and the sea are. Panama's national climate report singles out the Bocas archipelago for its exposure to sea-level rise.[36] In August 2026, the civil protection agency SINAPROC put Isla Colón, Changuinola, Almirante and Chiriquí Grande on green alert for rain and flooding.[37]

Low lots next to mangroves are the most vulnerable. Panama's own development plan for the province flags housing built in mangrove areas and calls for flood zoning.[7] Check a lot's elevation and drainage after heavy rain, and get an insurance quote before you sign.

Groceries, Building and Healthcare

Most shopping is in Bocas Town, so living elsewhere means a boat ride to stock up.[1] Building materials also arrive by sea and then by boat to your dock, which adds cost and delay to any construction or repair.

For healthcare, Isla Colón has a public hospital and private clinics, Changuinola has more, and specialists are in David or Panama City.[3] The guide to healthcare in Panama for foreigners covers the private hospitals and insurance options. Ask any seller for a real internet speed test at the house, since service varies a lot from island to island.

Process

How to Buy Property in Bocas del Toro Without Getting Burned

Buying safely in Bocas comes down to two habits: rent before you buy, and pay for your own lawyer's due diligence before any deposit. Most bad Bocas stories involve skipping one or the other.

The Purchase, Step by Step

1

Rent first a few months to a year

Long-time residents' most common advice is to rent in Bocas for a season before buying. It shows you which island suits you, what rain and boat life really feel like, and which agents are reliable.

2

Talk to several agents before you shortlist

Each agency carries different listings. Local names include Buena Vista Realty, Paradise Found Real Estate, United Country Bocas del Toro and Bocas Locals Properties. Treat for-sale-by-owner posts in Facebook groups with extra caution.

3

See it in person and walk the boundaries by boat if needed

View the property in person, by boat or by video, and walk the boundaries of any land. On ROP land, the boundaries may exist only as neighbors' agreement.

4

Your own lawyer's due diligence before any deposit

The lawyer checks the title or the ROP chain, surveys, the shoreline strip, protected areas and the border zone.[3] On ROP, insist on documented possession history good enough for a Law 80 application. On overwater homes, check the concession.

5

Promise to purchase with escrow

Agree on the price and sign a promise-to-purchase agreement, with the deposit held in escrow, never paid straight to the seller.

6

Close and register

Titled property is transferred through a Panamanian notary and registered at the Public Registry.[4] ROP is signed over as an assignment of rights, with no registry entry.[3]

Red Flags Specific to Bocas

  • A seller who cannot show how long, and from whom, they have held an ROP parcel.
  • Lots that include beach, mangrove or water inside their "boundaries".
  • An overwater house sold with no concession document, or one close to expiry.
  • Pressure to wire a deposit before your lawyer has seen the file.
  • Mainland acreage near Changuinola or the Sixaola river with no check against the 10 km border zone.
  • Rental income figures with no booking records behind them.

Questions

Before you go

Can a US citizen buy real estate in Bocas del Toro?

Yes. Foreigners, including US citizens, have essentially the same rights as Panamanians to own titled property. The exceptions are land within 10 km of the national border, which affects parts of the mainland but not the islands, and special rules for selling whole islands. Rights of possession (ROP) land can be bought too, but it is not a title. See buying property in Panama as a foreigner.

What is the difference between titled land and ROP in Bocas del Toro?

Titled land is registered at the Public Registry with a finca number and survey; ROP is a right to occupy national land with no registry record. Titled land can be mortgaged, title-insured and used for investor residency. ROP is almost always bought for cash, can have informal boundaries and does not qualify for residency by investment.

Can ROP land be turned into a title?

Often yes, through ANATI under Law 80 of 2009, after more than five years of peaceful, uninterrupted possession. A buyer can add the seller's documented years to their own, which is why the paper trail matters. Land in beach strips, mangroves, parks or Indigenous territories can never be titled.

How much does a house in Bocas del Toro cost?

Most houses are listed between about $150,000 and $1 million as of November 2026. A small two-bedroom on Isla Colón is listed around $149,500, island homes on Bastimentos around $325,000 to $356,000, and large Big Creek houses near $980,000. New two-bedroom houses in mainland Almirante start around $59,000.

Can I get a mortgage to buy property in Bocas del Toro?

Rarely. Most Bocas purchases are cash. Banks generally will not lend against ROP land, and even on titled property, Bocas financing is mostly unavailable. Where Panamanian banks do lend to foreigners, expect at least 30% down and terms of up to about 15 years. Some sellers and developers offer short-term financing.

Is it expensive to live in Bocas del Toro?

It costs more than most of Panama for everyday goods. Food and supplies arrive by boat, there are no large supermarkets, and living off Isla Colón adds water-taxi or boat costs. An off-grid home also needs solar and water systems, reported at upwards of $10,000 and $5,000 respectively.

Do I need a boat to live in Bocas del Toro?

On any island except Isla Colón, practically yes. Water taxis serve all the main islands, but fares rise with distance and most residents end up buying a boat. Isla Colón is the only island with roads linking town, Big Creek, Paunch and Drago.

Can I build an overwater house in Bocas del Toro?

Only under a state concession, never as owned property. The water and seabed are not part of any title. You generally need the land behind the site, a concession, often granted for 20 renewable years, plus permits including an environmental impact study, and projects over coral can be rejected.

Does buying property in Bocas get me residency in Panama?

Only if the property is titled and meets a visa's minimum. The Qualified Investor visa needs $300,000 in a new first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale, and the Friendly Nations visa accepts a $200,000 property for eligible nationalities. ROP land never qualifies. Compare the options in Panama residency visas.

What are the downsides of living in Bocas del Toro?

The main ones are title risk, cash-only buying, heavy rain and limited healthcare. Rainfall in the province reaches 6,000 mm a year, low lots near mangroves can flood, and specialist care means traveling to David or Panama City. Everything from groceries to building materials costs more because it comes by boat.
