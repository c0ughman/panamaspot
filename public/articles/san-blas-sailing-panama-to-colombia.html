A San Blas sailing trip from Panama to Colombia takes five days: about three days island-hopping in Guna Yala, then 26 to 60 hours of open sea to Cartagena, for roughly $525 to $750 per person on a shared boat as of November 2026. Blue Sailing, the largest booking agency on the route, lists the crossing from $695 per person for four or five nights aboard.[1] The Guna Yala entry fee of about $20 to $22, your transfer to the port, drinks and tips are paid on top.

No road connects Panama and Colombia. The Darién Gap cuts the highway, so anyone traveling overland between the two countries either flies or goes by boat. The boat options are San Blas sailing from Panama to Cartagena, or a speedboat that hops along the Guna Yala coast to the Colombian border villages.

This guide covers the sailing route in both directions: how the five days run, where the boats leave from, which operators and boats work the route, what it costs in 2026, and how to pick a boat and captain you can trust. If you only want time in the islands and plan to return to Panama City, our complete guide to the San Blas Islands (Guna Yala) covers day trips, island cabins and the Guna rules.

Your Options

Sailing Panama to Colombia: Sailboat, Speedboat or Flight

Sail if you want the islands and a real sea passage. Take the speedboat if you want more island time and no open ocean. Fly if you are short on days or know you get badly seasick. All three get you to Colombia, but they end in different places and cost different amounts once onward travel is added.

The speedboat is a different product, not a cheaper sailboat. San Blas Adventures sells a 5-day, 4-night trip across the archipelago for $595, with about two hours on the boat each day and a night in a Guna community.[2] Guests sleep in hammocks or simple huts on the islands, and the trip ends near the border rather than in Cartagena.[3]

That end point matters for your budget. From Capurganá you take another boat to Necoclí, then a bus that runs about 11 hours to Cartagena or Medellín.[4] Blue Sailing, which sells the sailboat alternative, adds that the onward trip often needs an overnight stop.[3]

Flying is the plain option. One-way fares between Panama City and Colombian cities have usually run somewhere around $130 to $300 in recent years, so the flight is often cheaper than the boat once you add the boat's extras. What the flight can't give you is three days at anchor in Guna Yala.

If you want the islands but not the crossing, a shared sailboat out of Panama City is the middle ground. Blue Sailing lists budget shared charters at $495 to $650 per person for three nights.[5] Our guide to San Blas catamaran and yacht charters compares day, weekend and week prices for private and shared boats, and San Blas tours from Panama City covers day trips and one- or two-night trips.

The Itinerary

The San Blas Sailing Experience, Day by Day

A Panama-to-Colombia sailing trip usually runs five days: a transfer and boarding day, about three days among the islands, then a non-stop open-sea leg that lands you in Cartagena on the morning of the last day. San Blas Dreams describes it as three days of snorkeling, fishing, swimming and relaxing in the islands, then two days sailing to Colombia.[4] The exact islands depend on the captain, the wind and the sea.

1

Pickup and transfer to the coast 3-4 h

Depending on the boat, you ride a shuttle or bus to Puerto Lindo, or a 4x4 to the Guna Yala coast followed by a speedboat to the anchored sailboat. The 4x4 leg takes about 2.5 to 3 hours and the speedboat about an hour.[6] Pickups in Panama City are usually before dawn, and some boats hold a briefing the day before.

2

First sail into the islands 1-8 h

Boats that board inside Guna Yala are among the islands soon after the speedboat drops you off. Boats leaving from Linton Bay Marina near Portobelo sail about eight hours to the first islands.[7] That leg is often done overnight.

3

Island days about 3 days

Typical anchorages include Chichime, Nugnugdup, Dog Island (Perro Chico) with its shipwreck snorkel, the tiny Coco Bandero cays and the Cayos Holandeses.[8] Days are swimming, snorkeling and going ashore. Guna fishermen often paddle out to sell lobster and crab, and a coconut on the islands costs about $2.[9]

4

The open-sea crossing 26-52 h

The boat leaves the reef shelter of the eastern islands, usually in the afternoon or evening, and sails non-stop to Colombia. Blue Sailing puts the crossing at 26 to 52 hours, depending on weather and sea.[3]

5

Arrival in Cartagena morning

Blue Sailing boats dock at Club Náutico in Manga, a five-minute taxi ride from the walled city. The captain coordinates the migration paperwork before you go ashore for good.

What the Open-Sea Crossing Is Like

210 nmopen-sea distance, about 390 km
26-60 htime at sea, weather-dependent
3 daysin the islands before or after

The crossing covers about 210 nautical miles of open Caribbean, and Blue Sailing quotes 30 to 60 hours at sea on its route page.[1] La Gitanita puts its own crossing at about 30 hours.[7] The spread comes from wind, swell and how much the captain motors versus sails.

There is little to do at sea except sleep, read, watch for dolphins and flying fish, and take your turn in the cockpit. Some travelers find it the dull part of the trip. Others find it the point of it. Seasickness is the most common complaint, so read the seasickness section before you book.

Alcohol rules vary by boat. Some captains ban drinking during the open-sea stretch because of the risk of falling overboard. Blue Sailing notes that its 64-foot Quest allows drinks but does not suit guests whose priority is heavy drinking.[10]

Getting to the Boat

Where the Boats Leave from and How to Get There from Panama City

Sailboats to Colombia leave from Puerto Lindo, Portobelo or Linton Bay on the Colón coast, or from inside Guna Yala via Cartí and El Porvenir, and your boat decides which. San Blas Dreams lists Puerto Lindo, Portobelo and Cartí as departure points.[4] Blue Sailing says its Panama departures and arrivals use Puerto Lindo or El Porvenir, depending on the boat and direction.[6]

Good to know · Road access

The road into the comarca runs under Guna rules and can pause. In October 2026 the Guna General Congress announced that transport would resume on its usual schedule on October 11. Ask your boat the day before whether pickups are running normally.

Boats and Operators

Blue Sailing: The Largest Agency on the Route

Blue Sailing has sold the Panama–Colombia route since 2012 and describes its fleet as independent captains.[3] Its boat list for the crossing runs to about 19 vessels carrying 10 to 20 passengers each, from the 10-passenger Quest to Sovereign Grace and Wild Card at 20.[11] After a departure is confirmed, the agency emails the boat and cabin options available on that date.[12]

Blue Sailing San Blas trips go both ways: Panama to Cartagena from $695 per person for four or five nights, and Colombia to Panama from $695 for five nights.[5] For last 2026 departures it lists selected dates from $670, with price varying by departure, boat and cabin type.[13]

Blue Sailing reviews are mostly positive, and its review badge requires a score above 4.5 from reviews over the past 12 months.[10] The weak point is the agency model itself. Independent travel blogs have reported uneven boats on the same platform, including bed bugs on some boats. Reviews of the agency tell you little about the specific boat. Ask for the boat's name and read reviews of that boat and its captain.

San Blas Dreams

San Blas Dreams is a broker that connects travelers with captains and says booking through it costs the same as booking directly with the captain.[4] It lists sailing fares of $525 to $750 and a speedboat trip to Sapzurro from $525, depending on season.[4] It is useful for finding a seat when the bigger boats are full, but confirm the boat, captain and itinerary directly before paying.

San Blas Adventures vs Blue Sailing

San Blas Adventures runs speedboats, not sailboats, and has done so since 2011.[2] The two products suit different travelers. San Blas Adventures gives you more time on islands and a night in a Guna community, with no 30- to 50-hour ocean leg. Blue Sailing's boats land you directly in Cartagena, with a kitchen, bathroom and bed aboard instead of a hammock ashore.[3]

Recent San Blas Adventures reviews, from July 2026, mention a storm that stopped one group reaching its final island, wet boat rides, and having to withdraw and carry a large amount of cash for payment.[2] The same batch of reviews praises the island time, and one reviewer noted that nobody got seasick. If you mainly want the islands and don't mind a long overland day at the end, the speedboat is the better fit. If Cartagena is your next stop, the sailboat saves a day or two.

Wild Card Sailing

Wild Card says it has carried more than 15,000 passengers since 2010 and advertises beach parties, snorkeling and swimming on its trips.[14] The boat is listed at 20 passengers, among the largest on the route.[11] It suits solo travelers who want a busy, social boat more than those who want quiet anchorages.

La Gitanita

La Gitanita is a 12-passenger monohull that departs from Linton Bay Marina near Portobelo.[11] It quotes about eight hours from Linton Bay to the first islands and about 30 hours on the open-sea leg to Cartagena.[7]

Ave Maria Sailing

Ave Maria is a 50-foot sailboat that has run the route out of Portobelo with a small group of about eight passengers plus crew, each with their own bed. Travel blogs have reported a standard rate of around $550 per person in past years. That figure is not a current published 2026 fare, so ask the boat directly for current prices and departure dates.

Boat Type

Monohull vs Catamaran for the Panama–Colombia Crossing

A catamaran is the steadier choice for the open-sea leg, and a monohull gives you more of the feeling of sailing. Most boats on Blue Sailing's crossing list are monohulls, so you may have to book early to get a catamaran on your date.[11]

Monohull

Most of the fleet10-20 passengerse.g. Quest, Koala X

A single hull gives the classic sailing sensation, and as a private charter a monohull can cost less, depending on size. The 64-foot steel Quest, built in 1995, takes 10 guests in four cabins and has a water-maker for freshwater showers.

Best for travelers who want to feel the boat sail

Catamaran

Fewer on the list12-17 passengerse.g. Santana, Ti-Vaou

Two hulls give more stability, more deck space and a shallow draft for calm lagoons. Catamarans of 45 feet and up are wide enough to cut movement noticeably, while 40- to 42-foot cats still move.

Best for first-timers worried about seasickness

Blue Sailing's catamaran guide credits catamarans with stability and deck space and monohulls with a more direct sailing feel.[15] A catamaran operator adds that boats of 45 feet and over almost cancel out movement, while 40- to 42-foot catamarans still roll a little.[16] Size and headcount matter as much as hull type. On a 20-passenger boat, space in the cockpit and the queue for the bathroom feel very different from a boat carrying 8 or 10.

Ask exactly how many beds the boat has and how many passengers are booked. Travelers on some budget boats have described 15 to 20 people with some sleeping on deck or on the floor. Boats with dormitory-style bunks and bare communal areas tend to be party boats.

What the Fare Includes

  • Your bed or cabin on the boat for four or five nights
  • Three meals a day, drinking water, coffee and tea
  • Snorkel gear and a dinghy with an outboard motor for trips ashore
  • Coordination of the usual migration procedures

That list is Blue Sailing's for its shared crossing.[12] San Blas Dreams says its fares also cover non-alcoholic drinks and the immigration paperwork to Colombia.[4]

What It Doesn't Include

  • Transport from Panama City to the departure point
  • The Guna Yala entry fee
  • Alcohol, snacks and other personal spending
  • Tips for the crew, which are optional
  • Travel insurance

Blue Sailing lists all of these as excluded from its route fare.[18] Special diets cost an extra $25, and guests are encouraged to bring their own snacks, drinks and alcohol.[1]

The Guna Yala Entry Fee

The Guna General Congress, not the national government or the boat, sets and collects the entry fee, and it is paid in cash.[19] One 2026 price guide gives $20 entry plus a $2 Cartí port fee, $22 in total for foreign visitors, with Panamanian nationals and residents paying $7.[20] Blue Sailing still quotes $20 per person.[21] Carry $22 per person in small bills, and ask your captain where the fee is collected on your route.

Deposits and Paying the Balance

Expect to pay a small deposit online and the rest in cash. Blue Sailing takes a deposit of 10 to 20 percent of the trip price.[22] San Blas Dreams takes a $50 down payment, and the balance goes to the captain in cash on departure day.[4] There are no ATMs in the islands, so withdraw the balance in Panama City before your pickup.

Safety

Safety: How to Choose a Boat and Captain

The sailing route is run by professional crews and many trips pass without incident, but safety depends on the specific boat and captain, not on the agency that sells the seat. Every boat on the route is crewed: bareboat charters are not allowed in San Blas because of unmarked reefs, shifting sandbars, Guna permits and cultural rules.[15] Your job is to check that the crew and boat you get are the right ones.

The Equipment a Properly Fitted Boat Carries

A boat set up for offshore passages lists its safety gear openly. One Bavaria C51 listed for San Blas passages, for example, carries AIS, radar, a VHF radio with DSC, a satellite phone, satellite tracking, an EPIRB, smoke and CO detectors, and branded life jackets.[23] Panama's Maritime Authority (AMP) reminds operators that life jackets and communication radios are mandatory.[24] Ask your boat for its equipment list. A captain who can't or won't answer is your answer.

The Captain's Papers

In Colombia, DIMAR issues navigation titles to crew and officers, and applicants must prove health fitness, the required sea time for the grade and a clean criminal record.[25] A Colombian charter operator advises asking for the boat's DIMAR registration, the captain's professional navigation licence and proof of current passenger insurance.[26] You can't easily look these up yourself, so ask for them in writing before you pay.

Get These in Writing Before You Pay a Deposit

  1. The boat's name, type and length, and the captain's name
  2. The maximum number of passengers and how many are booked on your date
  3. Your bed or cabin, and whether anyone sleeps on deck
  4. The departure point, meeting time and whether the transfer is included
  5. The weather-delay and cancellation policy, including refunds if the boat turns back
  6. Who handles your Panama exit and Colombia entry stamps
  7. Whether the boat allows alcohol during the crossing

Red Flags from Traveler Reports

The complaints that recur in reviews and backpacker blogs are consistent. They include captains drinking or high on duty, food poisoning from poor galley hygiene, tap water served as drinking water, bed bugs, and a trip that turned back after an hour for "weather" with no refund. Travelers have also reported hostels and agents claiming a boat was broken or cancelled to steer them toward a higher-commission boat. Check reviews of the specific boat by name, and contact the boat directly if a story sounds off.

Travel insurance is not part of any fare. Choose a policy that explicitly covers sea passages and medical evacuation from remote areas. For safety in Panama more broadly, including Panama City and transport, see our honest guide to whether Panama is safe.

Season

Best Time to Sail San Blas to Colombia

June to November gives the calmest crossing, and December to May gives drier days with stronger trade winds and bigger swell. Blue Sailing calls December to May the wind season, with steady trade winds and mostly dry days, and June to November the calm season, with brief showers, calmer seas and fewer travelers.[8] October and November tend to be the wettest months.

Blue Sailing describes San Blas as outside the hurricane belt and sails it every month.[8] Sudden local squalls still happen. The Guna call them "culo de pollo," because the palm fronds fan out like a chicken's tail when the wind hits.[8] Christmas and New Year departures run as limited holiday sailings and fill early.[12] For weather across the rest of the country, see our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Panama.

Good to know · Weather delays

Departures can be pushed back for bad weather or anything else the captain judges unsafe, so keep your onward plans flexible.[4] Panama's Maritime Authority also suspends departures from Caribbean ports in rough conditions, including Puerto Obaldía in Guna Yala.[24]

If you want to watch the sea state yourself, DIMAR's oceanographic center publishes marine forecasts for Cartagena and other Colombian ports.[27] Don't book a non-refundable flight out of Cartagena for the day you are due to land. A buffer of one day is the minimum.

Seasickness

Seasickness on the Crossing: What Works and When to Take It

Take your motion-sickness medicine before the crossing starts, not once you feel sick. The open-sea leg is where most people struggle. Several over-the-counter and prescription options work, and they differ in timing and side effects. Check with a doctor or pharmacist about which suits you, especially if you are pregnant, over 60 or traveling with children.

Scopolamine patch
Considered the most effective option, especially when used preventively.[28] Apply it behind the ear at least four hours before you need it. It can stay on for up to three days, after which you switch to the other ear.[29] In June 2025 the FDA warned of a risk of overheating, mostly in people under 17 or over 60. Remove the patch if your temperature rises or you stop sweating.[29]
Dimenhydrinate (Dramamine, Mareol)
Take the first dose 30 minutes to an hour before departure and repeat every 4 to 8 hours as needed.[30] Drowsiness is the most common side effect, and you should avoid alcohol while taking it.[30] Blue Sailing suggests Mareol or Dramamine one to two hours before the open-sea leg.[3]
Meclizine (Bonine)
Usually taken 30 minutes to an hour before travel, and considered milder than the scopolamine patch.[31]
Pregnancy
Meclizine and dimenhydrinate are the options generally recommended during pregnancy.[28] Confirm with your doctor before you travel.

Most of these medicines cause some drowsiness.[32] On a night crossing that can help. Buy your medicine in Panama City before the pickup, because there is nowhere to buy it once you leave the coast. If you know you get badly seasick even on short ferries, the speedboat route or a flight is the honest choice.

Immigration

Passport Stamps: Leaving Panama and Entering Colombia by Boat

You need a Panama exit stamp before the boat leaves Panamanian waters and a Colombian entry stamp in Cartagena, and on sailboats the captain or agency coordinates both. Blue Sailing includes coordination of the usual migration procedures in its fare.[18] San Blas Dreams includes the immigration paperwork to Colombia.[4]

Panama's migration rules require foreigners to use officially authorized land, air or sea posts and to be ready for document checks on entry or exit.[33] On the sailing route, the exit is handled at a port authority office on the Colón coast or at El Porvenir, which some boats use as an immigration checkpoint.[6] Speedboat trips stamp out near the border before crossing to Sapzurro.

On the Colombian side, Migración Colombia runs maritime control posts in Cartagena, Capurganá and Turbo.[34] Colombian law counts entering through an unauthorized point, or skipping migration control, as irregular entry.[35] Check that your passport carries a Colombian entry stamp before the captain hands it back. Colombia also runs an online pre-registration form called Check-MIG, so ask your captain whether to fill it out before arrival.

Families should note that Panama requires a notarized authorization, with the birth certificate, for minors leaving the country without both parents.[36] Carry the original and a copy, even though the rule is written with airports and land borders in mind.

Reverse Direction

Sailing Colombia to Panama: What Changes in Reverse

Sailing from Cartagena to Panama uses the same boats and route, but the open-sea crossing comes first and the islands come last. Departing from Cartagena, the first 30 to 50 hours are open sea before you reach the San Blas islands.[4] Trips usually leave in the afternoon or evening of day one and finish on the morning of day six.[4]

Blue Sailing lists the Colombia-to-Panama sailing from $695 for five nights aboard.[5] Its 2027 early-bird pricing also applies to Colombia–Panama sailings.[22] Boats finish at Puerto Lindo or El Porvenir, and from there you need a transfer back to Panama City.[6]

Which direction is better comes down to temperament. Going Panama to Colombia puts the islands first, while you are fresh, and leaves the rough leg for the end. Going Colombia to Panama gets the crossing over with, and you finish relaxed in the islands. Speedboat trips in this direction start near the border, and getting there from Cartagena takes most of two days overland. San Blas Adventures offers group transport from Cartagena for its trip.[2]

Entering Panama by sea, you need the same documents as at an airport: a passport valid for at least three months, proof of at least $500 in funds and proof that you will leave the country.[36] Have an onward flight or bus ticket ready to show.

Packing

Cash, Phone Signal and What to Pack

Bring all the cash you will need, in small US-dollar bills, because there are no ATMs anywhere in the islands.[6] You need it for the Guna Yala fee, the balance owed to the captain, coconuts, beers, molas and crew tips. Phone signal is weak or absent, and Wi-Fi is rare.[37] Some boats carry Starlink, but it is not guaranteed.[6]

Sail Sea San Blas suggests packing a dry bag, reef-safe sunscreen, a light long-sleeved shirt, motion-sickness remedies, a power bank, a headlamp, small bills, a quick-dry towel and a waterproof phone pouch.[38] Add a waterproof jacket for evenings and boat rides, which can be cool.[39] Pack your bag so it can get splashed in the dinghy, and bring your own snacks and drinks.

Good to know · Guna customs

Ask before photographing people in Guna communities.[37] On Blue Sailing charters you can bring a drone aboard, but flying it is prohibited.[5]

Questions

Before you go

How much does it cost to sail from Panama to Colombia in 2026?

About $525 to $750 per person on a shared boat, as of November 2026. Blue Sailing lists its standard crossing at $695, with some last 2026 departures from $670. Add $25 to $65 for the transfer to the boat and about $20 to $22 for the Guna Yala fee, for a realistic total of around $715 to $785 before drinks and tips.

How long does the San Blas sailing trip from Panama to Colombia take?

Five days, with four or five nights aboard. You spend about three days among the San Blas islands, then sail non-stop for roughly 26 to 60 hours to Cartagena, depending on wind and sea. Weather can delay a departure, so leave at least a day of slack in your plans.

Is sailing from Panama to Colombia safe?

It can be, but safety depends on the specific boat and captain, not on the agency selling the seat. Before paying, ask for the boat's name, its safety equipment (life jackets, VHF radio, EPIRB), the captain's licence and the passenger count. Read reviews of that boat by name, and buy travel insurance that covers sea passages and evacuation.

Should I choose Blue Sailing or San Blas Adventures?

Blue Sailing if you want to land in Cartagena by sailboat, San Blas Adventures if you want more island time and no open ocean. Blue Sailing is an agency for independent sailboats that dock in Manga, Cartagena. San Blas Adventures runs speedboats that sleep guests in hammocks on the islands and end near the border, followed by a long trip on to Cartagena or Medellín. See San Blas tours from Panama City if you only want the islands.

How much is the Guna Yala entrance fee?

About $20 to $22 per foreign visitor, paid in cash. It is set and collected by the Guna General Congress, and one 2026 guide lists $20 entry plus a $2 Cartí port fee. Panamanian residents pay less. It is not included in sailing fares.

What is the best time of year to sail San Blas to Colombia?

June to November for the calmest crossing, December to May for drier, sunnier days with stronger trade winds and bigger swell. Boats sail year-round, and October and November tend to be the wettest months. If seasickness worries you, the calmer season is the better bet.

Can I sail from Colombia to Panama instead?

Yes, the same boats run Cartagena to Panama. The 30- to 50-hour open-sea leg comes first, then about three days in the islands, and the trip ends at Puerto Lindo or El Porvenir. Blue Sailing lists it from $695 for five nights aboard.

How far is Panama from Colombia by sea?

About 210 nautical miles (roughly 390 km) of open sea separate the San Blas islands from Cartagena. Depending on wind and swell, the crossing takes from about 26 to 60 hours.

Can you drink alcohol on the sailboat?

Usually yes in the islands, but rules vary by boat. Alcohol is not included on budget crossings, so you bring your own. Some captains ban drinking during the open-sea leg because of the risk of falling overboard. Ask before you book.

Are there ATMs or Wi-Fi in the San Blas islands?

There are no ATMs, and signal is weak or absent. Bring all the cash you need in small US-dollar bills for the Guna fee, the captain's balance, drinks and tips. A few boats have Starlink, but it is not guaranteed.
