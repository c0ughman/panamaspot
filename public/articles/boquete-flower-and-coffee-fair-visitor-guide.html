The Boquete Flower and Coffee Fair (Feria Internacional de las Flores y del Café) runs for about 12 days each January on the fairgrounds beside the Caldera River in Bajo Boquete, and the 2027 edition is expected from January 6 to 17, though official confirmation was still pending as of November 2026. In 2026, adults paid $2.50, retirees with a Panamanian jubilado card paid $1.25, children up to 10 got in free, and the gates were open from 9 am to midnight.[1]

For these 12 days, Boquete has more visitors than its roads and hotels were built to handle. The fair opens Panama's national fair season.[2] Most of the crowd is Panamanian families who travel in from all over the country, and that local character is a big part of what makes the week worth seeing.

Time your trip to the fair if you want flowers, coffee tents, típica music and a town in party mode. If you are coming for quiet trails, cheaper rooms and coffee farms with open tour slots, the week after the fair closes has the same dry-season weather and much less competition for beds.

When

When the Boquete Flower and Coffee Fair happens in 2027, and why January is the busiest week

The 2027 Boquete Flower and Coffee Fair is expected to run from Wednesday, January 6, to Sunday, January 17, 2027. Those are the dates posted on the fair's own Instagram account. As of November 2026, the fair's organizing board was still planning the 2027 edition, and the dates had not appeared in an official government calendar. Confirm them in December before you pay for anything non-refundable.[3]

The expected dates match the recent pattern: the fair opens midweek in the first half of January and closes on a Sunday. The 2026 edition ran January 7 to 18, the 2025 edition January 8 to 19, and the 2024 edition January 11 to 21.[4][5][6] If the 2027 dates hold, the two fair weekends fall on January 9-10 and January 16-17.

In 2026 the gates opened at 9:00 am and closed at midnight.[1] Some listings show earlier closing times, but the evening is when the main stage concerts happen, so the late close is the figure to plan around.

Why fair week is the busiest week of Boquete's year

For the 2025 edition, the board's president expected about 190,000 visitors, and that was the first year the fair carried international status.[7] Most of those visitors come on the weekends. Boquete's own tourism master plan says the center already has poor pedestrian conditions because of the cars filling its streets, and that the problem gets worse on peak days such as fair week.[8]

The effect on prices is direct. The same hotel room can cost about 40% more in January than in October.[9] Flights to David and coffee farm tour slots also fill up. Booking details are in the sections below.

Ticket prices: is the Boquete flower festival free?

The fair is not free, but it is cheap. In 2026, general admission was $2.50, retirees paid $1.25, and children up to 10 entered free.[1] The board kept those prices the same as the year before.[10] The 2027 prices still need the board's confirmation, but entry has risen only slightly in recent years: it was $2.00 in 2020.[11][12]

You buy tickets at the box offices at the fairgrounds. There is no online pre-sale for general admission.[13] The 2026 entry ticket came with a grand prize draw for a brand-new car.[14]

Good to know · Senior discount

The $1.25 rate is the jubilado (retiree) discount, and it requires a Panamanian jubilado card. Visitors over 60 without that card should expect to pay the general rate. Foreign residents on a pensionado visa usually have the card.

Venues

Main venues: flower gardens, the orchid show and the coffee tents

Almost everything happens in one place: the fairgrounds along the Río Caldera in Bajo Boquete, the town center. The fair is run by a board (patronato) made up of the Municipio de Boquete, the agriculture ministry (MIDA), the tourism authority (ATP) and Boquete's chamber of commerce.[15]

The 2026 edition had 37 themed gardens.[1] The planting is the main draw: orchids, roses and hydrangeas set out in beds and arches.[16] Local townspeople spend months before the fair planting the beds by the river. Most visitors spend at least an hour or two just walking the garden paths.

The orchid exhibition

Growers display rare and award-winning orchids, many of them native to Chiriquí. The plants are labeled, and exhibitors are usually happy to explain how to care for them.[16] If you only have time for one indoor stop, make it this one.

Coffee tasting tents

Local farms pour their own beans at tasting tents, and some stalls explain the difference between washed and natural processing.[16] Tasting at the fair is a quick, cheap way to try several Boquete producers. That helps you decide which farm is worth a proper tour later in the week.

Main stage, crafts, food and rides

The main stage (tarima principal) hosts the fair's concerts, mostly Panamanian típica music, and folkloric dance groups in layered pollera dresses.[14][16] The craft stalls sell Ngäbe-Buglé handwoven bags, local honey and chocolate. The food stalls sell highland snacks such as empanadas and fresh strawberries.[16] The 2026 fair also had mechanical rides, food courts and cultural spaces.[14] Some editions include a parade of flower floats, but the route and timing are announced close to the event.[17][6]

Good to know · Orchids

Buy orchids to enjoy in Panama, not to take home. Orchids cannot cross borders without permits.[16] Panama's environment ministry has also suspended export permits for wild-origin species, orchids included, unless they come from legally managed sources.[18]

What to see and do on each fair day

The best day for the flowers is a weekday morning. The colors are freshest and the crowds are thinner early in the day,[16] and the weekend crowds have not arrived yet. Evenings belong to the main stage, and weekends belong to everyone.

Getting there

Getting to Boquete for the fair from Panama City and David

Most travelers reach Boquete through David, the provincial capital about 45 minutes downhill. The cheapest route from Panama City is a night bus to David, then the local bus up to Boquete. That costs about $18-20 in total and takes 8-9 hours.[19]

The night-bus route, step by step

1

Buy your ticket at Albrook same day

Padafront (also called Padovan) and Transportes Expreso David sell tickets at their windows at Albrook terminal. Online booking is unreliable, so plan to buy in person.

2

Ride the night bus to David about 6-7 hrs

Departures leave between 10:00 pm and midnight and cost $15-18.

3

Arrive at David terminal 5:00-6:00 am

The terminal is on Avenida Estudiante. The Boquete buses leave from the same station.

4

Local bus to Bajo Boquete 45-55 min

Buses run every 20-30 minutes from 5:00 am to 9:00 pm for about $1.75 and stop at the central park, a short walk from the fairgrounds.

Figures for this route come from 2026 timetables.[19] Published fares for the David-to-Boquete bus range from about $1.45 to $5, depending on the vehicle, so carry a few small bills.[20][21] A taxi from David costs about $15-20 one way.[20] Air Panama fares from Panama City to David rise to about $120-130 one way in December and January, compared with about $80 in September and October.[9]

Some guides also list direct buses from Albrook to Boquete, quoting about 6-8 hours for $20-30.[22] For the return, the last direct bus from Boquete to Panama City usually leaves around 4:00 pm.[22] The full comparison, including driving, is in our guide to getting from Panama City to Boquete by bus, flight or car.

Good to know · Day trips from David

The last bus down to David leaves around 9:00 pm, but the main stage runs until midnight. If you are staying in David and want the evening concerts, plan on a taxi back.

Getting around

Getting around Boquete during the fair: walking, taxis and bike rental

If you stay in Bajo Boquete, walking is the easiest way to reach the fair. The fairgrounds sit along the river in the town center, and the David buses stop at the central park nearby.[15] Driving in is the worst option on fair weekends, because the center's streets fill with cars even on ordinary days.[8]

Taxis

Taxis are the practical choice from the hillside areas such as Jaramillo, Alto Quiel and Volcancito, where the walk back up is steep and dark late at night. Agree on the fare before you get in. Around midnight, when the main stage closes, expect a wait at the taxi rank, or arrange a pickup with a driver you used earlier in the day.

Parking and parade days

Parking near the fairgrounds is hard to find on weekends, and streets in the center can close for a float parade. If you have a rental car, leave it at your lodging on fair days and walk or take a taxi into town.

Bikes and e-bikes

A bike is useful for getting away from the fair rather than to it. The roads up to the coffee farms and the Bajo Mono valley are quiet in the morning, and an e-bike handles the climbs. Our guide to Boquete bike rental compares e-bike and standard rentals, half-day and full-day prices, and the routes worth riding. Book a day ahead during fair week, because the fleet is limited.

Coffee

Coffee farm visits during the fair week

Boquete's coffee farms run their usual tours during fair week. The difference is demand: book before you arrive, not on the day. During high-demand periods, booking at least two months ahead is the safer choice.[23] The tasting tents at the fair do not replace a farm visit, but they help you pick which farm to tour.

Good to know · Cash

Bring small bills to the fair. Most stalls, including the coffee and craft vendors, do not take cards.[16] Farm tours booked online are usually prepaid or deposit-based, so you will not need much cash for those.

Lodging

Where to stay during the fair and how far in advance to book

For fair week, book your lodging at least two months ahead and expect to pay more than at any other time of year.[23] A room can cost about 40% more in January than in October. Hostel dorm beds rise from about $12-18 in the low season to $18-25.[9]

Which area to pick for fair week

  • Bajo Boquete (town center): walk to the fair, restaurants and the David bus. This is the most convenient area and the first to sell out. Rooms close to the river can hear the main stage until midnight.
  • Jaramillo, Alto Quiel and Volcancito: quieter and greener, with more cabins and houses. You will need taxis for every fair visit, especially at night.
  • David: a fallback when Boquete is full. It has more rooms, but it is 45-55 minutes away by bus, and the last bus up leaves at around 9:00 pm.

For noise, walkability, climate and price compared area by area, see our guide to where to stay in Boquete. If prices are the deciding factor, consider arriving the day the fair closes. You still get one afternoon of the last weekend and then a quieter, cheaper week afterward.

Weather

January weather, bajareque winds and what to pack

January in Boquete is dry, windy and mild. Expect warm, sunny days, cool nights and frequent bajareque: a fine, mist-like drizzle that the wind blows over the town, often with rainbows.[28] Daytime highs average about 21.5-24.5°C (71-76°F), and lows average about 15-17°C (59-62°F).[28][29]

Published averages for January rainfall range from about 52 mm to 101 mm, depending on the station and the years measured.[28][29] Either way, this is the dry season, which runs roughly December to April. The trade winds blow steadily from the northeast over the continental divide, and sometimes hard.[28] The bajareque comes with those winds, mostly in the dry, windy months.[30] It rarely stops a fair day, but it can soak a cotton shirt during a long garden walk. Our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Boquete explains how January compares with the green season.

What to pack for the fair

  • A light rain shell or windbreaker for the bajareque and the wind on open garden paths.
  • A warm layer for the evening concerts. By midnight, temperatures drop to around 15°C (59°F).
  • Sun protection: a hat and sunscreen for midday on the open grounds between the showers.
  • Comfortable walking shoes that can handle damp paths and a few hours on your feet.
  • Small bills for the entry ticket, food, coffee and crafts.
  • A small daypack for honey, chocolate and coffee bags. Leave the orchids for the photos.
Questions

Before you go

When is the Boquete Flower and Coffee Fair in 2027?

The 2027 fair is expected to run from January 6 to 17, 2027, the dates posted on the fair's own Instagram. As of November 2026, the dates were still awaiting official confirmation, so check again in December before you book anything non-refundable. Recent editions ran January 7-18 (2026) and January 8-19 (2025).

Is the Boquete flower festival free?

No, but entry is cheap: $2.50 for adults in 2026. Retirees with a Panamanian jubilado card paid $1.25, and children up to 10 entered free. You pay at the box offices on the fairgrounds, and general admission is not sold online.

What time does the Boquete Flower and Coffee Fair open and close?

In 2026 the gates opened at 9:00 am and closed at midnight. Mornings are best for the gardens and the orchid show. Evenings are for the main-stage concerts and dancing.

Which days are the least crowded at the fair?

Weekday mornings are the quietest. Crowds peak on the two weekends, especially the final one. If the 2027 dates hold, those weekends fall on January 9-10 and 16-17.

How do I get from Panama City to Boquete for the fair?

The cheapest route is the night bus from Albrook to David ($15-18), then the local bus to Boquete (about $1.75), for 8-9 hours in total. Flights to David cost about $120-130 one way in January. A private minivan costs about $90 per person. Our guide to Panama City to Boquete compares every option.

How far in advance should I book a hotel in Boquete for January?

Book at least two months ahead for fair week. Rooms can cost about 40% more in January than in October, and the walkable rooms in Bajo Boquete sell out first. See where to stay in Boquete for an area-by-area comparison.

Do Boquete coffee farms offer tours during the fair?

Yes, the farms keep running their regular tours during fair week, but book ahead. Published prices range from about $25-35 at Finca Lérida to $45-55 at Lamastus Family Estates, which requires advance booking. Our coffee farm tour comparison covers seven farms.

What is the weather like in Boquete in January?

January is dry, windy and mild, with highs around 21-25°C (71-76°F) and cool nights around 15-17°C (59-62°F). Expect bajareque, a fine wind-blown drizzle, so pack a light rain shell and a warm layer for the evenings.

Can I take orchids out of Panama after buying them at the fair?

Not without permits, and in practice it is rarely possible. Panama has suspended export permits for wild-origin species, orchids included, unless they come from legally managed sources. Enjoy the orchid exhibition and buy honey, coffee or crafts to take home instead.

Do stalls at the fair accept credit cards?

Most do not, so bring small bills. That goes for the entry ticket, food stalls, coffee tents and craft vendors. Withdraw cash in Boquete or David before you head to the fairgrounds.
