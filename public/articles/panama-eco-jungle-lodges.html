For an eco lodge in Panama, most travelers choose between two all-inclusive lodges on Isla Bastimentos in Bocas del Toro. Tranquilo Bay is the guided wildlife and birding lodge, at $306 to $366 per person per night in 2026, and La Loma Jungle Lodge is a seven-cabin chocolate farm reached by a daily 4 pm boat from Bocas Town.[1] Rambala (hike-in rainforest on the mainland), Isla Palenque (a luxury private island on the Pacific) and Canopy Lodge (birding in El Valle de Antón) suit different trips.

Most of Panama's eco lodges are in Bocas del Toro. Isla Bastimentos, the largest and least developed island in the archipelago, has no roads, and most of its interior is protected national marine park.[2] Staying there means boat transfers, steep jungle paths and all-inclusive rates, because there is nowhere else nearby to eat. Isla Palenque and Canopy Lodge take you to the Pacific and to the highland crater of El Valle instead.

Few of these lodges publish prices. Tranquilo Bay does. For the others, the figures below are booking-site and travel-media prices, dated, and they move with season and occupancy.

Bastimentos

Tranquilo Bay Eco Adventure Lodge: Rates, Transfers and Wildlife

Tranquilo Bay Eco Adventure Lodge is the Bocas lodge built around guided nature. It sits on Isla Bastimentos with rainforest on one side and the sea on the other.[5] The lodge borders the national marine park,[6] opened in 2005[7] and has six cabanas.[8]

Tranquilo Bay Rates for 2026

Tranquilo Bay publishes per-person, per-night rates based on double occupancy, valid through December 2026.[1]

The lodge splits the high-season rate into $130 for the cabana, $86 for meals and drinks and $121 for activities and facilities.[1] The rate covers air-conditioned cabanas with queen or double beds, meals, drinks, guided activities on site, use of the private conservation reserve and transfers between Bocas Town and the lodge.[1] Laundry is free up to what the water supply allows, guests eat with the hosts, and the kitchen plans menus around allergies and preferences.[9]

How to Get to Tranquilo Bay

You fly with Air Panama from Panama City to Bocas (Isla Colón), and the lodge meets the morning flight on Wednesdays and Saturdays. A transfer at any other time costs a $107 boat fee.[6] The boat ride is listed as 25 minutes in one listing and about 45 minutes, plus five minutes to the dock, in a birding-tour itinerary.[8][10] Our guide on how to get to Bocas del Toro compares flights with the bus and boat route.

Good to know · ARRIVAL DAYS

Plan the trip around Wednesday or Saturday morning flights. Arriving on another day adds a $107 boat fee to an already all-inclusive rate.

What You See, and Who It Suits

The lodge's Whisper Ecological Trail is a place to spot two-toed sloths in the canopy. Hummingbirds, red-eyed tree frogs and tropical warblers show up around the grounds, and parrotfish on snorkel trips.[5] Nature walks and night walks are guided.[11] The lodge also runs day tours and full-lodge buyouts for groups.[12]

The model suits travelers who want a set, guided program and no planning. It is less suited to anyone who wants to wander Bocas Town's bars and restaurants at night, since you depend on the lodge boat.

Bastimentos

La Loma Jungle Lodge and Chocolate Farm: Cabins, Prices and Getting There

La Loma Jungle Lodge is a working cacao and permaculture farm turned all-inclusive lodge. Its 55 acres run from the mangrove shore of Bahía Honda up to the highest point of Isla Bastimentos and border the national marine park.[13] You can only reach it by boat.[14]

Cabins and What Every Stay Includes

The lodge has seven cabins with 14 beds: Rainforest Queen (two queens), Rainforest King, King Treetop, Family Treetop (a queen and two singles), Rockpool (two queens), the three-bedroom Cacao Farm House and the two-bedroom Cacao Farm Suite.[13] The Cacao Farm House sleeps up to seven and sits at the bottom of the hill in the middle of the cacao groves.[15]

Every rate includes three daily meals, natural juices, coffee, teas and cacao, kayaks and binoculars, the hiking trails, the natural rock pools and a cacao and permaculture tour.[14] Cabins are open-air, with canopy mosquito nets, ceiling fans, hammocks and a private bathroom with a hot shower.[13] Coffee or hot chocolate and a freshly baked treat are delivered to your door each morning.[16]

La Loma Jungle Lodge Prices

La Loma does not publish a plain nightly rate list, so booking sites are the best guide. Hotels.com showed $359 to $390 for a mid-November night with taxes and fees included,[17] and Expedia listed 2026 stays from $536 with taxes and fees.[18] A Spanish booking site quoted from €325.38.[19] The lodge's guest handbook says the $10 per person marine park fee is included.[20]

The lodge's site has advertised a discount for September and October stays booked by late August, without a year attached. Check whether it is running before counting on it.

How to Get to La Loma

1

Reach Bocas Town 45 min flight

Fly from Panama City to Isla Colón, or take the overnight bus and boat. La Loma's own guide starts the trip at Albrook.

2

Be at Bocas Docks by 4 pm daily

The lodge's private boat leaves Bocas Docks in Bocas Town at 4 pm every day. If you miss it, a private late boat costs $60.

3

Cross the bay 15-20 min

The boat runs across the bay and through the mangroves to the lodge's bay-side entrance.

4

Climb to your cabin steps

Each cabin is up a series of jungle steps. A winch system carries bags up.

The included boat leaves daily at 4 pm, the late boat is $60, and the crossing takes about 20 minutes.[21] On departure day, the boat back to Isla Colón leaves at 9 am or 3 pm.[15]

Good to know · HILLS

La Loma sits on one of the highest hills in Bocas, just under 70 meters, and the lodge warns that some cabins and the main lodge involve steep walks that can be hazardous for children and older guests. Ask which cabin suits you if mobility is a concern.

Excursions such as snorkeling at the Zapatilla cays and visits to Red Frog Beach are also offered.[16] Our guide to snorkeling at Cayos Zapatillas covers what you see there. Monkeys are regulars on the farm: white-faced capuchins and night monkeys feed on cacao pods in the groves.[14] Bahía Honda is also one of the strongest spots in Bocas for bioluminescence, best on calm new-moon nights.[2]

Eating at La Loma Without Staying

Non-guests can book the dinner for $40 plus 7% tax per person, at least 24 hours ahead, with a minimum age of 8.[22] Dinner starts at 7 pm, and the boat is your problem: La Loma does not arrange it for outside diners, so plan a return around 9 pm with a Bocas water taxi.[22]

The lodge works with Bahía Honda, a bay community of about thirty Ngäbe-Buglé families. The scholarship group Education is Power has partnered with La Loma since 2011.[23] Guests can bring school supplies for children aged 3 to 13 through Pack for a Purpose.[23]

Mainland

Rambala Jungle Lodge: Hike-In and Off-Grid on the Mainland

Rambala Jungle Lodge is the choice for an off-grid rainforest lodge you walk into. It sits on 30 hectares of rainforest 3 km from the main road linking the Bocas del Toro archipelago with Boquete and David, and the lodge says it is reachable only on foot.[24] One travel agency describes access by foot or horseback from the road near Chiriquí Grande.[25] Go by the lodge's own wording and ask before you count on a horse for your bags.

What the Stay Includes and Costs

The all-inclusive package covers meals, snacks and drinks including alcohol, a bungalow, and access to the trails, waterfalls and natural pools. Activities include guided walks, horseback rides on jungle trails and night walks.[25] Rambala does not publish adult rates, and its agents say prices vary by season.[25] Children aged 5 to 12 are listed at $50 per person per night.[26]

Off-grid Setup and Climate

Electricity comes from micro-hydro turbines on the stream, showers use stream water, and drinking water comes from a forest spring, filtered mechanically and with ultraviolet light.[27] The stilt houses were built by hand by Ngäbe-Buglé builders from wood cut on the property, and a greenhouse, fruit trees and a henhouse supply the kitchen.[27] The lodge sits slightly above sea level and stays around 28-30°C by day and 23-25°C at night all year.[27]

Because it sits on the road between the highlands and the Bocas ferry, Rambala works as a break on the overland trip. Our guide to getting from Boquete to Bocas del Toro by shuttle or bus covers that road. No source gives a walking time for the 3 km, and trails in rainforest get muddy, so pack light and travel in daylight.

Pacific

Isla Palenque: The Luxury Eco Resort in the Gulf of Chiriquí

Isla Palenque is Panama's top-end eco resort: a 400-acre private island in the Gulf of Chiriquí with seven beaches and primary forest.[28] It belongs to the Cayuga Collection of sustainable hotels and has been named one of the best all-inclusive resorts for families by Travel & Leisure.[28]

Isla Palenque Rates

Published figures vary by source and season. Condé Nast Traveler puts low-season rates at about $980 per couple per night, including the boat from Boca Chica, meals and activities, but not alcohol.[4] Upgraded Points found a cash rate of $866 a night for a beachfront casita in June 2026 and $1,364 in March 2027.[29] The all-inclusive package applies when you book direct. Through an online travel agency it is added separately, and off-island excursions and spa treatments cost extra.[30]

Getting There and Wildlife Season

Most guests fly from Panama City to David, drive to the Boca Chica marina and take a short boat ride. One recent account describes a 90-minute drive and a 20-minute boat.[29] Other sources put the drive closer to an hour. The boat is included, but no source prices the land leg from David, so ask the resort when you book.

Wildlife activity picks up from May through November. Humpback whales arrive to calve from July, and dolphins are often seen offshore by mid-year.[28] That makes the green season the island's best months for marine life, the opposite of the usual dry-season logic.

Highlands

Canopy Lodge, El Valle de Antón: A Highland Birding Lodge

Canopy Lodge is the eco lodge for birders who want no boats and no island logistics. It sits beside a mountain stream in El Valle de Antón, a couple of hours west of Panama City, near the protected Cerro Gaital Natural Monument.[31] For the town itself, see our El Valle de Antón travel guide.

The lodge has 12 bedrooms with private bathrooms and either a king or two queen beds. Main-lodge rooms have balconies over the garden and stream.[31] The Observation Verandah, a few feet from the rooms, has feeders that draw motmots, tanagers, aracaris and euphonias, and there is Wi-Fi.[31] No current rate is published, so ask the lodge directly. Chorro El Macho waterfall and its swimming pool are close by.

Canal

Jungle Land: The Floating Lodge on the Panama Canal

Jungle Land is an all-inclusive floating lodge on the Panama Canal, about 30 km from Panama City.[32] Most people see it on a day tour: boats leave from a pier on Lake Gatún directly across from the Gamboa Rainforest Resort.[33]

The 6.5-hour tour runs Sundays, Mondays and Wednesdays with a 9:30 am pickup from Panama City hotels and the cruise terminal. It costs $115 per adult, $59 for children 6 to 12 and nothing for under-6s, and it includes a bilingual guide, the boat, kayaking, lunch and Monkey Island.[33] No current overnight rate is published, and the lodge's operating status has changed in the past, so confirm it is open before planning a night there. If the canal itself is the draw, our guide to Panama Canal boat tours compares partial and full transits.

Costs

Marine Park Fees and Other Costs on Top of the Lodge Rate

The main extra in Bocas is the Isla Bastimentos National Marine Park fee: $10 for foreign visitors and $4 for Panamanians arriving by sea, paid before you sail.[37] Payment is mandatory by law and collected by Ministry of Environment staff, and visits to protected areas are meant to be reserved at least 72 hours ahead.[37][38] Lodges and tour boats usually handle this. La Loma's handbook says its rate includes the fee.[20]

Good to know · ZAPATILLAS

Cayo Zapatilla 2 is open to visitors, while Cayo Zapatilla 1 has restricted visits during hawksbill turtle nesting. The park also closed briefly for bad weather and reopened in November 2025, so trips can be cancelled when the sea is rough.

The nesting restriction on Zapatilla 1 comes from the Ministry of Environment,[37] and the park reopened in November 2025 once the maritime authority resumed sailing permits.[39]

Water taxis add up if you leave the lodge on your own. Shared boats from Bocas Town to Old Bank on Bastimentos run about $2 to $3 per person as of 2026, though one 2025 fare list showed $5.[40] A water taxi to Red Frog Beach costs $8 to $10 riding alone or $3 to $4 per person on a full boat, plus a $3 to $5 trail fee, as of July 2026.[40] Missed transfers are the other trap: $60 for La Loma's late boat[21] and $107 for an off-schedule Tranquilo Bay pickup.[6]

Tranquilo Bay says it has removed nearly all single-use plastics, composts on site, recycles through local partners and supplies reef-safe sunscreen.[11] Its claim to protect over 200 acres of rainforest and reef, with a share of each booking going to conservation, is the lodge's own and gives no percentage.[11] La Loma suggests five things you can check at any lodge: how long staff have worked there, how much food is grown on site, where the energy comes from, how water and waste are handled, and how much money stays local.[2] Those questions are worth asking before you book.

Verdict

Which Panama Eco Lodge to Pick

Pick by what you want from the days, because the lodges are built around different things.

  • Wildlife and birding with everything arranged: Tranquilo Bay. It is the only lodge here with a published, all-inclusive 2026 rate and a guided program.
  • Food, chocolate and a farm stay: La Loma. It also has the most flexible transfer, a boat every day at 4 pm.
  • Off-grid rainforest without island logistics: Rambala, if you are happy to walk 3 km in.
  • Luxury, beaches and whales: Isla Palenque, best from July for humpbacks.
  • Birding close to Panama City: Canopy Lodge in El Valle, with no boats involved.
  • Only one free day in the city: the Jungle Land canal day tour.

If you want a pool, beach bar and kids' club more than trails, an eco lodge is the wrong fit. Our comparison of Panama's all-inclusive resorts covers those.

When to Go

Bocas del Toro stays warm and green all year, and its average temperature varies by only about 1.6°C across the seasons.[2] February and March are the busiest months in the archipelago.[2] Tranquilo Bay's lowest rate runs from May 1 to December 14, and Isla Palenque's wildlife season runs May to November. For a region-by-region view of rain and dry months, see our guide to the best time to visit Panama.

Children, Older Travelers and Limited Mobility

Hillside paths are the main issue. La Loma warns that its steep walks can be hazardous for children and older guests,[15] and Rambala starts with a 3 km walk. Isla Palenque has the strongest family credentials of the group. Rambala lists a children's rate, and La Loma's outside dinner has a minimum age of 8, so ask about age rules for overnight stays before booking.

Questions

Before you go

What is the best eco lodge in Panama?

It depends on the trip: Tranquilo Bay for guided wildlife and birding, La Loma for a chocolate-farm stay. Both are all-inclusive lodges on Isla Bastimentos in Bocas del Toro. Isla Palenque is the luxury pick on the Pacific, and Canopy Lodge in El Valle de Antón suits birders who want to skip boats.

How much does La Loma Jungle Lodge cost per night?

Booking sites showed roughly $359 to $536 a night in 2026, taxes included. La Loma does not publish a simple rate list, and prices vary by cabin and date. Three meals, drinks, the cacao tour, equipment and the boat from Bocas Town are included.

How do you get to La Loma Jungle Lodge?

Take the lodge's free private boat from Bocas Docks in Bocas Town at 4 pm. The crossing takes about 15 to 20 minutes. If you miss it, a late boat costs $60. Getting to Bocas Town itself is a short flight from Panama City or a long bus and boat trip.

How do you get to Tranquilo Bay Eco Adventure Lodge?

Fly from Panama City to Bocas, where the lodge meets the morning flight on Wednesdays and Saturdays. The boat ride takes roughly 25 to 45 minutes. Transfers on other days cost a $107 boat fee. Rates for 2026 run $306 to $366 per person per night, double occupancy.

How do you reach Rambala Jungle Lodge?

You walk in: the lodge is 3 km from the main road between Bocas del Toro and Boquete or David. The lodge says access is on foot only. One agency also mentions horseback, so confirm before you travel and pack light.

Does Panama have jungle you can stay in?

Yes. Isla Bastimentos in Bocas del Toro has no roads, and most of its interior is protected national marine park. Rambala sits in 30 hectares of mainland rainforest, and Isla Palenque has primary forest on a private island.

Which Bocas del Toro island is best for a jungle lodge?

Isla Bastimentos, home to both Tranquilo Bay and La Loma, is the least developed and wildest island. Isla Colón is better if you want restaurants and nightlife. Our guide to which Bocas del Toro island to stay on compares them.

Are Panama's eco lodges good for families and older travelers?

Some are, but check the terrain first. La Loma warns that its steep hillside paths can be hazardous for children and older guests, and Rambala requires a 3 km walk. Isla Palenque has been named one of the best all-inclusive resorts for families.

Is Canopy Lodge near Panama City?

It is about a couple of hours' drive west of Panama City, in El Valle de Antón. The lodge has 12 rooms and a bird-feeder verandah, and it is a highland option rather than a Caribbean one.

Are Panama's eco lodges safe?

The main risks are remoteness and terrain rather than crime. Plan around boat schedules, rough-sea cancellations and steep paths. Tranquilo Bay, for example, describes controls on food and water, insect protection and staff training. For the wider picture, see our guide to whether Panama is safe.
