Homes and land for sale in Pedasí, Playa Venao, Las Tablas and Santa Catalina, Panama: what each place is like, prices and how foreigners buy.

Pedasí homes for sale mostly ask between about $99,000 and $575,000 in late 2026. That range runs from new two-bedroom houses near the airstrip to three-bedroom homes in and around town, with gated building lots from about $61,500 and oceanfront estates at $2.5 million and up. Playa Venao, 34 km down the coast, is the pricier surf market, where most houses and villas ask $400,000 to $1 million, while Las Tablas and its beaches still have houses under $130,000.

This is a guide to buying a home or land on the Azuero coast, the quiet southeastern corner of Panama's Pacific side, and one of the cheaper places in the country to own near the beach. Pedasí is a small town known for sportfishing, with houses and building lots spread around its beaches. Playa Venao, farther down the coast, is a surf bay where much of the region's new building is going. Las Tablas and Chitré are the provincial towns with the hospitals, banks and supermarkets. Santa Catalina sits on the Veraguas coast west of the peninsula, but it draws the same surf-and-fishing buyers, so it is covered here too.

Every price below is an asking price from current broker and portal listings, not a sale price, and most listings carry no date. Read them as what sellers want in November 2026. The legal and tax figures are different: those are set by law and stated exactly.

Overview

Why Buy on the Azuero Coast, and Who It Suits

The Azuero coast suits buyers who want a low-rise Pacific coast with surf, fishing and inexpensive land, and who can live four and a half to five hours' drive from Panama City. Gated building lots near Pedasí ask roughly $41 to $55 per square meter, and a titled lot on the Pedasí–Playa Venao road works out to about $30 per square meter.[1][2] Serviced ocean-view land in Playa Venao costs two to three times that.

The trade-offs are real. Water, sewage and power are thin outside a few developments. Few finished homes come up for sale compared with land, so many buyers end up building. The coast also sits far from the capital's private hospitals. The upside is a market that still has $100,000 houses, real village life in Pedasí and Las Tablas, and year-round surf at Venao.

This coast is a poor fit if you need specialist care close by, plan to commute to the city every week, or want a deep pool of move-in-ready homes to choose from. It works best for people who will spend long stretches there, have a car, and are patient enough to build or renovate.

The Drive

The Drive from Panama City and the Flights to Azuero

Almost everyone reaches the Azuero coast by road, and the drive from Panama City to Pedasí takes about 4.5 to 5 hours.[3] Flights exist but are limited, so plan your life around the car.

By Road

The route follows the Interamericana west through La Chorrera, Penonomé and Aguadulce to Divisa, then turns south through Chitré or the Las Tablas corridor.[3] From Las Tablas, a paved two-lane road reaches Pedasí in about 45 minutes. The road on to Playa Venao and Cambutal is in good condition but poorly signed. Visitor surveys still rank roads among the weakest points of both Pedasí and Santa Catalina.[4]

For buyers weighing the mountains instead, Boquete is 6.5 to 7 hours from Panama City by car and Chitré about 4 hours, so the Azuero is the closer of the two retreats.[3] Most owners keep a car. Our guide to renting a car in Panama covers the insurance and deposit rules for the scouting trips before you buy one.

By Bus

Buses from Panama City reach Chitré in about 4.5 hours, and the connection through Las Tablas to Pedasí adds about 1.5 hours.[5] That works for scouting trips, less so for hauling building materials or groceries to a rural lot.

By Air

Air Panamá flies Panama City to Chitré in 30 minutes, with departures listed at 08:00 and 16:30, subject to change.[6] Its October 2026 route list does not include Pedasí.[7] A transport guide lists small-plane service from Albrook to Pedasí of about 45 minutes, with departures on Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, and tells travelers to confirm with the operator first.[3] Developers often quote a 35-minute flight followed by a 30-minute drive to Venao.[8]

Good to know · Old flight info

Older guides still mention Aeroperlas flights to Azuero. That airline stopped flying in February 2012, so any schedule naming it is out of date.

Pedasí

Pedasí Homes for Sale: Town Houses, Beach Builds and Gated Lots

Pedasí homes for sale mostly ask between about $99,000 and $575,000, and land outnumbers houses on the market.[9][10] One international portal shows 456 results for Pedasí, while a Panama-based portal lists just five houses for sale.[10][11] Most inventory is marketed to foreign buyers on international sites, often the same property shown several times by different agents.

At the bottom, a 47 m² one-bedroom house asks $48,000, and a 105 m² one-bedroom asks $185,000.[10] New two-bedroom homes near the airstrip are listed at $99,000.[9] A 230 m² three-bedroom asks $575,000, and seven-bedroom properties run from $1.8 million to $3.5 million.[10] The top listings are a four-bedroom house at $2.5 million and a seven-bedroom estate at $3.9 million.[12]

In Town

Pedasí town is small and walkable, and houses there come up less often than land. One local agency describes how its founders toured the beachfront developments, found them too isolated, and spent eight more trips over 12 months before buying a house just down from the square.[13] Few homes were for sale then, apart from some fixer-uppers, and land came with utilities or only "promises" of them.[13]

New builds fill some of the gap. Roy's Project offers Phase III three-bedroom, two-bath homes built in 2025 at $325,000, listed as titled and about a mile from both town and beach.[14]

Playa del Toro, Los Destiladeros and Punta Mala

South and east of town, the listings shift toward beach houses and bigger plots. An unfinished house on a 1,591 m² lot within walking distance of Playa del Toro asks $140,000 as is.[9] Casas Playa del Toro, still under construction, asks $450,000 for a house with a pool, 196.6 m² of construction and a 30 m² terrace on a titled 600 m² lot, a five-minute walk from the beach.[9] At Los Destiladeros, an oceanfront estate of about 23,334 m² has direct Pacific frontage.[9]

Income properties show up too. Villa del Sol pairs an owner's house with four rental apartments around a pool.[9] Sombra Verde, a 2,500 m² titled estate with three casitas, is marketed with an estimated 6% to 7% return, but the listing gives no basis for that figure.[9] Treat the 8% to 12% yearly appreciation figures in some expat marketing the same way: none comes with a dataset behind it.

Land and Lots Near Pedasí

Ungated land around Pedasí clusters around $30 to $35 per square meter. A titled 1,526.7 m² lot on the Pedasí–Playa Venao road asks $45,801, about $30 per m².[2] A 1,183 m² lot in Cañas asks $40,000, a 2,566 m² lot on the road to Punta Mala $88,000, and a 7,500 m² parcel at El Toro $240,000, all about $32 to $34 per m².[10] Large raw parcels go far lower: a development parcel of roughly 18 hectares on Carretera Nacional 2 asks $540,000, about $3 per m².[12]

The cheap per-meter figure is not the whole cost. A gated lot with security, roads and a well costs more per meter because someone has already paid for those things. On a rural lot, you pay for them yourself.

Playa Venao

Playa Venao Real Estate: Surf-Bay Prices and the Condo Boom

Playa Venao is the expensive end of the coast: the median house price tracked around the bay was about $672,500 in June 2026, and most houses and villas ask $400,000 to $1 million.[18] Beachfront and larger villas go above that.

The bay sits 34 km southwest of Pedasí and has surf year-round.[5][19] Its housing mixes beachfront and hillside homes, condos, duplexes and short-term rentals.[19] For the beach itself, the surf and the places to stay while you look around, see our guide to Playa Venao, Panama. Good to know · Asking vs sold

The Venao median is built from asking prices and market data, and closed sales can land lower. Ask your agent for recent sold prices on comparable homes, not just listings.

Houses and Villas

A two-bedroom, two-bath house of about 101 m² asks $600,000.[12] Villa Bella Venao, a two-bedroom main house plus a guest casita and pool on a 600 m² lot, with 170 m² built in total, asks $495,000.[20] A 101 m² two-bedroom apartment is listed at $435,000.[10]

Condos and Pre-Construction

Most new supply is pre-construction. Vervana Beach Condos, in Achotines Bay just past the main beach, sells two-bedroom lock-off units of 1,089 ft² (101 m²), and asking prices differ by listing.[21] One August 2026 market roundup puts them at around $505,000, while another listing starts them at $575,000 with delivery in September 2027.[22][21] Vervana's Phase II Templo condos and penthouses start at $450,000, and its villas at $625,000.[23] Owners can place units in a rental program run by Hamak Hotels.[8]

Blue Venao, a gated beachfront community, sells in phases: condos, ocean lofts and duplexes, surfer studios and a commercial plaza, with a Phase 6 announced.[24] It does not publish prices on its own site, so ask for the current price sheet by phase.

Know how much you pay before the keys. One published Vervana plan asks for a $10,000 reservation, then 10% at the promissory agreement, 10% at groundbreaking and 10% six months later, with the remaining 70% at the deed.[21] That is 30% at risk before completion. Your own lawyer should check the developer's title, permits and how deposits are protected.

Land Around the Bay

A 3,232 m² plot asks $335,000 and an 8,313 m² development plot $895,000, both about $104 to $108 per m².[10] Large raw parcels cost a small fraction of that per meter, while small serviced ocean-view lots cost the most.[18] Slope and distance to the beach move the price as much as size.

Building Rules Are Tightening

In 2024, the Environment Ministry (MiAMBIENTE) ordered an immediate halt to five construction projects in Playa Venao for building without an approved environmental impact study.[25] The tourism authority's planning work for Pedasí includes a partial plan for Playa Venao–Achotines that would set minimum lot sizes, heights and lot-by-lot land-use rules, and protect beach access.[26] The local turtle foundation, which reported more than 25,000 turtles released in 2022, has asked for construction controls on nesting beaches.[26] Expect stricter rules, not looser ones, and check what a lot allows before you buy it.

Good to know · "Residency in 30 days"

Some Venao listings promise investor residency in 30 days. Under the rules in force since 16 September 2026, the trade ministry certifies the investment within 15 business days and immigration then has 30 days to decide, so plan on longer.

Las Tablas

Las Tablas and Chitré Real Estate: Lower Prices, More Services

Las Tablas is the cheapest place on this coast to buy a house: a three-bedroom in town asks $95,000, and beach houses at El Rompío ask $125,000 to $225,000.[27] You give up the surf-town feel for a working provincial town with shops, services and a hospital.

Las Tablas is one of the main urban centers of Los Santos province, mixing commerce, residential neighborhoods and strong local traditions, with beaches a short drive away.[28] One portal shows 113 house results for the area.[27] At Playa El Estero, a four-bedroom house asks $249,000.[27] Small beach lots at El Rompío ask $45,000 for about 520 m² and $55,000 for about 630 m², roughly $87 per m².[12] That is more per meter than rural land near Pedasí, because small lots near a beach always cost more per meter.

Nearby Towns

The surrounding towns fill the middle of the market. A three-bedroom house in Pocrí asks $195,000, a three-bedroom in La Villa de Los Santos $250,000, and a four-bedroom in Tonosí district $300,000.[12] Houses in Cañas range from $275,000 to $995,000.[12]

Renting First

Long-term rentals are hard to find online: one portal showed just two rental listings in Las Tablas.[28] Renting for a few months before buying is still the best way to test a town, but expect to find rentals through local agents and word of mouth rather than portals.

Chitré

Chitré is the Azuero's working city rather than a beach market, about 4 hours from Panama City by car and the usual gateway to Las Tablas, Pedasí and Playa Venao.[3] It has the region's scheduled flights, 30 minutes to Panama City.[6] It gets almost none of the foreign-buyer marketing aimed at Pedasí and Venao. That makes it a choice for people who want city services during the week and the beach on weekends, rather than a place to buy a beach house.

Santa Catalina

Santa Catalina Real Estate: Surf and Coiba, Farther Out

Santa Catalina real estate runs from about $80,000 for a lot near town to $500,000 for oceanfront land, with finished homes asking roughly $430,000 to $610,000 in 2026.[29][30] It is a separate market in Veraguas province, not a short drive from Pedasí, and it does not share Las Tablas and Chitré as service towns.

Residential lots of around 1,000 m² near town were commonly listed at $80,000 to $130,000 in 2026, with oceanfront parcels near the point at $290,000 to $500,000.[29] Actual listings sit toward the top of that. A 450 m² commercial lot five minutes' walk from the beach asks $45,000, and a 2,743 m² oceanfront lot at La Punta $388,000.[31] A 3,000 m² ocean-view lot in Santa Catalina Villas asks $250,000.[32]

A two-bedroom ocean-view home on 1,000 m² in Altos de Santa Catalina asks $430,000.[31] In Santa Catalina Villas, Casa Maya asks $560,000, and Casa Brava Ventura, a colonial-style house on 5,000 m² with ocean views, $610,000.[30] For small businesses, a 50 m² income-producing eco cabin asks $180,000, and a beachfront eco-lodge at Playa La Coquita $1.4 million.[32][30]

Two cautions apply. The market mixes titled land and rights-of-possession land, so title checks matter even more here.[29] Roads and transport are among visitors' worst-rated parts of the destination.[4] The draw is the surf and the water: Santa Catalina is the jumping-off point for Coiba National Park, and our guide to diving Coiba National Park from Santa Catalina covers trips, seasons and prices.

Practicalities

Beachfront Rules, Water, Sewage and Hospitals

On this coast, the first 22 meters above the Pacific high-tide line are public beach and cannot be titled, and most lots outside gated developments need their own water and septic system.[33] Check both before you fall for a view.

What the Coastal Law Means for a Beach Lot

Ribera de playa (beach strip)
The strip from the high-tide line to 22 meters inland on the Pacific side. Public, never titled.
Zona costera (coastal zone)
The 200-meter strip measured inland from the high-tide line, set by Law 80 of 2009.
Zona costera adjudicable
The rest of that strip on the Pacific side, the 178 meters behind the beach strip, which the law treats as land that can be titled.
Concession
A permit to use public coastal land. Temporary, revocable, and it does not make you the owner.

Law 80 of 2009 also excludes beaches, rivers, estuaries, marine-coastal ecosystems and ecological reserves from titling.[33] Some broker material describes the 178-meter strip as concession-only land; the law text treats it as land that can be titled, so have your lawyer confirm the status of each lot. Concessions in the maritime zone confer no ownership and can be revoked.[34] Buyers should check where the 22-meter strip falls on the survey of any lot near the water.[35] Our guide to beachfront and land in Panama: what foreigners can own covers titled vs ROP land, islands and the public beach strip in more depth.

Water, Sewage and Power

Basic infrastructure is the coast's weak spot. Panama's tourism master plan reports water shortfalls, no sewerage system and power failures in peak seasons at Playa Venao, Cambutal, Playa El Toro and Pedasí town. Local groups at Venao have raised the lack of public sewerage in the official planning consultation too.[26] A septic system costs about $3,000 to $8,000 in areas without municipal sewage.[36]

This is why developments advertise their own solutions: Costa Pedasí runs a private well system, and Piedra Viva pairs a private water supply with solar power.[15][9] Authorities plan more frequent inspections of water-use concessions and construction in Playa Venao.[37] If a lot relies on a well, ask whether it has a water-use concession. Brokers market fiber internet across the area, but confirm a provider covers the exact address before you buy.

Environmental Permits

Any project that may alter the environment needs an approved environmental impact study under Executive Decree 1 of 2023, as amended by Executive Decree 2 of 2024.[38] For a buyer, that means checking that the developer had one, and budgeting time for your own if you build on raw land.

Hospitals and Clinics

Pedasí has a public health center, MINSA-CAPSI de Pedasí, and the province's hospitals are farther north: Joaquín Pablo Franco Sayas in Las Tablas, the rural hospital of Tonosí, and the regional Anita Moreno hospital.[39] Anita Moreno, in La Villa de Los Santos, has a blood bank, an 84-slice CT scanner, digital fluoroscopy and conventional X-ray.[40][41] From Pedasí, Las Tablas is about 45 minutes by road. For private hospitals and insurance, see our guide to healthcare in Panama for foreigners.

Costs

What Buying on the Azuero Coast Costs: Closing Fees and Property Tax

Beyond the price, a buyer usually pays around 1% to 1.5% in legal and closing costs. The seller normally pays the agent's commission, the 2% transfer tax and the capital gains advance. The bigger ongoing cost is property tax, which depends on whether the home is registered as your primary residence.

Transfer Tax and Capital Gains

The transfer tax (ITBI) is 2% of the higher of the price and the cadastral value, paid by the seller. Law 546 of 2026 added an exemption that has applied since 1 September 2026, so ask your lawyer whether the sale qualifies. The seller also owes 10% capital gains tax on the gain, with an advance of 3% of the price paid before registration, or 3.75% for developers. Because registration depends on those payments, your lawyer should confirm they have been made.

Annual Property Tax

Property tax runs from 0% to 1% of the cadastral value under Law 66 of 2017, with lower rates for a registered primary residence.[42] The rates apply in slices, like income tax brackets.[43]

A $95,000 house in Las Tablas registered as your primary residence pays nothing. The same house under the general rate pays about $390 a year. On a $325,000 home, the primary-residence tax is about $1,025 and the general rate about $1,920.

The tax can be paid in three installments, due 30 April, 31 August and 31 December.[42] Paying the full year before 30 April with no arrears earns a 10% discount under Law 208 of 2021, article 9.[42] One DGI FAQ page still gives 29 February as the deadline, so check the date with the DGI each year.[44] Primary-residence status needs an application, which takes around six months to process, so your first bill may come at the general rate.[44]

Rental Income

Panama taxes only Panamanian-source income, and rent from a property in Panama counts as Panamanian-source income. If you rent out a Venao condo or a Pedasí casita, that income is taxable in Panama even if you live abroad. You become a tax resident after 183 days in the country.

Buying

How to Buy Safely: Title, Rights of Possession and Residency

Buy titled land with a finca number in the Registro Público, have your own lawyer check it before you pay a deposit, and treat rights-of-possession land as a different, riskier product. Foreigners can own titled property on the same terms as Panamanians.[18] Our guide to buying property in Panama as a foreigner walks through the process nationwide. What follows is what matters most on this coast.

Titled Land vs Rights of Possession

Rural Los Santos has land held under rights of possession (derechos posesorios, or ROP) alongside titled parcels in the Public Registry.[45] If the seller has no registered finca and offers only an improvements contract, a local magistrate's certificate or an ANATI certificate, what you are buying is a right of possession, not a title.[34] Banks do not accept ROP land as collateral, and titling it through ANATI can take many months to several years.[46]

The delays are real. In 2019, ANATI handed titles to about 400 families in the Los Santos, Guararé, Tonosí, Las Tablas and Pedasí districts, and some had waited around 15 years.[47] Nationwide, only 35% of the territory has registered cadastral information.[48] Don't pay in full for ROP land until titling is complete or the money sits in a guarantee trust.[34]

1

Get the finca number and a fresh title certificate Before any deposit

Ask the seller for the finca number and a Registro Público certificate no older than 30 days. Anyone can request one for a fee, so your lawyer can pull it independently.

2

Check the cadastre and the survey 1-2 weeks

Compare the registered area and boundaries with ANATI's cadastral records and the approved plan. ANATI has a procedure for correcting titles with measurement errors, which involves a new survey and notice to the neighbors.

3

Check the coast, water and permits Same time

Confirm where the 22-meter public strip falls, whether there is water and power or a plan for them, and, for new projects, the environmental impact study and building approvals.

4

Sign the promissory agreement Deposit stage

The agreement sets the price, the deposit and the conditions. For pre-construction, it also sets the payment stages, so check how deposits are protected until the deed.

5

Sign the deed and register it Closing

The deed is signed before a notary, the seller's taxes are paid, and the deed is registered in your name in the Registro Público. The purchase is complete only at registration.

The 30-day certificate rule comes from local brokers' standard practice.[34] ANATI offers an online status check for its procedures and an informational cadastral certificate, and it has a regional office in Los Santos.[49][50] Its rules for correcting titles with measurement errors date from December 2025.[51] Hire your own lawyer, not one arranged by the developer, even when a developer's tour includes an attorney meeting.

Good to know · Promises of utilities

A lot sold with "utilities coming" has no utilities. Get the water and power plan in writing, or price the well, septic and solar into your budget.

Can Buying Here Get You Residency?

Property can support residency, but the thresholds changed on 16 September 2026, and most resale homes on this coast fall short of the investor route. Under Executive Decree 17 of 2026, the Qualified Investor visa requires $300,000 in a new, first-sale property, or $500,000 in a resale property. Only the lower of the price paid and the market value counts, minus any mortgage. The money must come from abroad, and you must keep the investment for five years. Rights-of-possession land does not qualify.

The investor route costs $5,000 to the National Treasury and $5,000 to immigration for the main applicant, plus $1,000 and $1,000 per dependent. Applications filed before 16 September 2026 follow the old rules, and contracts signed before then can use them if the application is filed by March 2027. A new Venao condo bought from the developer above $300,000 can qualify. A $450,000 resale house in Pedasí cannot.

Two other routes suit this coast. The Friendly Nations visa, for citizens of 51 countries, accepts a $200,000 property. The Pensionado visa needs a lifetime pension of $1,000 a month, or $750 a month with a $100,000 property. Savings, IRAs and 401(k)s don't count. Our comparison of Panama residency visas sets out who qualifies for each, and our guide to retiring in Panama covers the Pensionado discounts and monthly budgets.

Questions

Before you go

How much does a house in Pedasí cost in 2026?

Most Pedasí houses ask between about $99,000 and $575,000. The cheapest listing is a 47 m² one-bedroom at $48,000, a new three-bedroom near town asks $325,000, and oceanfront estates run from $2.5 million to $3.9 million. These are asking prices, so closed sales can land lower.

How much does land cost in Pedasí?

Ungated land near Pedasí asks roughly $30 to $35 per square meter. Gated lots in Costa Pedasí work out to about $41 to $55 per square meter, with $160 a month in HOA dues. Large raw parcels sell for a few dollars per square meter, and serviced ocean-view plots in Playa Venao cost around $105.

How much does a house in Playa Venao cost?

Most Playa Venao houses and villas ask $400,000 to $1 million, with a median of about $672,500 in June 2026. New condos start around $435,000 to $450,000, and beachfront villas go above $1 million.

How long is the drive from Panama City to Pedasí and Playa Venao?

Pedasí is about 4.5 to 5 hours from Panama City by car, via the Interamericana to Divisa and then south through Chitré or Las Tablas. Playa Venao is 34 km farther, around half an hour more. Flights to Chitré take 30 minutes, and small-plane service to Pedasí runs on a limited schedule.

Is Las Tablas cheaper than Pedasí?

Yes, for houses. Las Tablas has three-bedroom houses from about $95,000 and beach houses at El Rompío from $125,000, while most Pedasí houses start near $99,000 and climb faster. Small beach lots near Las Tablas cost more per square meter than rural land near Pedasí, though.

Can a foreigner buy beachfront property in Panama?

Yes, foreigners can own titled beachfront property on the same terms as Panamanians, but the first 22 meters above the Pacific high-tide line are public and can never be titled. Concessions on coastal land are temporary and revocable and do not make you the owner, so have a lawyer check each lot's survey.

What is a rights-of-possession property, and is it safe to buy?

A rights-of-possession (ROP) property is land without a registered title, held through contracts, local certificates or an ANATI certificate. It carries more risk: banks won't lend against it, it does not qualify for investor residency, and titling can take years. Don't pay in full until it is titled or the money is held in a guarantee trust.

Who pays closing costs and the real estate commission in Panama?

The seller normally pays the commission, the 2% transfer tax and the 3% capital gains advance. The buyer usually pays around 1% to 1.5% in legal and closing costs. Confirm the split in writing in the promissory agreement.

Can buying property in Pedasí or Playa Venao get me residency?

Yes, if the property meets a visa's threshold. The Qualified Investor visa needs $300,000 in a new, first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale property, and the Friendly Nations visa accepts a $200,000 property. Retirees can use the Pensionado visa with a $750 monthly pension plus a $100,000 property.

Is there a hospital near Pedasí?

Pedasí has a public health center, MINSA-CAPSI de Pedasí, and the nearest hospitals are in Las Tablas and La Villa de Los Santos. The regional Anita Moreno hospital has a blood bank and a CT scanner, and Las Tablas is about 45 minutes from Pedasí by road.
