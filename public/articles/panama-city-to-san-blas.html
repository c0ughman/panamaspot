Panama City to San Blas: 4×4, Boat or Flight, and What Each Costs

How to get from Panama City to the San Blas Islands: 4×4 and boat via Cartí, flights and sailboats, with times, costs and fees.

The standard way from Panama City to the San Blas Islands is a shared 4×4 to the port of Cartí (about 2.5 to 3 hours), then a Guna boat to your island (30 to 60 minutes), for around $150 per person round trip plus a $22 cash entry fee.[1] The only faster option is a small private plane from Albrook airport to a Guna Yala airstrip, which takes about 40 minutes and costs from roughly $180 per person each way. No ferry or public bus goes all the way.

San Blas is the name most visitors use for Guna Yala, an autonomous comarca on Panama's Caribbean coast. It was created in 1938, has about 365 islands and is governed by the Congreso General Guna.[2][3] The Guna run the checkpoint on the only road in, the boats from the piers and the entry fee, so every route from Panama City to the San Blas Islands ends in Guna hands.

This page covers getting there and what each route costs. For choosing islands, what a stay is like and the rest of the planning, see the complete San Blas Islands (Guna Yala) guide.

Compare

Panama City to San Blas Islands: Every Option at a Glance

Most travelers choose between two routes. Almost everyone takes the road and a boat. The alternative is a charter flight, which costs roughly two to five times more and saves three hours or more each way. Sailing in is a separate, multi-day trip, usually from Colombia.

All the road options use the same route: the Pan-American Highway east, the mountain road at El Llano and the piers at Cartí. What changes is whether the boat, lunch and island stops are bundled in. That is why the quoted prices look so far apart.

Distance

How Far Is San Blas from Panama City?

Allow about 4 to 5 hours from your Panama City hotel to the island or boat by road, or under an hour by plane plus a short boat ride.[4] The distance in kilometers matters less than the hours, because the last stretch is a steep, curving mountain road.

The drive has two parts. First comes about 1.5 hours east on the Pan-American Highway to the El Llano junction near Chepo.[5] Then comes the El Llano–Puerto Cartí road over the mountains, which Panama's public works ministry (MOP) puts at 38.7 km.[6] Another MOP release on the same project gives about 37.8 km.[7]

Most operators quote 2.5 to 3 hours for the whole drive to Cartí.[8][9] Lonely Planet gives two hours, and some transfer companies allow 3 to 3.5 hours.[10][1] The longer "4 hours each way" figure you may see is also accurate. It counts the pickup loop around city hotels, the checkpoint, the wait at the pier and the boat ride. One catamaran operator puts hotel pickup to boarding at over four hours.[11]

Good to know · Wrong San Blas

If a result talks about US-98 and an hour's drive, it describes Cape San Blas near Panama City, Florida. Panama's San Blas Islands are on the Caribbean coast east of Panama City, Panama.

By Road

4×4 and Boat via Cartí: How the Road Route Works

The road route starts with a pre-dawn 4×4 pickup at your hotel. You drive east, then north over the mountains to the piers at Cartí, and a Guna-run motorboat takes you out to the islands. Shared vehicles usually carry up to six passengers.[12]

1

Hotel pickup 5:00-5:30 am

Shared 4×4s collect guests from Panama City hotels between about 5:00 and 5:30 am.[13] Some transfer companies come a little later, between 5:30 and 6:00 am.[1] Some tours stop at a market on the way out of the city so you can buy water and snacks, which cost more on the islands.[12]

2

Pan-American Highway to El Llano about 1 h 30

The easy part is a highway run east past Tocumen airport and Chepo to the El Llano junction, usually with a short snack stop.[5]

3

Mountain road and the Nusagandi checkpoint 1-2 h

The road climbs through forest with many tight curves. The checkpoint at Nusagandi, around km 18, is run by the Congreso General Guna and Panama's border police (SENAFRONT). There you show your passport and pay the entry fee.[14] Expect no phone signal or internet during the mountain crossing.[8]

4

The Cartí piers around 8:30 am

Day trips typically reach the port between about 8:25 and 8:45 am.[15] Cartí has three piers: Barsukun, Tupile and the old airport pier.[8] The restrooms at the dock are your last chance before the island stops.[16]

5

Boat to your island 30-60 min

The crossing to the nearest islands takes around 30 to 40 minutes.[5] Outer cays and anchored catamarans are about 40 to 50 minutes out, and longer in rough seas.[17] Spray is unavoidable, so keep your passport, phone and cash in a waterproof bag.[1]

What the Road Transfer Costs

The most common quote is about $150 per person round trip for the 4×4 and the water taxi together.[1][4] Prices for the 4×4 leg alone vary widely, mainly because some quotes are one way and others round trip. Shared 4×4 seats are quoted at $30-35 per person one way in current 2026–27 price lists.[9] One catamaran operator charges $75 per person each way.[17] An Albrook charter company listed a shared seat at $80 round trip and a private car for up to six at $480 round trip, as of 2024.[8]

The boat leg depends on how far out you are going. A one-way transfer from Cartí to an island runs about $25-30 per person in cash, as of July 2026.[18] Round trips run $40-60 per person depending on distance.[1] Rates climb for the distant cays.[19] Before you pay, ask three things: is the boat included, is the price one way or round trip, and are the Guna fees included. Most island cabins and sailing boats arrange their own transfer. If you have not picked a base yet, the guide to where to stay in San Blas compares island cabins, lodges and sailboats.

Day Trip or a Transfer to an Overnight Stay

A day trip bundles the 4×4, boat, lunch and two or three islands. Sea San Blas, a Guna-owned operator, lists day trips at $125-140 per person depending on the island.[13] Atlas & Boots reports tours from $100.[5] At the top end, one Panama City agency charges $225 plus 17% tax, or $180 for children aged 3 to 8.[12] The entry fee is almost always extra.

Day trips are long. Return transfers usually reach Panama City between 6 and 7 pm, and sometimes as late as 7:30 or 8 pm.[12][15] You also only reach the islands closest to Cartí. Shared vehicles and boats are not wheelchair accessible, and operators advise against the trip during pregnancy or with back or hip problems.[15] Our comparison of San Blas tours from Panama City sets day trips against one- and two-night packages, with prices.

Do not plan a same-evening flight home after a day trip. Operators recommend sleeping in Panama City and flying the next day.[15] If you still need a base, the guide to where to stay in Panama City covers the areas where pickups are easiest.

Driving Yourself in a 4×4

You can drive to Cartí yourself, but only in a 4×4 or a high-clearance SUV.[8] A regular rental car will not be allowed past the checkpoint.[17] The comarca's 2017 land transport regulation names Nusagandi as the control point for vehicles and people.[20] The vehicle rule itself is a long-standing checkpoint practice rather than a published clause, so do not try your luck with a sedan.

From the city, take the Corredor Sur past Tocumen airport to its end. Continue through La 24 de Diciembre, Pacora and Chepo, then turn left onto the San Blas road about 10 minutes after the A/B gas station.[8] There was a $25 charge for bringing your own vehicle, plus the usual per-person entry fee, as of 2024.[8] The road was fully paved except near the piers.[8] In July 2026, MOP announced planned maintenance on the El Llano–Cartí road without giving dates.[21]

Driving only saves money if you have also arranged parking at the port and a speedboat.[17] Water taxis leave Cartí on a scheduled 8 am departure and should be booked ahead. If you turn up unannounced, you may wait for the next rotation.[1] For insurance and deposit rules on 4×4 rentals, see our guide to renting a car in Panama.

Is There a Bus to San Blas?

Not all the way. Buses from the Albrook terminal can get you as far as the El Llano junction.[22] No public transport runs on the mountain road to Cartí, though, so you still need a pre-arranged 4×4 pickup from El Llano. Travelers who reach the checkpoint without a booked boat or stay can end up stuck, so book the onward legs before you leave the city.

Good to know · Motion sickness

The last section of road has many curves, and plenty of passengers feel queasy on it. If you are prone to motion sickness, take your tablets before the 4×4 leaves the highway, not halfway up the hill.[17]

By Air

Flights from Panama City to San Blas: Charters, Airstrips and Prices

Flying to San Blas means a small plane from Albrook airport (PAC) to a Guna Yala airstrip, about 40 minutes in the air.[17] In practice, expect a private charter rather than a scheduled airline ticket.

Is There a Scheduled Flight to San Blas?

Sources disagree, and the airline itself does not settle it. Some airline-information sites describe daily Air Panama flights to Guna Yala for $50-150.[23] Charter and catamaran operators say every flight to San Blas is private.[11][17] Air Panama's own Puerto Obaldía page offers a charter booking but no timetable or fare.[24] Its fares page sets out fare rules but no route prices for Guna Yala.[25]

The practical rule is simple. Treat the flight as a charter unless Air Panama's site sells you a seat for your exact date. If you search for a seat, look for the airstrip's name or code, because searching for "San Blas" on booking sites does not work.[23]

Which Airstrip to Fly into

Six airstrips serve the comarca: Achutupo (ACU), Corazón de Jesús (CZJ), El Porvenir (PVE), Malatupo (MPP), Playón Chico (PYC) and Puerto Obaldía (PUE).[23] Which one you need depends on where you are staying.

  • Corazón de Jesús is where most charters land for catamaran guests, followed by a short dinghy ride to the boat.[17]
  • El Porvenir (Gaigirgordub) is the comarca's de facto capital, with government offices and a small airfield.[5] Wailidup Lodge in the Chichime cays is 15 minutes away by boat.
  • Playón Chico is far east of Cartí. Yandup Lodge there is reachable only by charter flight and costs from $165 per person per night, full board, as of 2024.[8]

What a Charter Flight Costs

Published charter fares start at $180 per person each way.[17] Another operator charges $400 per guest for its light-aircraft transfer.[4] Whole-plane prices depend on the date and how many seats you fill, and they are significant.[1] Flight times are quoted at 30 to 45 minutes.[1][4]

Flying in has its own entry charge: $20 per person at all airstrips except Playón Chico, where it was $10, as of 2024.[8]

Albrook or Tocumen, and Timing the Return

Charter flights normally leave from Albrook (PAC), Panama City's domestic airport near the center, not from Tocumen (PTY).[4] If you land at Tocumen, some private operators can send a plane to meet you there, so you avoid the cross-city transfer.[11] On the way home, book international departures for noon or later.[11]

Luggage and Checking the Operator

Light aircraft limit luggage to about 12-15 kg per person, so pack a soft bag.[4] If you do buy a regular Air Panama ticket, an extra checked piece up to 18 kg costs $35 plus tax, and excess weight is $5 per kilo up to 25 kg.[26]

Panama's civil aviation authority (AAC) has warned that only aircraft holding its operating certificates may sell air-taxi flights from Albrook. Uncertified operators face fines of $1,000 to $50,000.[27] Before you pay a deposit, ask the seller which company holds the operating certificate for your flight.

By Sea

Arriving in San Blas by Sailboat

You cannot sail to San Blas from Panama City itself. The city is on the Pacific and there is no ferry to the Caribbean islands.[17] Arriving by boat means coming from Colombia, leaving from Puerto Lindo on the Caribbean coast, or joining a charter that collects you at Cartí or an airstrip.

From Colombia

The Panama–Colombia sailing route takes 4 to 5 nights one way.[9] Speedboat island-hopping trips between the two countries run 3 days and 2 nights, from $350.[5] Some sailboats use El Porvenir as their immigration checkpoint.[9] Boats, prices and how to choose a safe captain are covered in the guide to sailing from Panama to Colombia through San Blas.

From Puerto Lindo

Puerto Lindo is a small town on the Caribbean coast where many sailboats to and from Colombia start or finish. You reach it by road and board directly, without the usual Cartí speedboat.[9] A shared shuttle from Panama City costs about $25 per person one way, and a public bus from the Albrook terminal about $8-10.[9] Buses to Puerto Lindo run less often than those to nearby Portobelo, so most sailors take the shuttle their boat arranges.

Charter Boats That Start in San Blas

Most private and shared charters begin in the islands. You still travel out by 4×4 and boat or by plane, and the crew meets you. Some charter prices include the road transfer and some quote it separately, so check. On the last day, boats usually run guests back to the mainland at 7 am or 3 pm.[4] The morning boat puts you back in Panama City around 11:30 am, and the afternoon one around 7:30 pm.[1]

Fees

Guna Yala Fees, Passport and Visitor Rules

Foreign visitors pay a Guna Yala entry charge of $22 per person in cash. Residents of Panama pay $7, and children under 10 are exempt, as of 2026.[15] You also need your original passport.

The Entry Fee, and Why Figures Differ

The $22 is usually explained as a $20 territorial entry tax plus a $2 Cartí port-use fee. Residents pay $5 plus $2.[29] Operators do not all agree on the details. One charges $23 from age 6, including the port fee.[12] An island lodge lists a $3 dock fee, for totals of $23 and $8.[14] Bring a few dollars more than $22 per person in small bills.

The tax is paid in cash to comarca officials at the Nusagandi checkpoint, and agency packages generally do not include it.[29] One operator says its drivers also take cards.[30] Another says there are no card machines at the checkpoint.[4] Cash is the safe assumption.

The charge rests on Guna law. Article 59 of the 2013 Ley Fundamental de Gunayala requires lucrative activity in the territory to contribute to the Congreso General.[31] The Panamanian state has not formally approved that law, but Guna authorities apply it in practice.[32]

Island and Flight-Route Fees

Many islands charge a small landing or beach fee of about $2-5 per person.[19][33] Tours often include these, so ask before you go. Visitors arriving by plane pay $20 at most airstrips and $10 at Playón Chico, as of 2024.[8]

Your Passport

Carry your original passport. Photocopies are not accepted, and you can be refused entry without it.[12] Panamanians and residents show their cédula instead.[29] This applies even on a day trip that starts and ends in Panama City.

Rules at the Checkpoint and on the Islands

Drones, jet skis and kites
Not permitted, according to the comarca's tourism regulation as reproduced by a local operator. Breaking the rules can bring fines or confiscation.[34]
Surfboards and spearguns
Surfing, kitesurfing and harpoon guns are banned to protect the coral, and surfboards are turned back at the border.[1]
Scuba tanks
Diving with oxygen tanks is not allowed. Snorkeling is fine.[34][10]
Photos
Ask before photographing anyone.[35] The congress house and ceremonies need express permission from local authorities.[34]
Dress
Swimwear in village streets and nudity on beaches are banned.[34]
Coconuts and starfish
Coconuts belong to someone and are a key source of income, so ask before you take one.[5] Never lift a starfish out of the water.

Good to know · Cash

There are no ATMs on the islands, and cards are close to useless.[1] Bring the entry fee, island fees and money for drinks, which run about $1.50-2 each, in small bills.[12]

Before You Book

What to Book, When to Go and What Can Go Wrong

Book the road transfer and the boat before you leave the city, and choose your season with the sea in mind. December to April brings trade winds and slightly rougher seas, while May to November is calmer but more humid.[16]

Checking an Operator

Panama's tourism authority (ATP) publishes a downloadable register of licensed travel agencies, last updated on 2 October 2026.[36] Nobody may present themselves as a tour guide in Panama without ATP certification.[37] Ask a tour seller for its registration before you pay a deposit. A common arrangement is a deposit at booking with the balance paid on the day, and some operators allow free cancellation up to 72 hours ahead.[30]

Weather and Cancellations

Operators cancel for safety but generally do not refund because the weather is not what you hoped for.[15] Rainy-season months average around 200 mm of rain, usually in short downpours, and the seas can be choppier.[5] December has the most tour departures from Panama City.[38] Some boats add 20% on Panamanian public holidays, and Christmas, New Year and Easter dates sell out first.[4] For month-by-month conditions across the country, see the guide to the best time to visit Panama.

Children and Luggage on the Road

Children aged 0 to 4 ride on an adult's lap in the shared vehicles.[15] At least one day tour is not recommended for infants.[12] Soft, waterproof bags fit the 4×4s and boats far better than hard suitcases.[16] Leave valuables in the city, because secure storage is scarce once you are on the islands.

Good to know · Boat times

If you arrange the boat yourself, the 8 am water taxi from Cartí is the one to book. Missing it can mean waiting at the pier for the next run.[1]

Questions

Before you go

How far is San Blas from Panama City?

About 4 to 5 hours door to island by road. The 4×4 drive to the Cartí piers takes 2.5 to 3 hours, and the boat to the islands another 30 to 60 minutes. The mountain section from the El Llano junction to Cartí is about 38.7 km. By charter plane from Albrook, the flight takes 30 to 45 minutes.

Can you do a day trip to San Blas from Panama City?

Yes, and it is the most common way people visit. Pickup is around 5:00 to 5:30 am, and you are back in Panama City between about 6 and 8 pm. Day tours cost roughly $100 to $225 per person plus the $22 entry fee. Our comparison of San Blas tours from Panama City sets day trips against overnight stays.

Are there flights from Panama City to San Blas?

Yes, small planes fly from Albrook airport in about 40 minutes, but plan on a private charter. Air Panama does not publish a Guna Yala timetable or fares, and its own site offers charter bookings. Charter seats cost roughly $180 to $400 per person each way, and the plane lands at an airstrip such as Corazón de Jesús or El Porvenir.

Is there a ferry from Panama City to San Blas?

No. Panama City is on the Pacific and San Blas is on the Caribbean, and no ferry links them. You go by 4×4 and boat via Cartí, by small plane, or by sailboat from Colombia or Puerto Lindo.

Can I drive my own car to San Blas?

Only in a 4×4 or a high-clearance SUV. Regular cars are turned back at the Guna checkpoint. In 2024 there was a $25 vehicle charge on top of the per-person fee, and you also need parking at Cartí and a water taxi booked in advance.

How much is the Guna Yala entry fee?

$22 per person for foreign visitors, $7 for Panama residents, and free for children under 10, as of 2026. It is usually paid in cash at the Nusagandi checkpoint and is rarely included in tour prices. Some operators quote $23 because they count the Cartí port fee differently.

Do I need my passport to go to San Blas?

Yes, your original passport. Officials check it at the Guna Yala checkpoint, photocopies are not accepted, and you can be refused entry without it. Panamanians and residents show their cédula.

How long is the boat ride to the San Blas Islands?

About 30 to 60 minutes from the Cartí piers. The nearest islands are 30 to 40 minutes out, and the outer cays and anchored catamarans take longer, especially in rough seas. Expect spray and pack electronics in a dry bag.

Can I get to San Blas without a tour?

Yes, by booking a transfer through your island cabin, lodge or sailboat. Buses from Albrook only reach the El Llano junction, and nothing public runs on the mountain road. Arrange the 4×4 and the boat before you leave the city so you do not end up stuck at the checkpoint or the pier.

What is the best time to go to San Blas?

December to April is drier and sunnier but windier, with rougher seas. May to November is calmer and more humid, with short rain showers. December is the busiest month for departures, and holiday dates sell out first. See the guide to the best time to visit Panama for month-by-month conditions.
