Coronado, Panama houses for sale mostly ask $250,000 to $500,000 for a mid-range home, with most three-bedroom listings between about $310,000 and $390,000. Condos start around $60,000 for a studio and reach about $300,000 for a two-bedroom ocean-view unit, and beachfront estates go past $1 million, as of November 2026. Coronado sits 80 to 85 km west of Panama City, about an hour to an hour and 15 minutes by car, and has the supermarkets, gated golf communities and established expat population that most foreign buyers on this coast want.

Search for Coronado, Panama houses for sale and the results run together. Coronado itself is a community in the corregimiento of Bejuco, in the district of Chame, Panamá Oeste.[1] Listing portals file homes from Chame, San Carlos, Nueva Gorgona, Punta Barco and Las Lajas under one "Playa Coronado" label, so a $129,999 house and a $1.45 million estate can show up on the same results page.[2][3]

This guide separates what sits behind that label. It covers the gated golf and equestrian communities, what houses, condos and lots ask as of November 2026, and the legal checks that matter on beach land. It also covers daily life an hour from the capital and how Coronado compares with Boquete, the country's other big expat base.

Overview

Why Coronado: The Closest Full-Service Beach Town to Panama City

Coronado is the closest full-service beach town to Panama City. It is 80 to 85 km west on the Pan-American Highway, about an hour to an hour and 15 minutes by car when traffic is light.[4][5] That distance is the main reason people buy here. You get the Pacific, golf and a supermarket a few minutes away, and the capital's private hospitals are still within a morning's drive.

The town grew up around the Coronado Golf & Beach Resort. Its 18-hole course was designed by Tom Fazio, and the resort also has a clubhouse, pools, tennis, an equestrian center, private beach access and 24-hour security.[5] In the mid-1900s the area was a handful of small Pacific ranches.[6] Today's buyers are a mix of foreign retirees, many of them North Americans on the Pensionado visa, and Panama City professionals who come out on weekends.[4][7]

Foreigners can buy titled property in Coronado with the same rights as Panamanians, and they do not need residency to do it.[5] Ownership is registered at the Registro Público, and Panama uses the US dollar, so every listing and closing is priced in dollars.[8][4]

Coronado does not suit everyone. One 2026 expat guide calls it a good match for retirees who want beach life without real remoteness. The same guide calls it a poor match for anyone looking for everyday Panamanian small-town culture, because social life leans toward expats and well-off city families.[4] You can get by in English: many locals in Coronado speak it.[9]

Heat, Rain and the "Least Rainy" Claim

Daytime temperatures are about 30-32 °C (86-90 °F), with a steady ocean breeze all year.[5] As on the rest of Panama's Pacific side, the dry season runs roughly December to April and the rainy season from May to November.[10]

Agents often say Coronado holds the record as Panama's least rainy spot.[6] The official data does not support that. The Ministry of Environment puts mean annual rainfall for the Chame district at about 2,500 mm, notes that the district has no active weather station, and says only that low-lying Chame and Gorgona get less rain than the hills around Sorá.[11] The driest part of the country is the Arco Seco of Coclé, Herrera and Los Santos, farther west.[12] Panama does not get hurricanes, which is worth knowing when you compare coastal houses.[8]

Good to know · LISTINGS

Portals tag homes as "Playa Coronado" even when they are in Chame, Nueva Gorgona, San Carlos or Punta Barco, and some even place Coronado in Coclé. Check the corregimiento on the title, not the listing headline.

Gorgona and Nueva Gorgona

This is where the cheapest houses on the stretch are. A two-bedroom in Nueva Gorgona lists at $129,999, a three-bedroom at $134,900, and a four-bedroom house in Gorgona at $400,000.[3] There are higher-end homes too: a three-bedroom with 450 m² on a Nueva Gorgona boulevard asks $550,000.[13] Coronado's supermarkets are five minutes away, which is why buyers on a tighter budget often start their search here.

Punta Barco and San Carlos

Punta Barco is Coronado's oceanfront end, and the prices reflect it. A 4,420 m² lot asks $1.1 million, and houses there list from $655,000 to $1.45 million.[13][3] Listings tagged "Coronado, San Carlos - San José" range from $210,000 to $335,000, while beach houses in San Carlos ask $1.15 million to $1.25 million.[13][3]

Río Hato, Buenaventura and Playa Blanca

Río Hato, across the line in Coclé, takes in Buenaventura and the Playa Blanca resort strip. Few home prices are published there. What does appear are large land parcels, such as a flat 120-hectare estate near Buenaventura listed at $849,000.[14] This end of the coast suits second homes near the resorts more than full-time life with town services next door.

Mountains or the Azuero Instead

If the coastal heat rules out Coronado, the nearest cool-climate option is El Valle de Antón, about 90 minutes from Panama City.[4] Anyone searching for El Valle real estate should first see what the crater town is like day to day, which our El Valle de Antón travel guide covers. The Azuero Peninsula, home to Pedasí and Playa Venao, has homes advertised from as little as $80,000.[8] The trade-off is that you give up the short drive to the capital's hospitals.

Coronado's gated housing is concentrated in a few places. The main one is the Coronado Golf & Beach Resort, which includes the Alcázar towers and the Alcázar Park estates. The others are the Club Ecuestre de Coronado and newer house projects such as Paradise Point. Outside the gates, central Coronado is open streets of houses, from renovation projects to large homes near the ocean.

The golf resort is the only community with the full set of amenities: the Fazio course, clubhouse, pools, tennis, equestrian center, beach access and round-the-clock security.[5] Inside it, a golf-view condo on the eighth floor of Alcázar's west wing asks $230,000, a hotel-style studio in PH Suites asks $199,000, and a flat lot in the gated golf community asks $100,000.[6] Club Ecuestre feels more residential, and its quieter end has lots that back onto a lake.[5] At Paradise Point, three-bedroom houses rent for $1,800 to $2,500 a month.[15][16]

Monthly Fees: The Number Listings Leave Out

Few Coronado listings publish their HOA fee, even when the listing page has a section for it.[17] Where a figure is public, it gives a sense of scale. Coronado Country Club quotes about $264 a month, covering water from the complex's own wells, gas and garbage collection.[18] A listing for an Alcázar unit mentions an extra $135 a month for the golf and beach club membership, so ask whether club dues are separate from the building fee.

Good to know · HOA

Before you make an offer on a condo or a gated house, get the current monthly fee in writing, along with what it covers. Ask whether club dues are separate and what the rules are on short-term rentals and pets.

Short-term rental is allowed in some buildings. A Coronado Country Club apartment is marketed as a vacation-rental investment, and one Coronado house was listed for both sale and short-term rental.[16] Rent you earn from a property in Panama is Panamanian-source income, so it is taxable in Panama even though the country taxes only local-source income.

The Coronado Expat Community, and Panama's Other Expat Bases

Coronado is one of the places where North American retirees on the Pensionado visa are most active. The others are Boquete and the residential districts of Panama City.[7] Social life centers on golf, tennis and clubs such as Mahjong groups.[9] Picasso's Bar and Restaurant is a regular happy-hour spot for expats and locals alike.[6] Nobody keeps an exact count of foreigners here. Estimates put Panama's foreign residents at 80,000 to 120,000 nationwide, with no precise official figure.[7]

The other expatriate communities in Panama have different profiles. Boquete has the country's most active retiree community. International executives on two- to four-year contracts cluster in Costa del Este and Santa María in Panama City, and English is widely spoken in buildings in San Francisco, Marbella and El Cangrejo.[7] Our guide to living in Panama as an expat compares the cost of living and the best places to settle. Searches for Panama retirement communities mostly lead back to the gated developments of Coronado and the Boquete area, and retiring in Panama covers the visa, budgets and towns retirees choose.

House or Condo: The Trade-Off

A condo is the lock-and-leave choice. Security, the pool and the grounds are someone else's job, and a part-time owner can close the door for half the year. In exchange, you pay a monthly fee you do not control and accept building rules on pets, renovations and rentals.

A house gives you a yard, your own pool and no building votes, but every repair is yours, and an empty house near the coast needs someone to check on it. A gated community gives you some of both: shared security and amenities, plus HOA rules and fees.

Lots and Land

Building your own home is an option, with lots from about $80,000. Examples: 800 m² in Chame at $80,000, 1,251 m² in Coronado at $100,000, and 1,560 m² in Rancho Santa Fe at $110,000.[15][13] Larger rural plots exist too, such as a 2.67-hectare farm near Coronado with a house, a well and fruit trees at $350,000.[16] Construction permits go through the engineering department of the Municipio de Chame.[22]

Renting Before You Buy

As of 2026, long-term furnished rentals in Coronado run from about $500 to $900 a month for a one-bedroom up to $3,500 for a large house near the ocean.[4][5] Some real examples: a one-bedroom in Coronado Golf at $580 a month, a 231 m² three-bedroom house rented at $1,300, and a house on Avenida Roberto Eisenmann at $2,200 a month.[15][14] A couple's full monthly budget, rent included, runs about $1,600 to $2,500.[4] A few months of renting, ideally covering both dry and rainy season, will tell you more about a street than any listing.

Legal

Buying Property in Coronado as a Foreigner: Title, Beach Rules, Taxes

Foreigners can buy titled property in Coronado on the same terms as Panamanians, without residency.[5] On this coast, three checks matter more than they do in the city: the title, where the beach line falls, and the tax status of the property.

The Checks, in Order

1

Confirm a registered title Before an offer

Have your lawyer search the property at the Registro Público.[4] Rights of possession (derechos posesorios) are occupation of state land without a registered title. With them, you buy the improvements and the hope of a future title, not a title a bank will lend against.[23] Titling possessory rights is the job of ANATI.[24]

2

Check where the beach line falls Beach and lakeside lots

Under Law 80 of 2009, the first 22 m inland from high tide on the Pacific is public beach strip. The law also defines a 200 m coastal zone, of which 178 m beyond the strip can be titled.[25] Some guides cite 20 m instead, but the law text says 22 on the Pacific.[26] Beach strips, lakes and rivers are excluded from titling, and mangroves cannot be privately titled.[25][27]

3

Clear debts and taxes Before signing

Ask the seller to show the property is clear of municipal and property tax debts. The Municipio de Chame lets taxpayers check and pay outstanding municipal debts through a virtual assistant on its website.[28]

4

Budget the closing and sign Closing

Expect 2-4% of the price in buyer closing costs.[5] The seller pays the 2% transfer tax unless the Law 546 exemption that has applied since 1 September 2026 covers the sale, which your lawyer can confirm.

5

Register and set your tax regime After closing

Annual property tax under Law 66 of 2017 runs in brackets from 0% to 1%, and registering the home as your primary residence lowers it considerably. Paying the full year before 30 April with no arrears earns a 10% discount (Law 208 of 2021, art. 9).

Our guide to buying property in Panama as a foreigner covers the closing process, costs and taxes in full. The beach-strip and titled-versus-ROP rules have their own page in beachfront and land in Panama for foreigners.

Pre-construction buyers in Coronado can sometimes get developer financing.[6] That ties your money to a building that does not exist yet, so check what the developer has already delivered on this coast. When you sell, capital gains tax is 10% of the gain, and an advance of 3% of the price (3.75% for developers) is paid before the sale is registered.

Residency Through a Coronado Purchase

Coronado prices line up with three residency routes. The thresholds below are current as of November 2026.

Friendly Nations
Open to citizens of more than 50 countries (51, after Ecuador was added by Executive Decree 16 of 31 August 2026); Colombia, Venezuela, Italy and India are not on the list. One way to qualify is a USD 200,000 property, which most Coronado two-bedroom condos and three-bedroom houses clear. You hold two years of provisional residency, then permanent. Fees are B/.250 plus B/.800.
Qualified Investor
Executive Decree 17 of 8 September 2026, in force since 16 September 2026, requires USD 300,000 in a new, first-sale property or USD 500,000 in a resale property. The lower of price paid and market value counts, minus any mortgage. Funds must come from abroad, ROP land does not qualify, and the investment must be kept five years. Government fees are USD 5,000 to the Treasury and USD 5,000 to Migración for the main applicant. MICI certifies within 15 business days and Migración decides within 30.
Qualified Investor transition
Applications filed before 16 September 2026 follow the old Decree 722 rules. Investments or contracts signed before that date can also use them if the application is filed by March 2027.
Pensionado
Requires a lifetime pension of at least USD 1,000 a month, or USD 750 if you also own a property worth USD 100,000. Add USD 250 per dependent. Savings, IRAs and 401(k)s do not count. The main applicant is exempt from the government fee and the repatriation deposit.
Good to know · SNOWBIRDS

Permanent residency can be cancelled if you spend more than two years outside Panama. Owners who only winter in Coronado should plan their visits with that rule in mind.

Requirements, timelines and costs for each route are compared side by side in Panama residency visas compared.

Daily Life

Daily Life in Coronado: Shopping, Healthcare and the Drive to Panama City

Day to day, you can handle groceries, banking, routine medical care and eating out without leaving Coronado. Specialist care and serious emergencies mean the drive to Panama City.

Shopping and Eating Out

Coronado has three shopping centers and supermarkets including El Rey, Super 99 and a small Riba Smith, plus an organic store.[6] The Rey supermarket in the Corowalk mall on the Pan-American has a licensed pharmacy inside.[29] Within ten minutes of the beach you also find El Machetazo, several banks, clinics, hardware stores, vets and dozens of restaurants.[5] Street vendors sell fresh produce for less than the supermarkets.[6] Restaurant bills carry a 7% tax, and a 10-15% tip is usual.[8]

Healthcare

Coronado's private facilities handle routine care. The Ministry of Health's registry lists a clinical laboratory at Hospital San Fernando Coronado.[30] The International Organization for Migration lists a private Coronado hospital with emergency, laboratory, radiology and ambulatory surgery services, though with weekday hours of 7 a.m. to 3 p.m.[31] Expat health guides describe the San Fernando facility as a clinic with an emergency room that refers complex cases to the group's main hospital in Panama City. They also name Clínica Hospital Brisas as another private option in Panamá Oeste.

For anything serious, plan on Panama City. Hospital Punta Pacífica, affiliated with Johns Hopkins, is about an hour away, and specialist consultations there cost $80 to $150 as of 2026.[8][4] The nearest large public hospital is Nicolás Solano in La Chorrera, on the way to the city.[29] Public hospitals are cheap but known for long waits and limited English. Our guide to healthcare in Panama for foreigners compares the private hospitals and insurance options.

The Drive to Panama City

Without traffic, the trip takes about an hour to an hour and 15 minutes.[4][5] The road is being rebuilt. The Corredor de las Playas section of the Pan-American, from La Chorrera to Santa Cruz, is under a six-lane widening contract, with payments to the builder logged through late 2024.[32] The design includes a new return at the Coronado exit, and the technical report's model estimates about 6.4% less travel time per kilometer across the project.[33] Farther west, the MOP awarded a public-private partnership in late 2025 to rehabilitate 192 km of highway from La Loma de Campana to Santiago, with 36 months of construction planned.[34] Expect work zones while these projects run, and allow extra time on Friday evenings and Sunday afternoons, when city weekenders are on the road.

Good to know · TRAFFIC

Panama pays its 13th-month bonus in three parts, typically on 15 April, 15 August and 15 December, and stores and roads are crowded around those dates.[8] Avoid trips to the city on those days if you can.

Retiree Discounts

Under the current consolidated law, retirees on the Pensionado visa get 25% off airfares, 30% off buses, trains and boats, 50% off hotels Monday to Thursday, 25% off restaurant meals, 15% off private hospital bills, and 20% off medical consultations and medicines.[35] A 2026 reform adds discounts on items such as lab tests, X-rays and assistive equipment, though reports differ on the month it was sanctioned.[36][37] The new discounts start six months after publication in the Gaceta Oficial, and the existing ones stay in force until then.[36]

Coronado vs Boquete: Beach Town or Mountain Town

Coronado suits buyers who want the beach, the heat and an hour's drive to Panama City's hospitals. Boquete suits buyers who want a cool highland climate and can accept six hours or more on the road to the capital. Some guides put the Boquete drive at four hours, but operators and route planners give 440-490 km and six to eight hours.[38][39]

Living costs are lower in the mountains. A furnished one-bedroom in downtown Boquete rents for $400 to $700 a month, and two-bedroom houses with yards outside the center start around $600 to $900.[4] A couple in Chiriquí can live comfortably on about $1,995 a month.[8] Coronado's equivalent is $1,600 to $2,500.[4]

Medical access is the bigger difference. From Boquete, the nearest larger city is David, 38 km away on a dual carriageway, about 50 minutes by car.[39][40] Reaching Panama City by bus means two buses and seven to eight hours: Panama City to David for $15 to $18, then David to Boquete for $1.75.[38] From Coronado, Punta Pacífica is about an hour away.[4]

Boquete has the deeper community: it is the center of Panama's most active expat retiree scene.[7] Coronado's case is the drive and, by one 2026 guide's account, an easier resale market, although no published sales data backs up that comparison.[4] If the mountains win, our guide to Boquete real estate covers the neighborhoods expats buy in, price ranges and Volcán as an alternative.

Questions

Before you go

How much do houses for sale in Coronado, Panama cost?

Most mid-range houses ask $250,000 to $500,000, and typical three-bedroom listings fall between about $310,000 and $390,000 as of November 2026. Houses on the beach start above $1 million, and cheaper homes from about $130,000 are found in nearby Nueva Gorgona. The median asking price for houses in Coronado was $1,427 per square meter in November 2026.

How much are condos for sale in Coronado?

Studios start around $60,000 to $65,000, one-bedrooms near the water run $155,000 to $185,000, and two-bedrooms $230,000 to $300,000. The main buildings are PH Coronado Bay, PH Coronado Golf, PH Ibiza and the Alcázar towers in the golf resort. Ask for the monthly HOA fee in writing, because few listings publish it.

How far is Coronado from Panama City?

Coronado is 80 to 85 km west of Panama City, about an hour to an hour and 15 minutes by car on the Pan-American Highway without traffic. Weekend beach traffic, highway works and the 13th-month bonus dates in April, August and December can add time.

Can a foreigner buy a house in Coronado, Panama?

Yes. Foreigners can buy titled property with the same rights as Panamanians, and no residency is required. Have a lawyer confirm the title at the Registro Público, avoid rights-of-possession land, and check where the 22 m public beach strip falls on any beachfront lot. See buying property in Panama as a foreigner for the full process.

Are there gated communities in Coronado, Panama?

Yes. The main ones are the Coronado Golf & Beach Resort, the Club Ecuestre de Coronado and newer projects such as Paradise Point. The golf resort has a Tom Fazio course, a beach club, pools, tennis, an equestrian center and 24-hour security. Monthly fees vary, and one nearby complex quotes about $264 a month.

Is there a hospital in Coronado?

Coronado has private clinics with emergency rooms, labs and radiology for routine care, but serious cases go to Panama City. The San Fernando facility in Coronado refers complex cases to its main hospital in the city, and Hospital Punta Pacífica is about an hour's drive away.

How much does it cost to rent in Coronado long term?

Furnished one-bedrooms rent for about $500 to $900 a month, and large houses near the ocean for up to $3,500, as of 2026. Three-bedroom houses in gated Paradise Point rent for $1,800 to $2,500. Renting for a few months before buying is a good way to test a neighborhood.

Is Coronado the least rainy place in Panama?

Not according to official data. The Ministry of Environment puts mean annual rainfall for the Chame district at about 2,500 mm and has no active weather station there. Panama's driest region is the Arco Seco of Coclé, Herrera and Los Santos. Coronado does have a clear dry season from December to April.

Is Coronado or Boquete better for retirees?

Coronado if you want the beach and an hour's drive to Panama City's hospitals; Boquete if you want a cool climate and lower rents. Boquete has the larger retiree community, but it is six hours or more from the capital by road. Compare prices in our Boquete real estate guide.

Where are the main expatriate communities in Panama?

The largest are Boquete, Coronado and Panama City, where Costa del Este, Santa María, El Cangrejo and San Francisco have many foreign residents. Smaller communities live in Bocas del Toro, El Valle de Antón and the Azuero coast around Pedasí. Our guide to living in Panama as an expat compares them.
