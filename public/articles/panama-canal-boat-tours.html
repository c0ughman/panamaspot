Panama Canal tours from Panama City: partial vs full transit, prices and schedule

Panama Canal boat tours compared: partial vs full transit, what you'll see, which days they run, prices and how to book.

A Panama Canal boat tour from Panama City comes in two forms. The first is a partial transit of about 4.5 to 7 hours through the Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks and the Culebra (Gaillard) Cut, priced at about $144 to $220 per adult in 2026. The second is a full ocean-to-ocean transit of roughly 9 to 12 hours that runs on only a few weekend dates, and in both cases the exact start time is set the day before.

Only a handful of passenger vessels carry tourists through the locks. Many listings sold under different company names put you on the same ship, the Pacific Queen, which is why the same Panama Canal boat tour can show up at $144 on one site and $220 on another.[1][2]

Two things catch first-timers out. You do not choose the direction or the departure time, because both follow the canal's daily traffic. The ride to the marina is usually not included either.

Compare

Partial vs full Panama Canal transit: which tour to pick

For most visitors the partial transit is the better choice. It covers the two Pacific lock sets and the Culebra Cut in half a day. The full transit adds Gatun Lake and the Gatun Locks, but it takes 9 to 12 hours and runs on only a few dates a month. If all you want is to watch a ship rise in a lock chamber, the Miraflores Visitor Center shows you that from dry land for a fraction of the price.

When the full transit is worth it

The full transit is the only way to cross from the Pacific to the Caribbean without joining a cruise. One listing has it leaving at 5:30 a.m., running about 11.5 hours and ending at Shelter Bay near Colón.[3] Other sellers describe it as 9 hours or more, with a bus or shuttle back to Panama City at the end.[4][5]

That is a long day, and it suits people who care about the canal itself. You go through all three lock sets, and a long middle stretch is open-lake cruising on Gatun Lake. Some passengers find that stretch the most scenic part of the day and others find it slow. Because it runs on so few dates, the trip has to fit your calendar rather than the other way round.

Skip the full transit if you have one free day and other plans for it, or if you are traveling with small children. The partial transit gives you the lock experience and has you off the boat by early afternoon. If you are weighing it against a trip out of the city, our ranking of day trips from Panama City compares the canal transit with El Valle, Taboga and San Blas on time and cost.

Or skip the boat: Miraflores Visitor Center

At the Miraflores Visitor Center, non-resident adults pay $17.22 and children 6 to 12 pay $7.22. Under-6s go free, and the IMAX film is included.[6] Tickets are sold online through the canal's official store and can be paid by card.[7]

A visit takes about an hour and a half. From the viewing decks you watch the same Miraflores chambers a partial transit goes through, just from the side.[8] Our Miraflores Locks visitor guide explains when ships transit and how to time your visit. On the Caribbean side, non-residents pay $5 to $10 at the Agua Clara Visitor Center.[9]

The route

What you'll see on a Panama Canal boat tour

A partial transit takes you under the Bridge of the Americas, through the Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks and along the Culebra Cut to Gamboa, with a guide talking about the canal's history on board.[4] Those two lock sets are where ships climb from the Pacific to the level of Gatun Lake, 26 meters (85 feet) above the sea.[10] You watch the chamber walls rise or fall around you as the water fills or drains.

The order depends on the direction, which you do not choose. The steps below follow the northbound version, which starts on the water at Amador. On a southbound day you ride the bus to Gamboa first, do the same stops in reverse and finish back at the causeway.

1

Check in at Flamenco Marina 6:00-7:00 a.m.

Northbound trips usually check in during this window at the marina on the last island of the Amador Causeway.[1] The marina sits next to the Panama sign, behind the duty-free shop.[11]

2

Under the Bridge of the Americas

The boat heads up the Pacific entrance of the canal and passes under the arched bridge that marks it. This is the first good photo of the morning.

3

Miraflores Locks

The gates close behind you and water floods in until the boat rises to the next level. Visitors on the Miraflores Visitor Center decks watch from just above. In the neighboring lane, large ships are kept centered by electric locomotives called mules.

4

Pedro Miguel Locks

A short way north, the last Pacific lock set lifts you to the level of the canal's central section.

5

Culebra (Gaillard) Cut

This is the channel dug through the continental divide. It is narrow enough that Panamax ships go through it one at a time.[12]

6

Gamboa and the bus back 45 min

You step off at Gamboa, and a coach takes you back to Flamenco Marina. The same 45-minute ride runs in the other direction on southbound days.[11]

What the full transit adds

Past Gamboa, the full transit crosses Gatun Lake, drops down through the Gatun Locks to the Caribbean and docks at Colón.[4] Gatun Lake was created by damming the Chagres River and is billed as the largest artificial lake in the world. Barro Colorado Island, in the middle of the lake, is home to the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute.[13] Day tours do not list a stop at the island. The two-day crossings run by Lindblad Expeditions land guests there by Zodiac.[13]

Wildlife is a bonus rather than a promise. Guests on transits through the lake section have seen mantled howler and white-faced capuchin monkeys, yellow-throated toucans and a three-toed sloth.[13] Swimming is not allowed in Gatun Lake, which has crocodiles.[12]

Getting the best view

The Pacific Queen has two enclosed, air-conditioned decks and an open sun deck that is partly covered, plus a bar on the lower deck and five restrooms.[14] Narration comes over the ship's speaker system.[14] Cruise lines give their passengers the same advice for canal transits: head to the front of the ship.[15]

The open deck is where you see the gates swing and the water level change. Bring sun protection and a rain layer for the open deck, and a light layer for the air-conditioned cabins.

Schedule

Panama Canal tour schedule: which days boats run and what time

Partial transits run mostly on weekends. The start time is only fixed the day before, because tourist boats are slotted in around commercial traffic. Trip Spanama, for example, sells Saturday and Sunday departures only and confirms the direction one day ahead.[16]

The Pacific Queen's older published schedule listed a partial transit every Saturday and one full transit a month. Some listings also mention occasional Friday departures.[14][17] Usually only one partial transit runs per day, either in the morning or the afternoon, and partial departures shift when a full transit runs the same day.[14][1]

When you plan your stay, keep a weekend morning free. Our 3-day Panama City itinerary shows how to fit the canal around Casco Viejo and the causeway.

Departure times

Northbound trips typically check in between 6:00 and 7:00 a.m. and southbound trips between 9:00 and 10:00 a.m. The Canal Authority confirms final times the day before.[1] Panama Canal Tours boards from Flamenco Marina, La Playita de Amador or Brisas de Amador, usually between 6:00 and 10:00 a.m.[18] One booking agent sends the meeting time and marina by email before 5:00 p.m. the day before.[4]

Good to know · DIRECTION

You cannot request northbound or southbound. If one direction matters to you, book a date and accept whatever the canal assigns, because the views are nearly the same either way.

Full transit dates

Full transits are rare. Barefoot Panama says they usually run on the third Saturday of the month, while Anywhere lists Saturday and Sunday availability with a 5:30 a.m. start.[4][3] Operators confirm full-transit dates when you contact them rather than publishing a long calendar.[4] Ask for the next date before you build a trip around it.

Best months to go

The canal runs year-round and so do the tours. The dry season, from mid-December to mid-April, gives you the best odds of a clear day on the open deck.[13] Outside those months rain is more likely, and the enclosed decks become the place to sit. Our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Panama covers the Pacific side in detail.

Low water and the 2026 drought measures

The canal has been under water-conservation measures since December 2025.[19] In an advisory dated August 20, 2026, the Authority cut daily Neopanamax slots to nine from September 3.[20] These measures limit the draft and number of the biggest ships. Partial transits use the original Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks, which opened with the canal in 1914.[13]

In May 2026 the Authority said it was keeping 38 daily transits and did not forecast transit restrictions through December 31, 2026.[19] During the 2023 drought, by contrast, daily transits fell to 24 from a typical 36.[21]

Booking

How to book a Panama Canal transit tour

You book a tourist transit through a boat operator or a reseller such as Viator, Anywhere or TripAdvisor, not through the Panama Canal Authority. The Authority's transit booking system is for commercial vessels. The steps below are the same everywhere, and what changes between sellers is the payment and cancellation terms. If you are planning in Spanish, our guide to the tour por el Canal de Panamá en barco covers the same partial and full transits.

1

Pick the trip and the date

Partial transits can be booked on a live calendar. For a full transit, you contact the operator, who confirms availability and the departure date.[4]

2

Pay a deposit or the full fare

Terms vary. The Panama Tours Site takes a $15 deposit per adult and $10 per child, while Trip Spanama requires full payment at booking.[1][16]

3

Check the cancellation terms

The Panama Marine Adventures listing gives a full refund if you cancel at least 24 hours before departure.[11] Ask any other seller for their terms in writing.

4

Watch for the day-before email

Operators usually email the exact check-in time the day before, and some tours issue a QR code 48 hours before departure.[2]

5

Get to the marina early

Arrive at the time and dock in your email. Panama Canal Tours uses three different docks on the Amador Causeway, so check yours.

Checking the operator

Panama's tourism authority requires operators of maritime tourist transport to meet these minimum rules:[24]

  • Register with the Panama Maritime Authority.
  • Carry civil liability insurance.
  • Provide a life vest for every passenger.

A 2025 executive decree gives the Maritime Authority oversight of nautical tourism through a dedicated office.[25] The Pacific Queen has run canal transits since October 2003.[14] A listing that names its vessel and its dock gives you more to verify than one that does not.

Good to know · ACCESS

Boarding and moving between decks involve stairs. If anyone in your group has limited mobility, ask the operator which deck is reachable without stairs before you pay.

Ship transits

How long does it take to transit the Panama Canal?

A ship takes about 8 to 10 hours to cross the canal, according to the Panama Canal Authority. Most ships spend far longer in canal waters once the wait at anchor is counted. On a tour boat the answer is simpler: 4.5 to 7 hours for a partial transit and roughly 9 to 12 for a full one.

8-10 hofficial average crossing time
9.7-11.8 hmonthly in-transit range, fiscal 2026
20-38 hmonthly canal waters time, fiscal 2026

The Authority tracks two numbers. In-transit time is the actual passage through the locks and the Cut. It ranged from 9.7 hours in November 2025 to 11.84 hours in April 2026.[26] Canal waters time adds the wait from arrival at anchor, and it ran from 20.2 hours in October 2025 to 37.97 hours in September 2026.[26]

Ships without a reservation waited longest, with peaks of 78.15 hours.[26] For comparison, ships took an average of 11.38 hours to pass between the two outer locks in 2017.[10]

Cruise ships and yachts

A cruise ship's full transit takes 8 to 10 hours. One reporter's ship began entering the Gatun Locks at about 6:30 a.m.[27] Carnival describes the crossing as taking at least 20 hours, a figure that likely counts the approach and waiting rather than the passage between the outer locks.[28]

Princess sells full transits from Los Angeles and San Francisco, and it also recommends mid-December to mid-April.[29]

Private yachts are slower. One sailing crew put the motoring time at about 15 hours.[12] The scheduler sends small boats through either in a single day starting at 4:00 a.m. or over two days, with a night tied to a buoy after the Gatun Locks.[12]

Tolls

How much does it cost to transit the Panama Canal?

It depends on the vessel. Under the tariff in force since January 2025, a yacht under 65 feet pays a fixed $2,130 plus a $500 scheduling charge, while the largest cruise and cargo ships pay hundreds of thousands of dollars per crossing.[30][13] As a tour passenger you never see a toll, because your ticket is the whole cost.

Cruise ships are charged by capacity, not by passenger. The Authority's tariff list prices passenger vessels at $4.25 to $5.50 per CP/SUAB, the canal's unit of capacity tonnage.[30] Cruise lines then fold their share into port taxes and fees, which Princess says can be calculated per passenger, per berth, per ton or per ship.[31]

For a small yacht, the real bill is higher than the toll. One crew paid just over $3,000 for a boat under 65 feet and got about $1,000 back two months later because the transit ran without delays.[12] Lindblad puts the low end at about $2,700.[13] The Authority's tariff page links a newer schedule dated October 15, 2026, so check it before you budget a crossing.[30]

Those tolls add up. The canal's toll revenue reached B/.4,007.9 million in fiscal 2025, up 26.07% on the year before.[32]

Questions

Before you go

What is the best Panama Canal tour?

For most visitors, the partial transit is the best Panama Canal tour. In 4.5 to 7 hours it takes you through the Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks and the Culebra Cut, with lunch included on most sailings. The full transit suits canal enthusiasts with a whole free day. The Miraflores Visitor Center is the cheapest way to see a lock in action.

How much does a Panama Canal boat tour cost?

A partial transit costs about $144 to $220 per adult in 2026, and some sellers add 17% tax on top. Full transits are listed from about $195 to $240. Hotel transfers are usually extra, but an Uber to Flamenco Marina costs only about $4 to $5.

How long does a Panama Canal tour take?

A partial transit takes about 4.5 to 7 hours, including the 45-minute bus between Gamboa and the Amador Causeway. A full transit runs roughly 9 to 12 hours from Flamenco Marina to Colón, plus the road trip back to Panama City.

How long does it take a ship to transit the Panama Canal?

About 8 to 10 hours of actual crossing, according to the Panama Canal Authority. Counting the wait at anchor, average time in canal waters ran from about 20 to 38 hours a month in fiscal 2026. Ships without a reservation waited longest.

How much does it cost to transit the Panama Canal?

It ranges from about $2,130 plus a $500 scheduling charge for a yacht under 65 feet to hundreds of thousands of dollars for the largest ships. Cruise ships are charged by capacity tonnage, at $4.25 to $5.50 per CP/SUAB under the 2025 tariff. Tour passengers pay only their ticket.

How much does it cost for a 40 ft sailboat to go through the Panama Canal?

A 40-foot sailboat falls in the under-65-feet bracket: a $2,130 toll plus a $500 scheduling charge under the 2025 tariff. Line and fender rentals and line handlers come on top. One crew reported a total of just over $3,000, with about $1,000 refunded after a delay-free transit.

What is the best month for a Panama Canal tour?

The dry season, from mid-December to mid-April, gives you the best odds of clear weather on the open deck. Tours run year-round because the canal never closes. In the rainy months, expect to spend more time on the enclosed, air-conditioned decks.

Can I see the Panama Canal locks without a boat tour?

Yes, at the Miraflores Visitor Center, where non-resident adults pay $17.22 and children 6 to 12 pay $7.22, with the IMAX film included. A visit takes about an hour and a half. Our Miraflores Locks visitor guide explains when ships pass.

Are canal boat tours cancelled when water levels are low?

The 2026 drought measures are aimed at large ships, limiting their draft and cutting daily Neopanamax slots. Partial transits use the original Miraflores and Pedro Miguel locks. Departure times are always confirmed the day before, so treat your time as provisional until that email arrives.

Who owns the Panama Canal today?

Panama does. The United States handed over the canal under the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties, and Panama took full control in 1999. The government-owned Panama Canal Authority now runs it, including the visitor centers.
