Boquete homes for sale ask from about $130,000 for a small two-bedroom house in Alto Boquete or El Francés to well over $1 million for estates, with most expat-market houses between roughly $200,000 and $600,000 as of November 2026.[1] Building lots start under $50,000. Gated homes in Valle Escondido and Boquete Country Club mostly ask $395,000 to $600,000, and Volcán, on the far side of Volcán Barú, is the cheaper highland alternative, with typical homes from about $90,000.

Boquete homes for sale are a bigger market than the small town suggests. Realtor.com International lists 1,029 properties across the district, from a $46,500 lot in Palmira to a $4,950,000 house. Most of them sit outside the walkable center, in Alto Boquete, the hills above town and the gated communities.[2]

Almost every figure you will see is an asking price. Panama has no official house-price index, so treat listings as a ceiling, not a valuation.[3] Real sale prices do exist: the Public Registry records the price of every registered sale, so an attorney can pull comparable sales near the house before you make an offer.[4]

Why Boquete

Why Foreigners Buy Real Estate in Boquete, Chiriquí

Foreigners buy in Boquete for the climate, the established expat community and simple ownership rules. Titled property in Panama gives a foreigner the same rights as a Panamanian, with no residency, local partner or special permit needed.[5] The town sits at about 1,200 meters (3,900 feet) in Chiriquí province and has spring-like weather year-round.[6]

The community is the second draw. Boquete has one of the largest concentrations of pensionado residents in Panama, so English-speaking neighbors are easy to find.[7] The Pensionado visa is the usual route for retired buyers. It needs a lifetime pension of at least $1,000 a month, or $750 if you also own a property worth $100,000. Our guide to retiring in Panama covers the visa, monthly budgets and the other towns retirees compare.

Access matters if you fly home often. David's airport is about 30 minutes by car, and buses between Boquete and David run every 30 minutes for $1.75.[6][8] For the town itself (trails, coffee farms, restaurants), see our complete Boquete travel guide.

The trade-offs are real. The wet season runs roughly May to November, and that is when hillside roads slide. The nearest full hospital is in David. The town has also grown faster than its planning: Panama's tourism authority describes Boquete's growth as disorderly and says it urgently needs a land-use plan.[9]

Neighborhoods

The first choice is between Bajo Boquete, where you can live without a car, and everywhere else. The other areas fall into two groups: those south toward David (Alto Boquete, El Francés, Palmira) and those on the slopes above town (Volcancito, Santa Lucía, Jaramillo, Los Naranjos, El Salto). Bajo Boquete: Walkable, Smaller, by the River

Bajo Boquete is the town center and the main choice for buyers who want to walk everywhere. One furnished three-bedroom house of 390 square meters lists at $350,000. It sits across from the police station, five minutes on foot from the central park, Romero supermarket, the market, banks and pharmacies.[11] Apartments in newer buildings such as Pino Alto start at $218,400 for a two-bedroom of about 52 square meters.[12]

Cheap riverside land does turn up, such as a 1,900-square-meter lot two minutes from downtown for $38,000.[13] Treat it with caution. The planning study for Boquete's land-use plan classes land along the Caldera and Cochea rivers, through Los Naranjos, Boquete and Alto Boquete, as exposed to floods and landslides.[14]

Alto Boquete, El Francés and Palmira: South Toward David

Alto Boquete has the most inventory and most of the gated developments. Realtor.com shows 291 houses there, from $132,000 for a two-bedroom of about 1,100 square feet up to $1.58 million.[1] It is also the district's most populous area. It sits lower than town, at about 1,010 meters, so it is warmer.[15] It is also closer to David: one listed property is 12.8 km from Bajo Boquete and 25.6 km from the city.[16]

El Francés is where the cheapest houses for sale in Boquete usually turn up. One listing is a two-bedroom garden home of 135 square meters on a 1,000-square-meter lot at $199,000. Another is a three-bedroom of 246 square meters at $215,000.[17]

Palmira, on the southern approach, is slightly lower and sunnier than town.[7] It also has the cheapest land: a lot of about 4,330 square meters lists at $46,500, while about 6,700 square meters in Palmira Abajo asks $250,000.[2]

Volcancito, Santa Lucía, Jaramillo, Los Naranjos and El Salto: The Hills Above Town

Volcancito lies north of town, closer to the volcano's slopes,[7] and this is where view lots get expensive. Realtor.com shows land there at $120,000 for about 2,000 square meters, $330,000 for about 2,200 and $450,000 for about 8,850.[2] A two-bedroom apartment in Volcancito asks $273,650.[12] Santa Lucía, within the Volcancito area, has the large canyon-view homes. A four-bedroom of 390 square meters asks $730,000, and a five-bedroom $695,000.[17][2]

Jaramillo, on the slopes above town, is known for custom homes with views of Volcán Barú. Cabins there rent for $1,200 and $1,500 a month.[10] Farther north, the listings turn agricultural. A titled farm in Los Naranjos asks $500,000, and a five-hectare mini farm with specialty coffee in El Salto asks $875,000.[18]

Caldera: Acreage Outside the Valley

Caldera is the district's largest and most thinly populated area, and it is where you find acreage at prices the valley cannot match. A 9.88-acre estate there lists at $295,000, negotiable.[10] Expect longer drives for groceries and medical care, and check the access road in the wet season.

Good to know · LANDSLIDES

In October 2025, SINAPROC recorded landslides affecting homes in Los Naranjos, Bajo Boquete, Jaramillo, Palo Alto, Palmira, Volcancito and El Salto, and seven families were evacuated.[19] Ask the seller and the neighbors what happened to the lot and its access road that month.

Climate

Climate by Altitude: How High to Buy

The higher the lot, the cooler it is, so the same budget buys a different climate in Alto Boquete than in Volcancito. Town sits at about 1,200 meters and Alto Boquete at about 1,010.[15] Listing agents put Volcancito at roughly 4,000 to 5,500 feet (about 1,200 to 1,700 meters).

Lower lots along the David road are clearly warmer. One titled lot 20 minutes from downtown quotes local temperatures of 75 to 90°F.[13] Volcán, at about 1,400 meters, sees daytime highs of 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F), nights of 13 to 18°C, and the occasional clear night below 10°C.[5]

The dry season runs roughly December to April.[7] That is also when most buyers visit, which makes it the wrong time to judge a house. Brokers themselves suggest seeing a property in both a dry and a rainy month.[5] Our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Boquete explains the windy, drizzly bajareque season and the wettest months.

Fees are rarely published. Listings in the Boquete Country Club area quote a maintenance fee of $250 a month, plus $25 a month for the club facilities.[20] For any other community, get the current fee, the last two years of increases and the rules in writing before you sign. The rules can include limits on rentals, pets and building changes.

Lucero's golf course, clubhouse, pool, clay courts and 24/7 security are listed in its sales material.[13] One detail is not: the emergency plan for the La Estrella hydroelectric plant notes that Lucero lies very close to the plant's Cámara de Carga reservoir and would be crossed by the flood if that dam failed.[21]

New gated homes are mostly in Alto Boquete. The View Boquete, a project still under construction with homes on 450-square-meter lots, is listed from $83,900.[22] Lots at Las Trancas View start at $126,005 for 520 square meters.[18] With pre-construction, the risk is the developer, not the house. Ask for the construction permit, check that the land title is in the developer's name, and confirm when the occupation permit is expected.

Local neighborhoods cost less to own because there is no monthly fee. They do vary more from house to house. Water supply, power reliability, road access and title status are things to check one property at a time, especially on rural land sold outside a development.

Volcán

Volcán, Panama Real Estate and Cerro Punta: The Cheaper Highlands

Volcán is cheaper than Boquete for both houses and daily life. The cost is a longer drive to David and a much smaller market. The town sits at about 1,400 meters (4,600 feet) on the western slopes of Volcán Barú, roughly 200 meters higher than Boquete town.[5]

Volcán is the commercial center of the Tierras Altas district, which also includes Cerro Punta, Cuesta de Piedra, Nueva California and Paso Ancho.[24] Super Lupe and Super Bambito cover groceries, with bigger stores in David. The expat community runs to a few hundred people, far smaller than Boquete's.[5] The main road is getting sidewalks, a bike path and a new town center around the park.[24]

Getting around is the main trade-off:[5]

  • David is 50 to 55 km by road, about an hour to an hour and a half.
  • Boquete is only 22 km away as the crow flies, but about 70 km and 1.5 hours or more by road via David.
  • The Costa Rica border at Río Sereno is about 45 minutes away.

The Ruta Sur, which runs from Boquete through Volcancito, Potrerillos and Cuesta de Piedra to Volcán, has shortened the trip between the two towns.[24] It has no public transport.[25]

Cerro Punta lies 16 km beyond Volcán by road.[26] It is farm country, where the soil grows strawberries, onions, potatoes, flowers and coffee alongside dairy and cattle.[24] Tierras Altas and Boquete are the two Chiriquí districts where landslides concentrate,[27] and the Tierras Altas land-use plan drawn up in 2022 has still not been approved for implementation.[28]

The market is thin. InmoPanama shows 8 properties for sale in Volcán against 42 in Boquete,[29][30] so expect a longer search, and a longer wait when you sell. If cool nights and highland rain are not what you want, Coronado real estate on the Pacific coast is the beach alternative most retirees compare with Boquete.

Daily Life

Living There: Healthcare in David, Roads, Internet and Running Costs

Healthcare: Local Clinics, Hospitals in David

Routine care is local, but hospital care means David. Bajo Boquete has pharmacies and medical services within walking distance of the central park,[11] and Volcán has a MINSA health center for primary care.[5]

The main public hospital is the Hospital Regional Dr. Rafael Hernández in David. It is a Social Security (CSS) hospital and the referral center for Chiriquí, Bocas del Toro, part of Veraguas and the Ngäbe Buglé comarca, with 602 beds as of 2021.[31] It runs a 24-hour emergency service on the Pan-American Highway at San Pablo Viejo.[32] It offered hemodialysis in October 2026[33] and held extra cardiac-procedure days in September 2026.[34]

The private hospitals in David are Hospital Chiriquí and Hospital Centro Médico Mae Lewis.[5] The public system has had gaps. In 2014 the regional hospital's endoscopy equipment was out of service for six months, and a Boquete patient paid just over $400 for the exam at a private clinic.[35] In June 2026, Panama's Ombudsman made an unannounced inspection of the hospital.[36] Accreditation, costs and insurance are covered in our guide to healthcare in Panama for foreigners.

Roads and Drive Times

Drives from town are short to David and long to anywhere else. Boquete is about 30 minutes from David's airport,[6] and Hacienda Los Molinos about 25 minutes.[11] One private community in Alto Boquete is about 29 minutes from David.[22] Properties farther out quote longer times: a 23-hectare property 15 minutes from downtown puts the airport an hour away.[13]

The road that matters most is the last kilometer to the house. In October 2025, SINAPROC recorded 40 landslides in the district, with 12 homes and 76 people affected. Three of those slides were on the road from Alto Boquete toward Palmira Centro.[37] Visit a hillside lot after heavy rain, and ask neighbors whether the access road has closed before.

Internet

Internet is the one service you cannot judge from a listing. No public coverage map shows which streets in Boquete, Alto Boquete or Volcán have fiber, so check the specific address:

  • Ask the seller for a recent internet bill.
  • Run a speed test at the house on a weekday evening.
  • Ask the provider in writing whether the street is wired.

If it is not, the house depends on wireless or mobile signal, which varies with the terrain.

Running Costs

On top of daily living, a homeowner pays the annual property tax, insurance, upkeep and, in a gated community, the monthly fee. That fee is $250 at Boquete Country Club, as above. For full monthly budgets by town, see our guide to living in Panama as an expat, which covers the cost of living in Boquete alongside Panama City and the coast.

Rentals

Renting Before You Buy, and Renting Your Home Out

Renting for a few months before buying is the cheapest way to test an area, and the long-term market is deep. Furnished homes rent for roughly $900 to $2,000 a month across most neighborhoods.[7] Current examples include a furnished three-bedroom apartment in Bajo Boquete at $850 and cabins in Jaramillo at $1,200 and $1,500.[10] Furnished apartments in Alto Boquete rent for $950 and $1,400.[16]

Short-term rental is common, but the rules for a single home are unclear. Boquete had around 280 homes on Airbnb in IPAT data from 2017 and 2018, most of Chiriquí's roughly 500.[38] The tourism authority's registration for public lodging falls under Executive Decree 82 of 2008. It asks for a Public Registry property certificate, a floor plan, a certificate of invested capital and zoning from the competent authority.[39]

That registration does not say whether a house rented by the night must register. If rental income is part of the plan, get your attorney's view before you buy, and check the community rules if the house is gated. Rent from a property in Panama is Panamanian-source income and taxable there. Occupancy is highest in the December to April dry season, when visitor numbers peak.[7]

How to Buy

How Buying a House in Boquete Works: Steps, Costs and Residency

Buying in Boquete follows the national process: a promise to purchase, a title search, then a notary closing and registration. A straightforward titled purchase usually takes four to eight weeks from accepted offer to closing, with a deposit of about 10% at the promise stage.[7] The attorney searches the title at the Public Registry and checks liens, boundaries, zoning and the seller's right to sell before you close at a notary.[5] Our guide to buying property in Panama as a foreigner goes through each document in detail.

1

Rent and look in both seasons Before you offer

Spend time in the area you are considering in at least one dry and one rainy month, and drive the access road to any house you like.

2

Hire your own attorney Before you offer

Choose a Panama-licensed attorney with experience in Chiriquí property, not one recommended by the seller.

3

Sign a promise to purchase About 10% deposit

The contract sets the price, the deposit, the closing date and what happens to the deposit if the title check fails.

4

Run due diligence During the promise

Title search, liens, boundary survey and tax status. Confirm the land is titled, not rights of possession, and ask the Municipio de Boquete or SINAPROC whether the lot sits in a mapped risk zone.

5

Close at a notary and register 4-8 weeks total

Wire the funds from abroad and sign the deed at the notary. The transfer is then registered at the Public Registry.

6

Set up the house After closing

Open a local bank account, then arrange insurance, utilities and the annual property tax payment.

Risk-zone maps exist for construction in Boquete, but the planning study for the district says they are not applied rigorously.[14] Asking for them is your job, not the seller's.

Good to know · TITLE

Rights of possession (ROP or derechos posesorios) are a claim to use land, not a title. They do not qualify for residency by investment and are harder to finance and resell. Rural lots and farms are where they turn up most.

The property transfer tax (ITBI) is 2% on ordinary sales.[40] It is charged on the higher of the price and the cadastral value and falls on the seller. One Panama law firm says any contract clause shifting it to the buyer is void,[41] while some brokers describe it as negotiable.[5]

New homes have a newer rule. For the first sale of a new home, Law 546 of 2026 exempts the first B/.120,000 of the taxable base if the sale closes within two years of the occupation permit.[42] It applies from 1 September 2026, with reduced rates up to B/.200,000 and the general rate on anything above that.[43] The saving goes to the developer, who still owes income tax of 0.5% to 2.5% on the first sale,[41] but it can give you room to negotiate on a new build.

When you sell later, capital gains tax is 10% of the gain. The seller pays an advance of 3% of the price before the sale is registered.

Annual property tax under Law 66 of 2017 runs in brackets from 0% to 1%, with a separate primary-residence regime. Paying the full year before 30 April with no arrears earns a 10% discount.

Residency Through a Boquete Purchase

The price band decides which residency route a purchase can support:

  • Qualified Investor. Executive Decree 17 of 2026 (in force since 16 September 2026) sets the minimum at $300,000 for a new, first-sale property and $500,000 for a resale. The lower of the price paid and the market value counts, minus any mortgage. The funds must come from abroad, the investment must be kept five years, and the main applicant pays $5,000 to the Treasury and $5,000 to the National Immigration Service.
  • Friendly Nations. Citizens of the more than 50 listed countries can qualify with a $200,000 property.
  • Pensionado. A pension of $750 a month is enough if you also own a property worth $100,000.

In Boquete terms, a 2026-built canyon-view house in Alto Boquete at $410,000 clears the Qualified Investor bar on its first sale. A resale in Boquete Country Club at $395,000 to $445,000 does not.[10] Rights-of-possession land never qualifies. Our comparison of Panama residency visas sets out who qualifies for each route, the money required and the timelines.

Questions

Before you go

Can a US citizen buy real estate in Boquete, Panama?

Yes, with the same rights as a Panamanian on titled property. You do not need residency, a local partner or a special permit to own a house or land. The caution is land held only as rights of possession (ROP), which is not a title and does not qualify for residency by investment.

How much does it cost to live in Boquete, Panama?

Housing is the biggest variable: furnished long-term rentals run roughly $900 to $2,000 a month. Current examples range from an $850 apartment in Bajo Boquete to $1,500 cabins in Jaramillo. Owners add property tax, upkeep and, in gated communities, fees such as $250 a month at Boquete Country Club. Full budgets are in our guide to living in Panama as an expat.

What are the cheapest areas to buy a house in Boquete?

El Francés and Alto Boquete have the cheapest houses, and Palmira the cheapest land. Two- and three-bedroom houses in El Francés ask about $195,000 to $215,000, Alto Boquete houses start around $132,000, and a Palmira lot lists at $46,500 as of November 2026. For lower prices still, look at Volcán.

Is Volcán cheaper than Boquete?

Yes, for both property and daily costs. Typical Volcán homes ask about $90,000 to $600,000 and small lots $40,000 to $90,000, with rentals and food also cheaper than in Boquete. The trade-offs are a drive of an hour or more to David, a smaller expat community of a few hundred people and a thinner resale market.

How much are closing costs, and who pays the transfer tax?

Buyers usually budget 2% to 4% of the price, including attorney fees of about 1% to 2%. The 2% transfer tax (ITBI) is the seller's obligation on ordinary sales. Since 1 September 2026, the first sale of a new home is exempt on the first B/.120,000 if it closes within two years of the occupation permit.

Can I get residency by buying property in Boquete?

Yes, if the price and the title fit the visa. The Qualified Investor visa needs $300,000 in a new, first-sale property or $500,000 in a resale. Friendly Nations needs a $200,000 property, and the Pensionado visa drops the pension requirement from $1,000 to $750 a month with a $100,000 property. Rights-of-possession land does not count.

Is healthcare in David good enough for retirees?

David covers most hospital needs, with a public referral hospital and private hospitals. The CSS Hospital Regional Dr. Rafael Hernández serves all of western Panama and has a 24-hour emergency service. The private options are Hospital Chiriquí and Hospital Centro Médico Mae Lewis. Boquete itself has pharmacies and clinics for routine care.

How many US expats live in Boquete?

There is no official count. Agencies describe Boquete as having one of the largest concentrations of pensionado residents in Panama, and gated communities such as Hacienda Los Molinos operate in English and Spanish. Volcán's expat community is much smaller, at a few hundred people.

Is Boquete, Panama safe for Americans?

For a homeowner, the risk that shows up most clearly in official records is landslides and flooding, not crime. In October 2025, SINAPROC recorded 40 landslides in the district, affecting homes in areas such as Jaramillo, Volcancito and Palmira. For crime and neighborhood safety, see our guide to whether Panama is safe.

Can I rent out a house in Boquete on Airbnb?

Many owners do, but whether a single home must register with the tourism authority is not clearly set out. Boquete had around 280 homes on Airbnb in older IPAT data. The authority's public lodging registration asks for a property certificate, floor plan and zoning, so get an attorney's view first. Rental income is taxable in Panama.
