For a first trip, the best place to stay in Bocas del Toro is Isla Colón, in or just outside Bocas Town (Saigon Bay or the Paunch road), or across the bay on Isla Carenero. Those bases keep the airport, ATMs and every water taxi route close. Travelers who want beaches, jungle or an overwater bungalow usually add two or three nights on Bastimentos or Solarte, where beds run from hostel dorms at about $10 to overwater bungalows from $328 a night as of 2026.[1]

Bocas del Toro is an archipelago, so picking a place to stay really means picking how many boat rides you take each day. Isla Colón is the only island with paved roads, which makes it the only one where you can walk, cycle or take a cab between your room, dinner and the dock.[2] Everywhere else, from Carenero to Bastimentos and Solarte, a boat is the only way in or out.

That is also why booking sites look lopsided. Booking.com lists 181 places to stay in Bocas Town alone, while the island lodges are a scattering of small properties with four to nine rooms each.[3] For the wider trip (beaches, tours and what each island is like by day), our Bocas del Toro travel guide covers the rest.

By traveler type, the choice usually comes down to this:

  • Party travelers and social backpackers: Bocas Town. Filthy Friday, the weekly bar crawl across several islands, ends back in town, and the hostels are walking distance from the bars.
  • Couples on a first trip: three nights in Saigon Bay or on Carenero, then two nights on Solarte or Bastimentos for the overwater or jungle part of the trip.
  • Families: Boca del Drago for calm, clear water, or a Saigon Bay B&B with a swim dock. Check age rules before booking overwater: Nayara is adults-only, and one listing says Sol Bungalows does not take children under 12.
  • Surfers: the Paunch and Bluff road on Isla Colón. Bluff is not a beginner wave.
  • Remote workers and long stays: Bocas Town or Saigon Bay. Several island lodges run on solar power, and some have no Wi-Fi in the rooms.
  • Tight budgets: Bocas Town or Old Bank hostels, where dorms start around $10.
Isla Colón

Staying on Isla Colón: Bocas Town, Saigon Bay, Paunch, Bluff and Boca del Drago

Isla Colón is the easiest island to stay on. It holds the airport, the provincial capital, Bocas Town, and the archipelago's widest spread of hotels, hostels, tour desks, pharmacies and ATMs.[2] It is also the most populated island, so "staying on Colón" can mean anything from a party street to an empty surf beach 30 minutes away.[4]

Bocas Town

See on Booking.com →

Bocas Town is the transport hub: boats to every island and the colectivos to the far ends of Colón all leave from here. It has more cheap beds than anywhere else in the archipelago. The trade-off is noise, especially around Filthy Friday, and travel writers in 2026 have described the town as chaotic during ongoing road and building works. You cannot swim in town either: boat traffic is heavy and the water near the docks is not clean.

Booking.com's list of the ten best Bocas Town hotels starts from about US$63 a night as of 2026.[3] Names that come up repeatedly include Swans Cay Hotel, Klika Bocas del Toro (which took over the former Selina hostel), Bambuda Bocas Town and Surfari Bocas for budget beds, and Tropical Suites, an overwater hotel with kitchens in the rooms and an overwater pool. Hotel Palma Royale sits on the main street with air-conditioned rooms, and Divers Paradise Boutique Hotel has a 24-hour front desk and sea views.[3]

The airport is about a 15-minute walk from the center of town, so if you are staying here you rarely need a taxi on arrival.

Saigon Bay

Saigon Bay is a 15 to 25 minute walk or about 10 minutes by bike from Bocas Town, and bikes rent from about $5 a day. The water is mangrove and seagrass, so it is not crystal clear, but most places have docks you can swim and snorkel from. This is the strongest all-round base on the island: dinner in town, sleep somewhere quiet.

Places to stay run from Barrbra BnB Over The Sea at the budget end, through Saigon Bay Bed & Breakfast and The Sunsetter Bed & Breakfast, to Santuarios del Mar at the top. Private boats from Saigon Bay cost more than the shared boats from town: one operator lists $16 for one or two people to Carenero and $20 to Bambuda Lodge on Solarte, plus $6 to $8 per extra person.[5]

Big Creek and Paunch

Big Creek and Paunch Beach are 10 to 20 minutes' drive from Bocas Town, with taxis charging up to about $10 each way. The beaches have heavy swell for most of the year, so they are for surfing and looking at, not swimming, and water taxis generally do not run here.

Budget options include Skully's House and RAB Hostal Bocas, which has a small pool. Nowhere rents jungle cottages about 3 kilometers from town with mesh-screened hatches that let in the sound of howler monkeys; there is no food for sale on site, so you bring your own. La Coralina Island House has an outdoor pool, fitness center and garden.[3] For groups, Casa Botanica sleeps 10 across three buildings inside the Finca Los Monos botanical garden, about 10 minutes from town by car.

Playa Bluff

See on Booking.com →

Playa Bluff sits at the end of the road on the east coast, about 30 minutes from town. Taxis charge up to about $15 each way, or at least $30 for a round trip, and they are hard to find out there. Accounts of public transport conflict: one guide lists a colectivo route to Bluff, another says no buses serve the coastal road and the walk to town takes about 1.5 hours. Plan on a taxi or a rented scooter.

Good to know · Swimming

The beaches on Colón's east coast, Paunch and Bluff included, have strong undercurrents and are usually unsafe for swimming. If swimming from your room matters, look at Saigon Bay, Boca del Drago or a lodge with a dock on Solarte, Bastimentos or San Cristóbal.

Bluff stays include Tesoro Escondido Lodge & Cabinas (budget), S Boutique Hotel, Sand Dollar Beach B&B, The Hummingbird and Oasis at Bluff Beach. Bluff is a powerful beach break that is not suitable for beginners.

Boca del Drago

Boca del Drago is the quiet northwest corner of Colón, close to Starfish Beach (Playa Estrella), with calm, clear water for swimming and snorkeling but no surf. Getting to town takes 30 minutes or more on an infrequent colectivo, or by taxi, which locals put at roughly $15 to $30. One Saigon Bay operator also runs a shared boat to Playa Estrella and Boca del Drago at $25 per person round trip, leaving 10 to 11 a.m. and returning around 4 p.m.[5]

Bird Island Bungalows has a pool, restaurant and bar, and Hospedaje Yarisnori is a 10-minute walk from Playa Estrella.[3] Drago Beach House is the upscale option. Staying here lets you reach the starfish before the day-trippers arrive; our Starfish Beach guide covers the best hours and why weekends get crowded.

Isla Carenero

Isla Carenero: two minutes from town, quieter at night

Carenero is the pick for travelers who want to be close to Bocas Town without sleeping in it. It is a small, forested island about two minutes by boat from town.[4] Shared water taxis cost about $1 to $2 per person as of 2026.[6]

The island has restaurants, beach bars and small hotels, one dive shop and a path that circles the whole island. The south side has surf breaks. Because everything outside your hotel's own restaurant involves a short boat ride, most guests cross to town for variety at dinner, and the cheapest crossing at night depends on boats still running.

Places to stay include Aqua Lounge (a social hostel) and Villa Elena at the budget end, and RIVA B&B, Bocas Beachfront Lodge, PirateArts Experience and Casa Acuario in the mid-range. Casa Acuario includes breakfast and faces both the sunrise and the sunset.

Bastimentos

Isla Bastimentos: Old Bank, Red Frog Beach and the jungle lodges

Bastimentos is the base for beaches and rainforest, at the cost of a boat ride every time you need town. It is the largest island in the archipelago.[4] It is home to the Afro-Caribbean community of Old Bank and to Bastimentos Island National Marine Park, Panama's first marine park.[2] There are no cars, and nightlife is close to nonexistent.

Old Bank

Old Bank is the island's main village, a place of footpaths, stilt houses and Afro-Caribbean food. It is the base for the western half of Bastimentos, including Wizard Beach. Shared boats from Bocas Town take about 10 minutes and are listed at $2 to $3 per person as of 2026, though a January 2025 fare list put the same trip at $5.[6][7]

Old Bank has some of the cheapest beds in the archipelago: hostels advertise dorms from about $10 and basic private rooms from about $20. El Jaguar has two large guest kitchens and a long nightly happy hour. El Pelicano rents apartments a few minutes' walk from the village and Wizard Beach. Bastimentos Hill Guest House, A&K Island Apartments and Live the Jungle Refuge cover budget, mid-range and upscale.

Red Frog Beach

Red Frog Beach is on the island's north side, home to the tiny strawberry poison dart frogs it is named for. As of July 2026, a water taxi from town costs $8 to $10 if you ride alone or $3 to $4 per person on a full boat, plus a $3 to $5 trail fee paid in cash at the Bastimentos landing.[8] From Old Bank it is a long jungle walk or a short paid boat ride, and Playa Larga, 6 kilometers long, is reached by trail from here.

Stays include Red Frog Beach Island Resort, Red Frog Bungalows and Palmar Beach Lodge, which sits on the sand and offers dorms, tents and higher-end bungalows with air conditioning and kitchenettes. Surf on this coast can be rough, so read our Red Frog Beach guide for when it is safe to swim.

Jungle lodges: La Loma and Casa Cayuco

The quieter far side of Bastimentos is lodge country, where meals are part of the price because there is nowhere else to eat. La Loma Jungle Lodge & Chocolate Farm is about a 20-minute skiff ride from Bocas Town, with open-air cabins, ceiling fans and mosquito nets but no Wi-Fi in the cabins.[9] Booking.com rates it 9.6 out of 10 from 103 reviews.[10]

La Loma's prices are hard to pin down. An older published rate sheet gave $120 to $130 per person per night for its four cabins, with three meals, certain tours and transfers to and from Bocas Town included, and a 50% single supplement.[9] A Booking.com listing showing October and November 2026 dates starts at $487.50 a night for two adults, roughly $244 each, so budget on the higher, current figure.[10]

Casa Cayuco Eco Adventure Lodge in Punta Vieja includes all meals plus kayaks, paddleboards and snorkel gear. For how these compare with lodges elsewhere in the country, see our comparison of Panama's eco and jungle lodges.

Good to know · Park fee

Foreign visitors arriving by sea pay B/.10 (US$10) to enter Bastimentos Island National Marine Park, which includes the Zapatilla Cays; nationals pay B/.4. The fee can also be paid online, and the park can close at short notice in bad weather, as it did briefly in November 2025.

The park fee rule comes from a December 2024 MiAmbiente notice, the most recent official statement.[11] Online payment has been available since August 2024.[12] The November 2025 closure lasted from November 2 until visits resumed on November 3.[13][14] If the park is on your list, our Cayos Zapatillas snorkeling guide covers the day trip from any base.

Outer islands

Isla Solarte and San Cristóbal: lodges with no town

Solarte and San Cristóbal suit travelers who want to unpack once and stay put. Solarte has no roads and no town, and few amenities beyond its handful of lodges and some dive operators. Anything you need from a shop, you buy in Bocas Town before you go.

Shared boats to Solarte run about $3 to $5 per person as of 2026.[6] Fares to specific docks can be higher: a January 2025 list showed $10 to the Blue Coconut bar.[7] Bambuda Lodge is the budget and social choice, with a pool, a 150-foot water slide, dorms, private rooms and thatch-covered domes. At the other end are Solarte EcoLodge and Sol Bungalows, the small overwater property covered in the next section.

San Cristóbal sits about 25 minutes by boat taxi across the lagoon. Its best-known stay, CocoVivo, has cabins from $55 to $100 a night, with breakfast and dinner an extra $30 per person per day.[9] Snorkel gear and paddleboards are free, and the dock has coral and a diving board.

Good to know · Power and Wi-Fi

CocoVivo runs on solar panels and usually runs out of electricity after dark, and Punta Caracol has limited power and no air conditioning. If you need to work, charge devices at night or sleep with AC, confirm the power setup before booking any island lodge.

CocoVivo's Wi-Fi, fans and solar setup are described by a travel editor who covered the lodge.[9] The Punta Caracol details come from its Trivago listing.[15] Humidity is the other enemy of electronics here, and keeping laptops in a sealed bag is the standard advice.

Sol Bungalows: rates and what they include

Sol Bungalows' own rate page lists $328 a night for Bungalows 1 and 2 and $398 for Bungalows 3 and 4 through May 2027. Over the December 2026 holidays to January 5, 2027, those rise to $358 and $428, and a 10% tourism tax is added to every rate.[1] Breakfast is included. Dinner is a set three-course menu at $35 to $38 per person, not served on Sunday nights, and the property states it is not all-inclusive.[1]

Week-long stays get 5% off, and bookings made within 30 days of arrival get 15% off.[1] Guests can also rent all four bungalows to have the place to themselves.[16] One third-party listing describes only two bungalows and says children under 12 are not permitted, while the operator's own pages describe four bungalows, so confirm the age policy if you are traveling with kids.[17][1]

Good to know · Booking sites

Sol Bungalows says its prices on Booking.com and Airbnb are raised to cover a 15% commission, so the same night can cost more on a booking platform than on the property's own site. Small Bocas lodges often work this way.

Punta Caracol: why the prices disagree

Published figures for Punta Caracol range widely. An undated luxury-travel listing gives $344 to $578 a night, including breakfast and dinner.[18] Booking sites show starting prices from about $150 to $173.[19][15] TripAdvisor shows €119 to €262 per room per night.[20] The gap usually comes down to dates, room type and whether meals are in the rate. The lodge has three room types: Standard, Luna and Master suites.[21]

Nayara: treat online prices with suspicion

Expedia describes Nayara as all-inclusive.[22] Some booking sites show snapshot prices under $100 a night, which do not match an all-inclusive overwater resort.[22][23] A long-running local guide puts the real figure at around $2,000 a night. TripAdvisor also notes that its displayed prices are partner averages that may exclude taxes and charges.[24]

Getting around

Getting to your hotel: water taxis, transfers and last boats

Every base except Isla Colón depends on water taxis, and their cost and timing should shape where you stay. Shared boats are cheap. Riding alone, after dark or to a lodge's private dock costs more. Many hotels and tours can pick you up and drop you off at the hotel's own pier.[2]

Shared water taxi
Boats from the Bocas Town docks that leave when full. Posted shared fares are about $1 to $2 to Carenero, $2 to $3 to Old Bank and $3 to $5 to Solarte per person as of 2026.[6] Expect to pay cash and confirm the price before you board.[7]
Solo or private boat
Riding alone to Red Frog costs $8 to $10, against $3 to $4 each on a full boat.[8] Private charters run about $40 to $120 depending on distance and time.[6]
Lodge transfer
La Loma's rate sheet has included transfers to and from Bocas Town.[9] Other lodges charge separately, so ask what the transfer costs and whether it covers luggage on arrival day.
Land taxi
Only on Isla Colón. There is no Uber in Bocas del Toro. Expect up to about $10 to Paunch and up to about $15 to Bluff; outside town, taxis are scarce.
Colectivo
The cheap, slow minibus from Bocas Town to Boca del Drago, which some guides say also serves Bluff. Departures are roughly every two hours.
Almirante boat
If you arrive overland, the boat from the mainland port at Almirante to Isla Colón takes about 35 minutes and costs around $6, cash.[2]

Last boats are the most common way to blow a budget in Bocas. Shared boats to the outer islands thin out after dark, and the run home then becomes a private trip. Ask your lodge the day before what time the last shared boat leaves from town and what a night run costs. Our Bocas del Toro island hopping guide has the full water taxi picture by route.

The flight from Albrook in Panama City to Bocas Town takes about 45 minutes.[2] By land, the bus or car goes to Almirante, about six hours to Chiriquí on the Pan-American Highway and then roughly three more via the Gualaca turnoff.[26] Every route, with costs, is in our guide on how to get to Bocas del Toro.

When to book

When to book, and which season you are paying for

Book the small island properties well ahead for Christmas through Easter, the peak of the December-to-April high season. Sol Bungalows has four bungalows, La Loma four cabins and Punta Caracol nine, so a single wedding party can fill one. Sol Bungalows charges its holiday rates from December 2026 to January 5, 2027.[1] For the rest of the year, Sol's 15% discount for bookings made within 30 days shows that late booking can pay off outside peak weeks.[1]

Monthly averages show January to March as the driest stretch, at 28, 10 and 24 millimeters. May to October each get more than 250 millimeters.[27] Some local guides call September and October a second calm window, good for diving and for non-surfers, but the long-term averages show them as the wettest months.[27] In 2026, IMHPA forecast August to October rain 15% to 25% above normal in Bocas del Toro.[28]

The practical upshot: May to October brings cheaper rooms and fewer people, but plan on afternoon downpours and pick a base where you can wait them out comfortably. For how Bocas fits into a wider Panama trip by month, see our guide to the best time to visit Panama.

Reddit advice

Where to stay in Bocas del Toro: what Reddit and travel forums keep repeating

The advice that comes up again and again in forum threads and travel blogs is to split your stay. Spend a few nights near Bocas Town as a base, then a few nights somewhere quieter, as long as you have four nights or more. Several writers suggest at least four nights in total because the archipelago's own weather can wipe out a day or two, and seven nights across two islands is a common sweet spot.

The mistakes people describe most often:

  • Booking Bluff or Paunch for the beach. These are surf beaches with strong currents, not swimming beaches.
  • Sleeping on Bocas Town's main strip without checking the night. Friday is the loudest night because Filthy Friday ends in town.
  • Arriving on an outer island with no supplies. Solarte has no town and no shop, so choose a meal plan or stock up in Bocas Town first.
  • Comparing overwater rates without the extras. A $328 room is about $431 for two once tax and dinner are added; an all-meals lodge at $488 can work out close to it.
  • Running out of cash. Water taxis, trail fees and many small places take cash, and Bocas Town is where the ATMs are.
  • Planning remote work from an off-grid lodge. Solar lodges can lose power after dark, and some have no Wi-Fi in the rooms.

Questions

Before you go

Where should I stay in Bocas del Toro for the first time?

Saigon Bay on Isla Colón or Isla Carenero suit most first-time visitors. Both are a short walk, bike ride or two-minute boat from Bocas Town's restaurants and tour boats, but quieter at night. If you have four nights or more, add two nights on Bastimentos or Solarte for beaches and jungle.

Is it better to stay in Bocas Town or on another island?

Stay in Bocas Town only if you want nightlife and the cheapest beds. Town has the most services and boats, but it is noisy, especially on Fridays, and you cannot swim there. Most travelers sleep better on Carenero, in Saigon Bay or on Solarte and Bastimentos, and go into town for dinner.

Are there overwater bungalows in Bocas del Toro, and how much do they cost?

Yes, from about $328 a night plus 10% tax at Sol Bungalows on Isla Solarte, with breakfast included, as of 2026. Punta Caracol Acqua Lodge on Isla Colón lists $344 to $578 with breakfast and dinner, and Nayara Bocas del Toro, the adults-only all-inclusive resort, is usually quoted around $2,000 a night.

How much does accommodation cost in Bocas del Toro?

From about $10 for a hostel dorm to around $2,000 for the top overwater resort, as of October 2026. Booking.com's top-rated Bocas Town hotels start from about $63 a night, and eco-lodges like CocoVivo on San Cristóbal run $55 to $100 with meals extra. December to January holiday rates are higher.

How many days do you need in Bocas del Toro?

Four to seven nights is the usual recommendation. Rain can take out a day or two in any month, so short stays are risky. With four nights or more, split your time between a base near Bocas Town and a quieter island.

Is it safe to swim in Bocas del Toro?

It depends on where you stay. Bocas Town has no safe swimming because of boat traffic, and Paunch and Bluff on Colón's east coast have strong currents most of the year. Boca del Drago, Saigon Bay and the lodge docks on Solarte, Bastimentos and San Cristóbal are the calm-water options.

Is Bocas del Toro safe to stay in?

The practical risks are mostly about the water and the boats. Avoid swimming on the surf beaches, and do not get stuck on an outer island after the last shared boat. For crime and neighborhood safety across the country, see our guide Is Panama safe?

Is there Uber in Bocas del Toro?

No, there is no Uber. On Isla Colón you use taxis, colectivos, rented bikes or scooters; between islands you use water taxis. Carry cash and agree on the fare before you set off.

What is the best month to visit Bocas del Toro?

February is the driest month, averaging about 10 mm of rain, and January to March is the driest stretch overall. May to October is cheaper but much wetter, with October the wettest month on long-term averages.

How do I get from Bocas del Toro airport to my hotel?

If you are staying in Bocas Town, walk: the center is about 15 minutes from the airport. For other islands, take a taxi to the docks and a water taxi from there, or arrange a pickup, since many lodges collect guests at their own pier.
