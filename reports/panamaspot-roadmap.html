Authority

Backlinks. Genuinely a constraint here — but worth roughly twice as much after stage 3 as before it, which is the whole reason it sits at four and not one.

2h + slow4 items
gated on #9

Stage 2, item 9 blocks all of this. No directory accepts a site with no contact page, and no tourism board links to one with no named humans. Two hours of writing gates the entire channel.

The diagnostic is unusually clean in your data: you rank position 5–15 on long-tail and position 47–76 on head terms — same site, same pages, same depth, same schema. Long-tail is decided by relevance, which you win. Head terms are decided by authority, which you don't have.

Easy tier — two hours, this week

  1. The operators you already write about.Highest conversion rate on the list. You send El Valley Bikes traffic for free — a "recommended local guide" link costs one email. Repeat for every coffee farm, hot spring and tour operator you cover favourably.
  2. Panamanian expat sites and forums.Chiriqui.life, Boquete community boards, r/panama. Your stage-3 practical pages are exactly what those audiences ask for weekly.
  3. Regional tourism and chamber listings.ATP business directory, Cámara de Turismo de Chiriquí, municipal pages for Antón and Boquete. Free, slow to approve, real local relevance.

Best tier — worth real effort

  1. A linkable data asset.Month-by-month rainfall and trail conditions by region, or Barú summit sunrise times. Original, maintained, and it earns links passively forever instead of one at a time. This is what separates sites that stall at 50 referring domains from ones that don't — and stage 3 item "best time to visit" is the seed of it.
  2. Panamanian press travel desks.La Prensa, TVN, Panamá América. Pitch a data story — "what tourists actually search about Panama" — not your site.
  3. Lodges as partners, not targets.Boquete and El Valle lodges keep "what to do nearby" pages and maintain them badly. Their domains are older and more trusted than any blog you could pitch.
  4. The STRI orbit.The Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute is headquartered in Panama. A properly cited piece on quetzal season can pick up conservation and academic citations — slow, but these are the links that move head terms.

On paying for links. Buying them violates Google's spam policies and the enforcement risk is real. Practically: paid guest posts on generic travel blogs are close to worthless — those sites sell to everyone. If you spend money, spend it where the placement would drive referral traffic even if the link were nofollow: an expat newsletter sponsorship, a genuinely-used tourism directory, a lodge partnership. Never buy a package priced per link.

Why this order

The stages aren't arbitrary. Each one lowers the cost of the next, and doing them out of order wastes work.

  • Hubs came first because everything links into them. The footer links, the internal-linking gain and the trapped-page retargets all needed a target to point at. That's done — which is why stage 2 item 7 is now possible at all.
  • Stage 1 before stage 2 because the mechanical fixes are free and independent, and shipping them first gives you a clean two-week window to measure the stage-2 rewrites against. Judge those on CTR at constant position, not raw clicks.
  • Stage 2 item 9 before stage 4 because About and Contact gate every directory and every outreach email. Two hours unlocks a whole channel.
  • Stage 3 before the serious link campaign because links multiply the surface they lift. The same effort applied to 84 pages instead of 48 is worth roughly double — and the "best time to visit" page from stage 3 is the seed of the linkable asset in stage 4.
  • Stage 5's tracking starts in stage 1, not stage 5. You need sixty days of data before the pitch, so the counter has to be running long before the conversation.
The one that decides everything

Stages 2 and 4, on top of the stage 1 work now shipped, get you to roughly 1,500 clicks a month and then flatten — you'll have exhausted what 48 pages can produce. Only stage 3 has no ceiling. The site is in good shape and the foundation is now genuinely solid; the variable is whether articles get published next week. You went five weeks dark in August and the curve is still coasting on the July batch.

Loose ends worth knowing

  • The Spanish nav is fixed — it went in with stage 1 rather than waiting. All 27 pages now read "Destinos / Ecoturismo / Guías / Planifica tu viaje", with the anchors pointing at /es rather than the English homepage.
  • Two language toggles still land on a homepage — Coiba and San Blas have no English article at all. Correct behaviour until that content exists, not a bug.
  • Two commits are now on main — 5de9c46 (hubs, footer) and 5d8a62a (stage 1). Nothing reaches readers until Netlify builds both; confirm the deploy went green before judging anything by GSC.
  • Raw .jpg and .HEIC files never deploy by design — .gitignore commits only optimised .webp. I checked: no article references an ignored image, so this is working as intended. Worth remembering when adding photography.
  • Your image filenames don't describe their contents — elvalle-panorama.webp is a toucan. Harmless until someone writes alt text from the filename instead of looking.
