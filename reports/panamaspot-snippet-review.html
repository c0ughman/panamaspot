Found while doing this — a real content bug, not a snippet one

Your two Caldera Hot Springs pages disagree on the entry price. The English page states the entrance "costs $7 per person in cash." The Spanish page's at-a-glance box says "Entrada $3–$5 por persona," and its body only calls it "una entrada simbólica." One of those is wrong, and between them they carry 3,186 impressions — the biggest page pair on the site.

I did not put a price in either title because I don't know which is correct, and a wrong number in a title is worse than no number at all. Once you know the real figure, both pages should say it and both titles can lead with it — price-led titles are exactly what converts best for you.

The Chorro El Macho pair has a milder version of the same thing: English quotes "$3 trail / $5 pool", Spanish quotes "$5 adultos / $2.50 niños". Those may both be true of different things, but they read as contradictory. Worth one pass to reconcile.

How I wrote these

Each title leads with the keyword, then promises one specific thing a searcher would have to click to find out — a price, a distance, a duration, a count. Every number was read out of the page it belongs to, and I've quoted the source line under each row so you can check any of them. Where the two language versions disagreed, I left the number out rather than pick a side. Nothing is carried across languages.

Item 8 came in much smaller than scoped — deliberately

The roadmap called for FAQ blocks answering ~12 zero-click questions. I audited the pages before writing anything and found eleven of the twelve are already answered — the FAQ wording just differs from the query wording. "¿Cuánto cuesta la entrada?" already answers chorro el macho precio; "¿A qué hora abre?" already answers chorro el macho horario; "¿Dónde comer por menos de $15?" already answers restaurantes casco viejo económicos. Adding duplicates would have made those pages worse, so I didn't.

The one genuine gap was el volcan baru esta activo — 62 impressions at position 9.8 with zero clicks. Both Bárú pages discuss volcanic status in the body but neither surfaced it as a question, so it never won the snippet. Added to the visible FAQ and the FAQPage schema together, in both languages, using each page's own wording. One question added, not twelve.

What to watch, and how

These are snippet changes, so they should move CTR without moving position. That is the whole measurement.

  • Judge on CTR at constant position, not on clicks. In GSC, filter to these 26 pages and compare the 28 days before against the 28 after. If CTR rises while average position holds, the rewrites worked. If position moves too, you can't attribute anything either way.
  • Give it three weeks before reading anything. Google has to recrawl and re-render each page, and it will sometimes keep showing an old title for a while, or rewrite your title itself. Both are normal.
  • Expect the retargeted five to move slowly. A title change signals new intent but doesn't rebuild topical authority. Going from position 30 to page one takes internal links and time, not a better snippet. The snippet just stops wasting the impressions you already get.
  • The two zero-CTR outliers are the ones to watch hardest — tours-in-boquete-panama at 0.11% and tours-en-el-valle-de-anton at 0.22%. Both now lead with prices. If price-led titles don't move those, the problem is the SERP, not the copy.
  • Fix the Caldera price discrepancy and both pages can lead with the number. That page pair is your largest single block of impressions.
