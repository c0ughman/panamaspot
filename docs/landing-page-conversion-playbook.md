# Landing Page Conversion Playbook

A synthesis of two conversion frameworks we use as the house reference when building or
optimizing landing pages. Feed this to the `cro` and `copywriting` skills alongside the
`.agents/product-marketing.md` context file.

- **Part 1 — Verlex:** 5 psychological variables that decide conversion, independent of design/offer.
- **Part 2 — Alex Hormozi:** the value-equation-driven landing page structure + CRO cadence.

The two agree more than they disagree. Where they overlap (headlines, above-the-fold, proof,
simplicity), treat it as settled: do it. Part 1 tells you *why* a page converts; Part 2 tells you
*how to lay it out and test it*.

---

## Part 1 — The 5 Psychological Variables (Verlex)

> Premise: the gap between a page converting at 1–2% and one converting at 15–20% is usually **not**
> the designer or the offer. Layout is only half the story. Underneath layout sit small psychological
> decisions most people never make on purpose. These are those decisions.

### 1. Sell the core desire, not the outcome
"Sell outcomes not features" is correct but stops one layer too early. The outcome (make money, lose
20 lbs, clear skin) describes *what* they get. It skips *why* they want it — and the "why" is where
buying happens.

- Money → security, safety, peace of mind.
- Weight loss → feeling attractive again, playing with your kids 20 years from now, a long healthy life.

**Life Force 8 (Drew Whitman)** — the eight desires every human is biologically wired for. Anchor
headlines and imagery to one (or more) of these:

1. **Care & protection of loved ones** — e.g. pool-fence client headline "Protect your kids."
2. **Freedom from fear, pain, danger** — e.g. "Don't wait until it's too late."
3. **Social approval** — accepted, liked, validated. e.g. lawn client "Get a lawn your neighbors will envy."
4. **To be superior / winning** — status, success. e.g. film-producer course "Travel the world on global sets and connect with top creators" + shots filming a celebrity.
5. **Sexual companionship** — feeling attractive/desired. e.g. med-spa client testimonial about the compliments they started getting.
6. **Comfortable living** — physical comfort, nice home, relaxation. e.g. bathroom-remodel headline on comfort.
7. **Survival / enjoyment of life / longevity** — e.g. personal-training headline.
8. **Enjoyment of food & drink** — rarer; e.g. nutrition client.

> **Apply:** for every headline, ask "which Life Force 8 desire is this hitting?" If the answer is
> "none — it just states the outcome," rewrite it.

### 2. Cognitive fluency
The easier something is to process, the more the brain trusts it — purely because it took less effort
to understand. The reverse kills technical products: jargon ("cyber security posture"), dense
paragraphs, and cluttered layouts all reduce trust *even when the claims are true*.

> **Apply:** short sentences, clean layout, lots of white space. Write so a 12-year-old (≈5th-grade
> reading level) understands it. Tool: run copy through Hemingway, or paste it into Claude and ask
> "rewrite this at a 5th-grade reading level."

### 3. Labor illusion
People assign more value and trust when they can *see* the work behind the result. (Harvard flight-
comparison study: two sites, identical prices; the site that showed a progress bar "scanning 200
airlines…" was trusted more and converted better *even though it was slower*.)

> **Apply:** don't just state the outcome — spell out everything that goes into it. Franchise client:
> "make up to $300k profit in your first two years" felt weak alone; listing every facet of what's
> included made the offer feel more valuable and justified the price.

### 4. Loss aversion
Losing feels roughly twice as bad as winning feels good. Most pages only talk about what you gain; a
stronger close also shows what the visitor *loses* by not acting.

> **Apply:** shipping-audit client headline: "You're losing $30k–$50k on every $1M shipment — you just
> don't know it yet," followed by everything they lose by not fixing it.

### 5. Halo effect (first impressions)
The brain forms a first impression in ~50ms and decides stay/leave within ~5 seconds. A premium,
high-end top section (typography, color, branding, copy) creates a positive halo — people perceive the
whole brand positively before reading anything. A cluttered/generic first view poisons everything below
it, with no second chance.

> **Apply:** clean modern layout, generous breathing room, clear top-to-bottom visual hierarchy,
> consistent fonts/colors, strong copy. Win the first 5 seconds.

## Part 2 — The Alex Hormozi Landing Page Framework

> Cornerstone mindset: **efficiency**. Instead of "spend more" (where CPA/CAC blow up and lead quality
> drops), win by converting more of the traffic you already have. CRO compounds — better conversion →
> better economics → you can outbid competitors for attention → "ethical monopoly." Biggest jumps in
> conversion happen **above the fold**. ~90% of tests should be **headline or image** tests.

### The Value Equation as page structure
Hormozi structures the page around the value equation:

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Success) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

#### A. Dream outcome — in the headline
Don't state what you do; state the end result the customer actually wants (money, weight loss, fixing a
relationship, looking cool to friends). Solar-panel-cleaning example:
- Before: "Exterior cleaning services" (just states what they do).
- After: "We clean your solar panels so they produce more energy, so you cut your energy bill, so you
  can feel confident your solar is actually working." → **+64% conversions.**

#### B. Hero image = visual proof of the dream outcome
The hero image should *show* the promised outcome, not decorate. Luxury closet → show the luxury
closet. Straighter/brighter smile → show the smile. Keep the dream outcome present throughout the whole
page (headlines + imagery), not just above the fold.

#### C. Increase perceived likelihood of success
Two levers:
1. **Social proof** — proof out-sells promises, because promises are only an *approximation* of the
   likelihood of the result. Put it **above the fold**. Be as **visual** as possible: a screenshot of a
   bank account > text review; a photo of 20 lbs lost > text; a weigh-in/weigh-out video > photo.
   - **Stack it (Wall of Love):** overwhelm with video + image + text reviews together. Show it all up
     front, especially on mobile. **Never** hide proof in a carousel (low engagement; hidden = unseen).
2. **Reduce risk** — guarantees, and time framing ("in just two weeks") stated prominently.

#### D. Reduce time delay & effort/sacrifice
- **Sub-headline:** explain *how* you achieve the headline's result while making it feel effortless.
  Often "without X, Y, Z." Example headline "Launch a high-paying career or business, fully debt-free"
  + sub-headline listing personal success coach, 24/7 tutoring, hands-on training, lifetime support.
- **"How it works" section:** 3–4 steps, never 5–6 (more steps = more perceived effort).

### Headlines everywhere
"When you've written the headline you've spent 80 cents of your ad dollar" (Ogilvy). Most people only
scan headlines. Write every section's headline so the value prop is clear *from the headlines alone*,
assuming no one reads the body:
- Not "What makes us unique" → say what actually makes you unique.
- Not "How it works" → say how it works and how simple it is.
- Not "What our customers say" → say the actual result customers got.

### Ongoing CRO & A/B testing cadence
- **One test per week.** Weekly marketing meeting: "What was last week's test? Did we beat the control?"
  Forces focus on the single highest-leverage change.
- **Traffic reality check:** traditional A/B testing needs volume for statistical significance
  (Verlex minimum ≈ 40k–50k visitors/month). Use an A/B test duration calculator.
- **Low traffic (<~20k/mo):** don't run underpowered tests. Instead make **large-scale revamps** based
  on best practices already validated on higher-traffic pages. Winners compound (snowball effect —
  e.g. $3M incremental revenue via CRO; an e-commerce client doubling conversion rate via CRO alone).

## Combined checklist (use on every page)

Above the fold:
- [ ] Headline names the **dream outcome**, tied to a **Life Force 8** core desire (not just the outcome).
- [ ] Sub-headline explains *how*, framed as effortless ("without X, Y, Z").
- [ ] Hero image visually *proves* the dream outcome.
- [ ] Social proof present above the fold, as visual as possible.
- [ ] Premium first impression — wins the 50ms / 5-second halo test.
- [ ] Reads at a 5th-grade level; no jargon; short sentences; white space (cognitive fluency).

Below the fold:
- [ ] "How it works" in 3–4 steps max.
- [ ] Labor illusion — the offer's components are spelled out, not just summarized.
- [ ] Loss aversion — page shows what they lose by not acting.
- [ ] Wall of Love — stacked video/image/text proof, no carousels, visible on mobile.
- [ ] Risk reversal — guarantee and/or time framing stated prominently.
- [ ] Every section headline carries the value prop on its own.

Process:
- [ ] One focused A/B test per week (if traffic supports significance); else large-scale revamp from validated best practices.
- [ ] ~90% of tests are headline or image tests, focused above the fold.
